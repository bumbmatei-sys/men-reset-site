**How to quit the whole habit** gets fuzzy when you treat the parts as separate projects. For many people the two things are one sequence. The image starts it, or the boredom starts it and the image finishes it. If you ban the site and keep the rest, you have only renamed the problem.

This is direct teaching. I am not saying every marriage or every body looks the same. I am talking about the private habit that runs on fantasy and a screen or a substance or a game. Whatever your loop is, this still applies.

## Close the loophole

Write the rule while you are not in the middle of it. "I do not act out to porn or to a replay of porn." If your conviction goes further, write that rule too, in your own words. Keep it specific. Vague rules get lawyered at midnight.

Fleeing, in the verse for this lesson, is physical. You leave. You do not stand in the doorway and negotiate a cleaner version of the same act. Fantasy that replays the video is the video with the screen off. Call it that. Then you stop congratulating yourself for a technicality.

Basically, if it feeds the same loop in your head, it is the same habit.

## Expect the urge and move

The urge is not an emergency and it is not a command. Your body can be loud and still not get a vote on the plan you wrote in daylight. The move is the same as in [How to Deal With an Urge in the First Ten Minutes](/tracks/break-free-from-porn/how-to-deal-with-porn-urges). Change rooms. Change posture. Do something that uses your body without feeding the image.

Cold water on your face. A walk. A set of pushups. A shower with the door unlocked if the locked bathroom is the danger for you. Pick one in advance. Deciding during the urge is how the loophole wins.

If you are married, do not use your spouse as a tool to get it out of your system while someone else is in your head. That is not intimacy. If something in your marriage is broken or missing, that is a real problem and the habit will offer a counterfeit. The counterfeit makes the real problem worse. Say the truth to your wife or your husband if it is safe, or to a pastor or counselor who will not wink at it.

## Watch the helpers that are not helpers

Alcohol lowers the argument you were going to make. So does exhaustion. So does a long game session that already trained you to chase the next hit. If those are part of your slide, name them in the rule. "No drink on nights I am alone." "Games off at 10." The [gaming track](/tracks/quit-gaming-and-the-phone) is not written yet. The boundary can still start today.

Do not replace the habit with a feed that is not quite the habit. You know the difference in your gut. If you would hide the screen when someone walks in, it counts.

## The day after you want it and do not

You may feel restless or irritable. You may be convinced that you need the release to sleep or to focus. That feeling is the habit talking, not proof that the rule is wrong. Go to bed anyway. Get up and do the first task of the morning. Eat. The feeling is allowed to be unpleasant. It will pass.

## The places the rule usually dies

The rule dies in a few places more than anywhere else. Know yours.

The bed. You are lying down, the day is over, and the phone is in your hand for the alarm. The alarm can live across the room. If you need sound, use a separate clock. Do not argue that you are the special case who can hold the phone and not use it. The data of your last month is the argument.

The bathroom. A lock, a few minutes, a phone. Leave the phone outside before you go in. It feels silly. Silly is cheaper than the loop.

The car or the office with the door shut. If you are early, do not spend the extra minutes in the parking lot with a browser. Walk in. If the closed door is the cue, prop it open when you are not on a call. Do the risky tasks in a shared space.

Write the place into the one-line rule. "I do not take the phone to the bathroom." A rule that names the room is harder to lawyer than a rule about purity.

## What you tell your body afterward

The urge to finish what you started is the habit trying to complete a script. You can stop mid-script. It will feel unfinished. Unfinished is allowed. Get dressed, leave the room, and do something with your hands that is not private. The unfinished feeling fades faster than the shame of a second round.

If you are shaking, angry, or wide awake, do not treat that as a sign that you need the release to sleep. Get up, drink water, and read two pages of something slow. Or pray in one sentence. Or fold laundry. Then go back to bed without the phone. Sleep may come late. Late sleep is still better than training the script to the end.

If you break the rule, do not add a second round to make the failure worth it. Stop. Write down what happened. Return to the plan the same night if you can, or the next morning if you cannot. [Why you keep going back](/tracks/break-free-from-porn/why-you-keep-going-back-to-porn) explains the second round. Read it before you need it.

Jesus is not standing there with folded arms. He already paid for this. The rule is not how you earn Him back. It is just you walking like the person He says you already are.
