When a thought is attacking you, it is hard to think clearly. You can know a hundred verses and not remember one of them at the moment you need it.

That is why this lesson is not about knowing more verses. It is about having the right one ready.

## Where we are in this course

You have learned that a thought is not yet a sin, what renewing your mind means, how to tell accusation from conviction, and how to catch a thought in the first seconds. Now we come to the answer. Once you have caught a thought, what do you say back?

## How Jesus answered in the wilderness

After forty days without food, Jesus was hungry, and the devil came with three temptations (Matthew 4:1-11). Look at how specific each answer was.

The devil said, "Command that these stones become bread." It was a temptation to meet a real need His own way. Jesus answered, "It is written, 'Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God'" (Matthew 4:4, quoting Deuteronomy 8:3).

The devil said, throw yourself down from the temple and let the angels catch you. It was a temptation to force God to prove Himself. Jesus answered, "It is written again, 'You shall not tempt the LORD your God'" (Matthew 4:7, quoting Deuteronomy 6:16).

The devil offered Him the kingdoms of the world if He would worship him. It was a temptation to take a shortcut. Jesus answered, "Away with you, Satan! For it is written, 'You shall worship the LORD your God, and Him only you shall serve'" (Matthew 4:10, quoting Deuteronomy 6:13).

Three temptations. Three different verses. Each one answered that exact lie. And He said them out loud.

## Why a matching verse matters

Paul calls the word of God "the sword of the Spirit" (Ephesians 6:17). A sword is used up close, against something in front of you. It is not a library you browse during the fight.

A general encouraging verse is good. But if the lie is "God is tired of forgiving you," a verse about God's creation will not answer it. You need a verse that speaks to that line. "His compassions fail not. They are new every morning" (Lamentations 3:22-23). Now the lie has met something that says the opposite.

## Read it in context

Here is a detail people miss. In the second temptation, the devil quoted Scripture too. He used Psalm 91, about angels guarding you (Matthew 4:6). He just pulled it out of its meaning.

So before you build your answer on a verse, read the chapter around it. Ask, "Who is this written to? What is it really promising?" A verse used the way God meant it is strong. A verse twisted to say what you want is not.

This is also one reason to keep reading Scripture daily, like we talked about in lesson 2. The more you know the whole story, the harder it is for anyone, including your own mind, to bend a verse.

## Why say it out loud?

Jesus could have answered silently. He spoke.

When a thought is loud in your head, saying the truth out loud breaks the loop. You hear it with your ears, not only in your mind. It is harder for the lie to talk over your own voice.

It does not have to be loud. A quiet sentence under your breath at your desk counts. In the car, you can say it with your full voice.

## How to pick and keep one verse for one lie

1. **Write the lie in its exact words.** Not "I struggle with shame." Write the sentence. "You will never be free of this."
2. **Find a verse that says the opposite.** Ask your heavenly Father to show you. Search a phrase from the lie in a Bible app.
3. **Read the chapter around it.** Make sure it means what you think.
4. **Shorten it to one line.** Keep the heart of the verse in words you can say from memory. "If the Son makes you free, you shall be free indeed" (John 8:36).
5. **Say it every time the lie shows up.** Not once. Every time.

David wrote, "Your word I have hidden in my heart, That I might not sin against You" (Psalm 119:11). Hidden means you carry it inside. You do not have to go looking for it.
