In the last lesson you saw that a son does good works from love, not to earn. That sounds right until the night you fall. Then the old voice comes back fast. "You knew better. You have used up your chances. Stay away until you have cleaned yourself up."

This lesson is about that moment. Not to make sin small, but to make sure you know the way back.

## Does God's grace run out?

No. Paul wrote, "Where sin abounded, grace abounded much more" (Romans 5:20). Whatever the size of your sin, grace is bigger.

Jeremiah wrote this after watching his city burn: "Through the LORD's mercies we are not consumed, Because His compassions fail not. They are new every morning; Great is Your faithfulness" (Lamentations 3:22-23). New every morning. Not rationed. Not counted down.

John said that from Jesus' fullness "we have all received, and grace for grace" (John 1:16). One wave of grace after another.

If the question under your question is, "Does God still love me after what I did?", read [Does God Still Love Me After Porn?](/teachings/does-god-still-love-me-after-porn) It answers that directly. Here we look at grace as the way you get back up.

## Doesn't that make sin easy?

In lesson 1 we saw Paul's answer: "Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? Certainly not!" (Romans 6:1-2). Grace is not permission.

But here is what surprises people. It is grace, not fear, that actually turns a man around. "The goodness of God leads you to repentance" (Romans 2:4). Shame tells you to hide. Kindness draws you home. The man who knows grace is waiting is the man who comes back quickly, and the man who comes back quickly falls less often.

## Grace is the way back, not the reward for coming back

Many men treat grace like a prize at the top of a hill. First they climb. A week of being good. Extra prayer. Feeling bad enough. Then maybe they will be allowed back.

That is earning again, dressed up as being sorry.

"If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). Confess, and He forgives. Not confess, wait, prove, and then He forgives. "And if anyone sins, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" (1 John 2:1). You have someone speaking for you before you even open your mouth.

"There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1). Conviction says, "Come back." Condemnation says, "Stay away." Learn to tell them apart. [Accusation or Conviction](/tracks/renew-your-mind/accusation-or-conviction) walks through how.

## Come boldly to the throne of grace

"For we do not have a High Priest who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses, but was in all points tempted as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need" (Hebrews 4:15-16).

Look at what you find there. Mercy for what you did. And grace to help in time of need. Grace is not only forgiveness for the past hour. It is strength for the next one.

And notice the word boldly. Not crawling. Not hoping He is in a good mood. You come because Jesus understands temptation from the inside, and the throne is called a throne of grace.

## Jesus prayed for Peter before he fell

The night before the cross, Jesus told Peter, "Satan has asked for you, that he may sift you as wheat. But I have prayed for you, that your faith should not fail; and when you have returned to Me, strengthen your brethren" (Luke 22:31-32).

Jesus knew Peter would deny Him. He prayed for him anyway, before it happened. And He already spoke about what Peter would do after he came back. After the resurrection, Jesus asked him three times, "Do you love Me?" and three times gave him a job: "Feed My sheep" (John 21:17).

A fall is serious. But it does not cancel your place in the Kingdom or the work the King has for you. "For a righteous man may fall seven times And rise again" (Proverbs 24:16). "When I fall, I will arise" (Micah 7:8).

## Does God's grace cover all sins?

For anyone who comes to Him, yes. There is no sin too big for the blood of Jesus. The only person grace does not reach is the one who refuses to come. If you are worried you have gone too far, the very fact that you care is a sign the Holy Spirit is still drawing you.

## Your next-hour plan after a fall

| In the first hour | The old way | The grace way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What I tell myself | "I have ruined everything." | "I fell. I am still His son. I am coming back now." |
| What I do with God | Hide and wait until I feel worthy | Confess plainly, and thank Him for 1 John 1:9 |
| What I do with people | Tell no one | Text one trusted man: "I fell. Can we talk?" |
| What I do with the door | Leave it open "for now" | Close the door that let it in, tonight |
| What I do tomorrow | Punish myself with a bad day | Start the day like any other day with Him |

Write your own version, in your own words.

## When it is more than a fall

If you keep falling in the same place and cannot get out, that is not a reason to give up. It is a reason to get more help. Talk to your pastor. Consider a Christian counselor. An accountability partner can make a real difference; [Christian Accountability Partner](/teachings/christian-accountability-partner) explains how to find one. Getting help is wisdom, not weak faith.

If after a fall you feel so low that you think about hurting yourself, call or text **988**, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, any time. If you are in danger right now, call **911**. Your life matters to your heavenly Father, and to the people around you.
