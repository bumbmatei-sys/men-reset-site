You sat down after dinner for one game. Twenty minutes, maybe thirty. Then you looked up and it was after midnight.

If that happens to you a lot, you are not lazy and you are not broken. You are a normal person using something that was made to keep you there. This course is about getting your evenings back. Not by hating games or throwing your phone in a lake, but by learning how the night slips away and putting a few simple things in its path.

We start by looking honestly at what happens.

## Why can't I stop playing video games once I start?

Because there is almost never a natural end.

A board game ends when someone wins. A movie ends when the credits roll. Most modern games and every social feed have no last page. There is one more match, one more level, one more daily reward, one more video. Each one is short, so each one feels harmless. The trap is not one long decision. It is fifty small ones, made when you are already tired.

And the later it gets, the worse you get at deciding. At 8 p.m. you can say "that is enough" fairly easily. At 11:30 p.m. your mind is slower, the game is louder, and "one more" sounds very reasonable.

So the first thing to see is this: if you do not decide the end before you start, the night will decide it for you. Usually, that means it ends when you are too tired to keep going.

## Is it the game or the phone?

For a lot of people it is both, and they feed each other.

You finish a session and pick up the phone to check something. Or you lie in bed with the phone and open a mobile game. Or you watch other people play for an hour. The screen changes. The evening disappears the same way.

That is why this course covers both. If you only lock the console, the phone takes the hours. If you only fix the phone, the game takes them. We are working on the evening itself.

If you want the bigger questions first, like whether gaming is a sin or when it becomes an idol, read [How to Quit Gaming as a Christian](/teachings/quit-gaming-christian-2026). Here we stay practical, one evening at a time.

## Where do the hours come from?

Time is not created when you game late. It is taken from somewhere.

It comes out of sleep, so the next morning starts tired and behind. It comes out of work, because the email you meant to answer or the task you meant to prepare is still waiting. And it comes out of the people in the house. A wife, a husband, kids, a roommate, a parent. They get whatever is left of you, and most nights that is not much.

Nobody plans this. You never decided, "Tonight my family gets ten minutes and the game gets four hours." It just happens, one more at a time.

Paul wrote, "Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside, redeeming the time" (Colossians 4:5). To redeem something is to buy it back. Your evenings can be bought back. Not all at once, and not by trying harder, but by seeing clearly and then making a few small changes.

## When is it a real problem?

You do not need a diagnosis to want your evenings back.

The World Health Organization describes gaming disorder as a pattern with three parts: trouble controlling your gaming, giving it more and more priority over other things in life, and carrying on even when it is clearly causing harm ([WHO, gaming disorder](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gaming-disorder)). For a diagnosis, it has to seriously affect your life, usually over about a year.

Most people taking this course will not fit that full picture. But if you read those three parts and saw yourself, take it seriously. Talk to someone you trust, and think about seeing a counselor or your doctor. That is wise, not weak.

Either way, the steps in this course help.

## Write it down before you fix it

Here is the hard part. Before you try to change anything, just watch.

For the next seven evenings, write down three things: when you started, when you planned to stop, and when you really stopped. Then add one line. What got less of you tonight?

Do not lie to the paper, and do not punish yourself with it. You are not collecting evidence against yourself. You are getting honest numbers so the next lessons fit your real life.

Most people are surprised. The session they thought was "about an hour" was closer to three. The "quick check" of the phone in bed was forty minutes. That is not a reason for shame. It is information.

## You do not have to earn your way back

One more thing before you start.

Your heavenly Father does not love you less because you lost last night to a game. He is not waiting at the end of this course with a report card. Jesus did not come for people who had their evenings in order. He came for people who needed help.

So when you write down a bad night, tell Him about it in plain words. "Father, I meant to stop at ten. I stopped at one. Help me." That is a real prayer. Self-control is part of the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23). It grows as you walk with Him, not as you try to force it on your own.

If shame is loud for you right now, read [Working From Rest](/tracks/know-who-you-are/working-from-rest). You change from a place of being loved, not to become loved.
