Now we give the night an end.

## Why "one more game" never works

"One more game" sounds like a limit. It is not. It is a promise made by the tired version of you, and he never keeps it.

The problem is not that you are weak. The problem is the moment you are deciding in. You are in the middle of something, you just lost or just won, and the next round is one button away. That is the worst possible time to make a choice.

So we move the choice. You make it before you start, when you are still clear.

## How do I limit my gaming time?

Pick a clock time. A real one.

Not "after this match." Not "when I feel tired." Not "about an hour." A time on the clock. 9:30 p.m. 10:00 p.m. Something you could write on a sticky note.

Here is a simple way to choose it. Start from the time you want to be asleep. Go back at least one hour for winding down, getting ready for bed, and time with the people in the house. That is your stop.

If you want to be asleep by 11:00, your stop is 10:00 at the latest. Maybe 9:45, so there is room to talk to someone before bed.

## When should you stop playing video games before bed?

A lot of people ask this, and there is no perfect number. Many sleep guides suggest putting screens away around an hour before bed. That is a good place to start.

But the deeper answer is this: stop early enough that the rest of your evening still gets something. Sleep is one part. Your wife or husband, your kids, a friend, a call to your mom, a quiet few minutes with your heavenly Father, those need room too. If the game stops at the exact minute you fall into bed, the people around you still got nothing.

## Decide it before you start

The stop is decided before the first game, every time.

When you sit down, say it out loud or write it down. "I am playing until 9:45." Then set two alarms, one for 9:35 and one for 9:45. The first is a ten minute warning. It lets you finish the match or find a save point without arguing with yourself.

Put the alarms across the room. Not on the controller. Not on the phone in your hand, where you can swipe it away without thinking. You should have to stand up to turn them off. Standing up breaks the spell more than you would expect.

When the second alarm rings at 9:45, you stop. Even in the middle of something. Even if your team needs you. That is what makes it a hard stop.

## A stop that means what it says

Jesus said, "But let your 'Yes' be 'Yes,' and your 'No,' 'No'" (Matthew 5:37).

He was talking about honesty, about people who swore big oaths because their normal word meant nothing. That is what "one more game" does to us. We say it so often that our own word stops meaning anything, even to ourselves.

A hard stop is a small way to make your word mean something again. "I will stop at 9:45." Then you do. It sounds tiny. But each kept stop rebuilds a bit of trust, with yourself, with the people who live with you, and in your walk with your heavenly Father.

Proverbs says, "Whoever has no rule over his own spirit Is like a city broken down, without walls" (Proverbs 25:28). A city without walls is not free. Anything can walk in and take what it wants. A hard stop is one stone in the wall. It does not make you rigid. It keeps what matters from being carried off.

## Tell someone your stop time

This is the step most people skip, and it is one of the strongest.

Tell one person your stop time. Your spouse. A roommate. A friend you play with online. "I am off at 9:45 tonight." If you play with a group, say it at the start: "I can play until 9:45, then I am out." Then they are not surprised when you leave, and you are not tempted to stay so you do not let them down.

If you want someone to check in with you more often, read [Accountability Apps Compared](/teachings/accountability-apps-compared-2026). An app can help. A real person who knows your stop time helps more.

## If you want to quit all at once

Some of you will read this and think, a time limit is not enough for me. I need to stop gaming completely for a while.

That can be the right call. If every session ends past your stop no matter what you try, a full break may be wiser than a better limit. You can quit all at once or slowly. Both are real paths. The live article [How to Quit Gaming as a Christian](/teachings/quit-gaming-christian-2026) walks through how to choose.

If you take a full break, your "stop" is simply the moment you would normally start. Use the rest of this course the same way. The next lessons still apply.
