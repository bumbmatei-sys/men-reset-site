Most morning routines you see online start at 5 a.m. A cold shower, a workout, an hour of reading, a journal, all before the sun is up.

Maybe that works for some people. For most of us with a job, kids or a long commute, it lasts about three days. Then we sleep through the alarm once, feel like a failure, and quit the whole thing.

So we are not starting there. We are starting with one question. What time can you actually get up?

## Why this course starts with the clock

Every other part of a morning routine sits on top of this one. If you do not get up at a steady time, there is no morning to build on. You are always rushing, always behind, and the phone fills the gap.

This course has six short lessons. Each one adds one small move. A wake time. A phone that sleeps in another room. One chore before you sit. A few minutes with Scripture. Leaving the house without a scroll. And what to do when the night went badly.

If you want to see the whole routine in one place first, read [A Christian Morning Routine for Men With a Job](/teachings/christian-morning-routine-men-2026). Here we build it one piece at a time.

## How do I wake up at the same time every morning?

Pick the time from your real life, not your ideal one.

Here is a simple way. Take the time you have to leave the house, or start work if you work from home. Count back how long it takes you to get ready. Then add twenty minutes. That twenty minutes is your morning. It is the space this course will slowly fill.

If you have to leave at 7:30 and it takes you thirty minutes to get ready, your wake time is 6:40. Not 5:00. Not 6:00 because it sounds better. 6:40.

Then set one alarm. Just one. Put it across the room, so you have to stand up to turn it off.

## Four days, not seven

Here is the goal for this week. Hit your wake time four days out of seven.

Not every day. Four.

That might sound too easy. It is meant to. A small target you hit builds trust in yourself. A big target you miss teaches you that you never keep your word. We have all had enough of that.

Paul said, "And let us not grow weary while doing good, for in due season we shall reap if we do not lose heart" (Galatians 6:9). Due season. Not the first week. Discipline grows slowly, like a tree. You water it, and one day you notice it is taller.

## Should you wake up at the same time every day, even on weekends?

As close as you can.

Sleep experts at the National Sleep Foundation say a steady sleep and wake schedule matters for health and for how you feel during the day ([their consensus statement](https://www.sleephealthjournal.org/article/S2352-7218(23)00166-3/fulltext)). Many sleep doctors say the wake time is the easier one to anchor. Bedtime moves around because life happens. A fixed wake time pulls the rest of the day into line.

So on your day off, try to get up within about an hour of your normal time. You can rest later in the day. But if you sleep until noon on Saturday, Monday morning will feel like jet lag.

If you work shifts, pick a wake time for each kind of shift and write them down. The point is not the same number on the clock. The point is that you decide it ahead of time, instead of letting the morning decide for you.

## The alarm is not the test

When the alarm rings, your mind starts negotiating. Five more minutes. You were up late. You deserve it.

Do not argue. Just put your feet on the floor.

That is the whole win. Feet on the floor. You do not have to feel awake, happy or holy yet. You just have to stand up. Feelings usually catch up a few minutes later.

If you can, open the curtains or step outside for a minute. Morning light helps your body know the day has started.

## Getting up to meet someone

This is the part I care about most.

David wrote, "My voice You shall hear in the morning, O LORD; In the morning I will direct it to You, And I will look up" (Psalm 5:3).

He was not getting up to prove something. He was getting up to talk to someone. That is the difference between a routine and a relationship.

Jesus did the same. "Now in the morning, having risen a long while before daylight, He went out and departed to a solitary place; and there He prayed" (Mark 1:35). He was not trying to earn His Father's love. He already had it. He got up because He wanted to be with Him.

So when your feet hit the floor, say something simple. "Good morning, Father. Here I am." That is enough for now. We will add more in lesson 4.

## Discipline is not how you earn love

I want to say this clearly. Getting up early does not make your heavenly Father love you more. Sleeping in does not make Him love you less.

"It is vain for you to rise up early, To sit up late, To eat the bread of sorrows; For so He gives His beloved sleep" (Psalm 127:2).

If you are getting up out of fear or shame, that will not last. A child who wants to be with his father uses discipline to get back to that delight on the days he does not feel it. That is all discipline is here. A way back to what you already want.

If that is new to you, read [Working From Rest](/tracks/know-who-you-are/working-from-rest). You work from His approval, not for it. Mornings too.
