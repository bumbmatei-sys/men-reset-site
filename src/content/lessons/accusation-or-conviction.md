Some voices in your head sound religious. They quote verses. They sound serious about sin. And they leave you feeling like you should stay away from God until you get your act together.

That is not the Holy Spirit. But it can be hard to tell in the moment. So this lesson is practice.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 1 we saw that a thought is not yet a sin. In lesson 2 we saw that renewing your mind means agreeing with what God says. Now we face a harder question. When you have actually done something wrong, which voice do you listen to?

The article [Take Every Thought Captive](/teachings/take-every-thought-captive-2026) lists the signs of each voice. Here we put them to work in real moments.

## What is conviction vs condemnation?

Conviction is the Holy Spirit showing you something wrong so you can be restored. Jesus said the Spirit would "convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment" (John 16:8). He shows you the problem and points you to the answer at the same time.

Condemnation is a verdict. It says you are guilty, you are the problem, and there is no way back. But for a believer, that verdict has already been dealt with. "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1).

So when a voice says "you are condemned," it is saying something God does not say about you. The Bible calls the enemy "the accuser of our brethren" (Revelation 12:10). That is his job description, not the Holy Spirit's.

## One question that sorts them

You do not need a long checklist in the moment. Ask one question.

**"Where is this voice sending me?"**

Conviction always sends you toward your heavenly Father. "The goodness of God leads you to repentance" (Romans 2:4). Even when it is uncomfortable, there is a pull toward Him underneath it.

Accusation sends you away. Hide. Stay quiet. Do not pray until you feel clean. Give up, you will only fail again.

## Peter and Judas

On the night Jesus was arrested, two of His disciples failed Him badly. Judas betrayed Him. Peter denied Him three times.

Both felt terrible. Judas "was remorseful" (Matthew 27:3), and he went away and took his own life. Peter "went out and wept bitterly" (Luke 22:62).

Days later, Peter was fishing when he saw Jesus on the shore. "When Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he put on his outer garment ... and plunged into the sea" (John 21:7). He did not wait to clean himself up. He swam toward Jesus. And Jesus fed him breakfast and gave him his calling back.

Paul names the difference. "Godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, not to be regretted; but the sorrow of the world produces death" (2 Corinthians 7:10). Both kinds of sorrow hurt. Only one leads home.

## Is guilt conviction or condemnation?

It can be either. Guilt is a signal. What matters is what it tells you to do next.

If it points to something specific, like "You lied to your boss this morning," and leads you to confess and make it right, that is conviction doing its work.

If it is a cloud with no clear edge, like "You are a terrible person and God is tired of you," that is accusation. Even if there is a real sin underneath, the accusation twists it into a verdict on your identity.

Here is another sign. Conviction ends when you confess. "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us" (1 John 1:9). Accusation keeps replaying the same sin after you have already brought it to God. Once you have confessed, that replay is not the Spirit. You can say, "That has been forgiven," and move on.

## What does conviction feel like vs condemnation?

Let us practice with three real moments.

**After a fall into lust.** Accusation says, "You did it again. You are disgusting. God must be done with you. Do not even bother praying tonight." Conviction says, "That was wrong, and it hurt you. Come here. Tell Me about it. Let us look at what happened before it, so tomorrow can be different."

**After snapping at your spouse or your kids.** Accusation says, "You are just like your father. You will ruin this family." Conviction says, "Your words hurt them. Go and say sorry, and mean it. I will help you be gentle."

**When fear about money or the future hits.** This one is sneaky, because it is not about sin at all. Accusation says, "This is your fault. God is not going to help someone like you." Conviction, if there is anything to convict, sounds like, "Trust Me with this. Do the one wise thing in front of you today."

Notice the pattern. Accusation attacks who you are and leaves you stuck. Conviction names what you did and gives you a next step.

## Do this today

Today, when you feel bad about something, stop and ask, "Where is this voice sending me?" If it is sending you away from your heavenly Father, say, "That is not His voice." If it is showing you something specific to make right, confess it to Him in one sentence and do the next right thing.

## Reflect

Think of the last time you felt terrible about yourself. Was that voice sending you toward your heavenly Father or away from Him?
