Somewhere in the middle of this, a lot of husbands hit a wall. You have been honest. You have been consistent. You have listened. And it still feels like nothing is moving.

That is the moment many men quietly leave. Not by walking out the door. By disappearing inside the house.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 3, [When She Is Still Hurting](/tracks/rebuilding-trust-after-porn/when-she-is-still-hurting), we learned to listen without defending when her pain comes back. Now we look at your own reaction to how slow this is, and how to stay present when every part of you wants to pull away.

## How long does it take to rebuild trust in a marriage?

The honest answer is that no one can give you a number. For many couples, it is many months before things feel steadier, and often a few years before trust feels natural again. Some couples find it faster. Some find it slower. A counselor who knows your story can help you both see where you are.

What matters more than the number is this. Her pace is not a verdict on your effort. She is not slow because you are failing. She is healing at the speed a deep wound heals. You cannot rush that by trying harder, and you will not help by asking how much longer.

Paul wrote, "Let us not grow weary while doing good, for in due season we shall reap if we do not lose heart" (Galatians 6:9). Due season. Not your season. His.

## Why men disappear

When it hurts and nothing seems to change, men tend to reach for one of a few exits.

**Going cold.** You stop trying to connect, because every attempt feels rejected. You tell yourself you are giving her space. But you are really protecting yourself.

**Hiding in work.** Long hours feel productive and keep you out of the hard conversations.

**Hiding in a screen.** Not porn, maybe, but games, sports, or endless scrolling. Anything that fills the evening so you do not have to feel it.

**Hiding in shame.** You decide you are so bad that you should just stay out of the way. That sounds humble. It is still leaving.

The first man did this too. After Adam sinned, God called to him, "Where are you?" And Adam said, "I heard Your voice in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked; and I hid myself" (Genesis 3:9-10). Shame tells men to hide. God keeps calling them out.

## Withdrawal feels like leaving to her

Here is the hard part. You may think your distance is a gift to her. To her it can feel like another betrayal. For years, part of you was somewhere else. Now, just as she is trying to see whether you are really here, you go somewhere else again.

Paul wrote, "Husbands, love your wives and do not be bitter toward them" (Colossians 3:19). Coldness is often bitterness that has gone quiet. It says, "I have done enough, and you are not giving me anything back." Watch for that in yourself. Name it to God before it hardens.

## Space is not the same as distance

Sometimes she will ask for space. Give it, gladly and without sulking. But there is a difference between giving space and going cold.

Space says, "Take the time you need. I am right here." Distance says, "Fine. I am done trying."

When she asks for space, stay reachable. Stay kind in small ways. Make the coffee. Pick up the kids. Send one short message: "Thinking of you. No need to answer." Then let it be. You are showing her that you can be close without demanding anything.

## Patience that rests on God

You cannot manufacture patience from willpower. It comes from trust in your heavenly Father.

Jesus never rushed. When His own brothers pushed Him to go and show Himself, He said, "My time has not yet come" (John 7:6). He waited on His Father's timing again and again. That was not passivity. It was trust. His patience came from knowing the Father was at work.

Love "suffers long and is kind" (1 Corinthians 13:4). It "bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things" (1 Corinthians 13:7). That is not a description of a man gritting his teeth. It is a description of a man who knows God is faithful, so he can afford to wait.

"In returning and rest you shall be saved; In quietness and confidence shall be your strength" (Isaiah 30:15). When you feel like giving up, go to Him first. Tell Him the truth: "This is slow, and I am tired, and I am scared it will not work." He can handle that. Then go back into the room.

## How to be emotionally present for your wife

Presence is not a big gesture. It is small and daily. Put the phone in another room for your evening time. Ask her one real question, like "How was today, really?" and listen to the whole answer. Notice when she is tired and carry something without being asked. Sit with her when she is sad, even if you do not know what to say.

You do not need to be impressive. You need to be there.
