Let's start with what God didn't do. He didn't send the addiction. He didn't plan the abuse, the divorce or the relapse to teach you a lesson. God answered to a broken world by sending Jesus.

But here is what He does. He takes what you hand Him and uses it. Causing something and using something are two very different things. People mix them up, and then they either blame God for their pain or think their pain is useless. Neither is true.

## Read the verse in its context

Romans 8:28 is famous, but it doesn't stand alone. Two verses earlier Paul says the Holy Spirit helps us in our weakness. When we don't even know what to pray, He prays for us with groans too deep for words. So before Paul promises anything about good, he tells you God is in the mess with you, groaning with you.

Then comes the promise. "Work together" comes from the Greek word synergeo. It's where we get the word synergy. It doesn't say every single thing is good. A lot of it isn't. It says that in His hands, things get combined. Think of flour, salt and yeast. You wouldn't eat any of them on their own. But if you put them together, it becomes bread.

And verse 29 tells you what the "good" is. It's being shaped into the image of His Son. Not an easier life. A life that looks more like Jesus.

## Who the promise is for

The verse has an address on it: "to those who love God, to those who are called according to His purpose."

The word for purpose there is prothesis. It's the same word the Bible uses for the bread of the Presence, the bread set out before God in the tabernacle. Which means your purpose isn't mainly a job title. It's a life lived before His face.

So what does loving God look like on a Tuesday? Jesus said, "If you love Me, you will keep My commandments" (John 14:15). In practice that's simple. You stay honest with Him about where you are. You hand Him the thing instead of hiding it. And you do the next small thing He's put in front of you. You don't have to feel strong to do that.

## Three ways He redeems a past

Joseph was betrayed by his brothers, sold, falsely accused and forgotten in prison. Years later he told those same brothers, "You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to save many lives" (Genesis 50:20). Notice what happened in between. Running Potiphar's house and the prison trained him to run a nation. God used a foreign, unfair system to pay for his training.

Peter denied Jesus three times, the night it mattered most. After the resurrection Jesus didn't lecture him. He cooked him breakfast and asked him three times, "Do you love Me?" (John 21). One question for each denial. Then He said, "Feed My sheep." The place of Peter's biggest failure became the place he was sent from.

Paul hunted Christians. Later he called himself the worst of sinners and said that's exactly why he received mercy, so Jesus could show His patience through him as an example for everyone who'd believe after (1 Timothy 1:15-16). His past became the proof that grace reaches anyone.

Training, restoration, and grace on display. Your story probably fits one of those, maybe all three.

## What to do with regret

You might be thinking, "That's fine for Joseph. He didn't do anything wrong. I did."

So did Peter and Paul. Regret and repentance both look back, but they look back for different reasons. Regret looks back to accuse you and keep you stuck. Repentance looks back to hand the thing over to God and move on. You don't need to be proud of a season to let Him use it. You just need to stop guarding it.

If shame still tells you that season is who you are, read [what your identity in Christ actually means](/teachings/identity-in-christ-2026). What you did isn't your name.

## Your story is where your authority comes from

Paul says God "comforts us in all our troubles, so that we can comfort those in any trouble with the comfort we ourselves receive from God" (2 Corinthians 1:3-4). The comfort doesn't stop with you. It's meant to pass through you.

When Jacob was old and dying, he worshipped leaning on the top of his staff (Hebrews 11:21). That staff had walked every road of his life with him. In a way he was leaning on his history with God. You can do the same. When you believe the story God has walked you through, that's where your confidence and your authority come from. Not from being impressive.

And you don't need a dramatic testimony for this to count. Every believer is radically saved, whether your past looks wild or ordinary. The person you help won't need you to be perfect. They'll need you to be honest and a step ahead.
