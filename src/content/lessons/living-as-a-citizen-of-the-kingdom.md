This is the last lesson in the course. You have seen that grace is a gift, faith is trust, the Kingdom is the King's reign, good works are what a son does from love, and grace is the way back when you fall. If you skipped ahead, start at [What Grace Actually Is](/tracks/faith-grace-and-the-kingdom/what-grace-actually-is).

Now the long work. Not a mountaintop week. A way of life.

## What does "seek first the kingdom of God" mean?

Jesus said, "Therefore do not worry, saying, 'What shall we eat?' or 'What shall we drink?' or 'What shall we wear?' For after all these things the Gentiles seek. For your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things. But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you" (Matthew 6:31-33).

Notice the setting. Jesus is talking about worry. Money, food, clothes, the future. Everyone chases those things. He tells His followers to chase something else first: the King's rule and His right way of living. Then He promises that the Father, who knows what you need, will take care of the rest.

Seeking first does not mean ignoring your job or your bills. It means they are not your king. When you make a decision, the first question is not, "What keeps me safe?" but "What does my King want here?"

## Your citizenship is in heaven

"For our citizenship is in heaven, from which we also eagerly wait for the Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ" (Philippians 3:20). You "are no longer strangers and foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God" (Ephesians 2:19).

You can love your country and still know that your first citizenship is somewhere else. Being a citizen of the Kingdom does not make you less useful where you live. It makes you more useful, because you bring something with you.

## An embassy in another country

Paul wrote, "Now then, we are ambassadors for Christ" (2 Corinthians 5:20).

Think about an embassy. It sits in a foreign city, surrounded by another country's culture, but inside it runs by the rules of its home nation. An ambassador lives in that foreign city every day. He shops there, works there, knows his neighbors. But he represents his home country, and he carries its values wherever he goes.

That is you. Wherever you step, at work, at home, at the gym, online, you carry the King's ways with you. Honesty when others cut corners. Patience when others snap. Kindness to the person nobody notices.

## How do you live in the kingdom of God day to day?

A Kingdom life is not built in one dramatic moment. It is built from small, repeated habits. Here is a simple shape. Pick from it. Do not try to do everything at once.

**Daily**

1. **Hand over the day.** Before your phone, pray, "Your kingdom come, Your will be done" over the day ahead. Name what is on your plate.
2. **Hear from the King.** Read a short passage of Scripture. A Gospel chapter is a good place to start.
3. **Work as to the Lord.** Do your job "heartily, as to the Lord and not to men" (Colossians 3:23).
4. **One act of the King's kindness.** Help one person, on purpose, without needing credit.
5. **Close the day.** Thank Him for one thing, confess what needs confessing, and rest.

**Weekly**

1. **Gather with His people.** The first believers "continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers" (Acts 2:42). Be part of a church. Not as a spectator.
2. **Share a table.** Eat with someone outside your own home once a week. Faith grows around tables.
3. **Rest and worship.** Take one day to stop, worship, and enjoy what God has given.
4. **Check the map.** Look at your Kingdom table from [lesson 3](/tracks/faith-grace-and-the-kingdom/the-kingdom-is-not-far-away). Is there one more area to hand over?

Micah sums up the whole thing in one line: "He has shown you, O man, what is good; And what does the LORD require of you But to do justly, To love mercy, And to walk humbly with your God?" (Micah 6:8).

## Roots before fruit

When a young tree is planted, gardeners sometimes stake it down. It looks like it is being held back. But the stake keeps it from shooting up too fast before its roots can hold it. The roots grow deep where no one sees.

Every man who has fallen badly in public first lost his private life with God. Guard the part no one sees. Your morning prayer, your honest confession, your time in the Word. That is what holds up everything else.

## When it feels slow

Some weeks the Kingdom life will feel like nothing is happening. "Let us not grow weary while doing good, for in due season we shall reap if we do not lose heart" (Galatians 6:9).

Jesus told a story about servants who were trusted with a little. To the faithful one the master said, "Well done, good and faithful servant; you were faithful over a few things, I will make you ruler over many things" (Matthew 25:21). Faithful in small things is how the King trains His people. And remember, "it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom" (Luke 12:32). He is not reluctant. He is glad.
