In the last lesson you named the loneliness and remembered you are a son, not an orphan. Now we take the first step toward people.

Not ten people. Not a whole new social life. One friend.

## Why start with one?

Because one is possible, and one changes a lot.

Solomon wrote, "A man who has friends must himself be friendly, But there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother" (Proverbs 18:24). The Bible does not measure friendship by numbers. It talks about the one who sticks.

Think of David and Jonathan. "The soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul" (1 Samuel 18:1). David had a whole army around him. What carried him through the hardest years was one friend who stood with him.

Many men have dozens of contacts and nobody who knows how their week really went. If you have one man like that, you are not a lonely man anymore. You are a man with a friend.

## Where can I find Christian friends?

Look for places where you will see the same men again and again. Friendship grows from repeated time, not from one great conversation.

A professor at the University of Kansas, Jeffrey Hall, studied adults who had just moved to a new city. [His study](https://news.ku.edu/news/article/2018/03/06/study-reveals-number-hours-it-takes-make-friend) estimated that it takes roughly 50 hours together to go from acquaintance to casual friend, and more than 200 hours to become close friends. That is not a rule to stress over. It just tells you why a weekly rhythm matters so much more than a single event.

Good places to look:

- **The same church, every week.** Same service, same seat area. Stay after. More on this in lesson 4.
- **A men's group or small group.** One. Go for at least a couple of months before you decide.
- **A serving team.** Setting up chairs, parking, kitchen, children's ministry. Working side by side is an easy way to start talking.
- **Something regular you already do.** A gym class, a running group, a pickup game, a hobby club. Men often find it easier to talk while doing something.
- **Your own street and your work.** A neighbor or a coworker you already like is a door that is already a little open.

Shared faith matters a lot. A friend who loves Jesus can pray with you and point you back to Him. But you can start a friendship with any man you get along with, and let it grow.

## Pray first

Before you go looking, ask. "Father, I am asking You for one friend. Show me who."

Jesus Himself chose His friends after prayer. He "went out to the mountain to pray, and continued all night in prayer to God. And when it was day, He called His disciples to Himself" (Luke 6:12-13). Ask your heavenly Father for the friend you have not met yet. Pray for the men you meet by name. Then pay attention to who He puts in front of you.

## How do I make the first move?

Go first. Most men are waiting for someone else to do it. You might be the answer to another man's prayer for a friend.

Here are some plain things to say:

- After church: "Hi, I'm (your name). I've seen you here a few weeks. How long have you been coming?"
- Following up: "You mentioned your job interview last week. How did it go?"
- An invitation: "I'm getting coffee after this. Want to come?"
- A specific plan: "Want to grab breakfast Saturday at nine? There's a diner near the church."

Notice the last one. It has a day, a time, and a place. "We should hang out sometime" almost never happens. "Breakfast Saturday at nine" gives him something he can say yes to.

If he says no, or he is busy, that is fine. It is not a verdict on you. Try again another week, or try someone else.

## What not to expect overnight

Do not expect a best friend in a month. Early conversations will feel a little awkward. Some men you try with will not click, and that is normal. Not every Christian man will become a close friend, and he does not have to.

Also, do not unload your whole story on the first coffee. Trust is built slowly. Share a little, see how he handles it, and let it grow. The [accountability partner guide](/teachings/christian-accountability-partner) covers the deeper kind of friendship where one man knows everything. Most friendships start much lighter than that.

What you are looking for over time is simple: a man who is honest, who shows up, and who points you to Jesus.

## Are friendship apps a good way to find Christian friends?

They can help you find a group near you or a church in a new city. But the friendship itself grows in person, over time, in the same place. Use an app to find the door. Then walk through it and keep showing up.
