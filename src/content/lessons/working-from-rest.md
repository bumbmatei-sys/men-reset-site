A lot of men's advice online says nothing is free. Sleep is a luxury you earn. Respect is earned. A day off is earned. Everything is earned.

That holds some truth in the gym and at work. Output matters there.

But many men carry that thinking into their faith. They feel they must earn God's love by having a clean week. After a fall they stay away, and only come back once they have made up for it. Two days clean, then prayer. A whole week, then the Bible again.

With your heavenly Father it runs the other way.

## Where we are in this course

[In the first lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/you-are-not-an-orphan) we saw the difference between living like an orphan and living like a child of God. An orphan has to earn his place. A child already has one.

[In the second lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/adopted-for-the-full-inheritance) we saw that you are a full heir, not a second-class member of the family.

[In the third lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/crying-abba) we learned to call your heavenly Father "Abba".

[In the fourth lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/free-to-be-small-again) we saw that maturity means leaning on Him more, not less. I ended that lesson by promising this one: you do not work for your heavenly Father's approval. You work from it.

## Approval came before the work

At His baptism, before He had preached one sermon or healed one person, Jesus heard these words from heaven:

"This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased" (Matthew 3:17).

Not "well done". Not "keep this up and we will see". Approval came first. Then the work.

Everything He did flowed from that word. You are not building a case for your acceptance. Paul says you have been "accepted in the Beloved" (Ephesians 1:6). The life you live comes out of that.

## The first attack was on identity

Right after the baptism, Jesus was led into the wilderness. After forty days of fasting, the devil came to Him:

"Now when the tempter came to Him, he said, 'If You are the Son of God, command that these stones become bread'" (Matthew 4:3).

Notice what was attacked. Not His method or His plan. His identity. Prove it. Do something.

Jesus did not perform to prove who He was. He answered with what is written: "It is written, 'Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God'" (Matthew 4:4).

The same pressure comes to you. "If you were really His, you would not have fallen again. Prove it. Show me a long streak, then you can pray again."

You do not have to prove what your heavenly Father already said.

## We started with rest

Here is a simple observation from the text. Adam was made on the sixth day. His first full day was the seventh day, the day God rested.

"And on the seventh day God ended His work which He had done, and He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had done" (Genesis 2:2).

Man did not start with work. He started with a day of rest beside his Creator, then went to work. That is the order in Genesis. Rest first, then labor.

## Can you earn God's love?

No. Not before you had it, and not after.

"For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast. For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them" (Ephesians 2:8-10).

Read the order. You are saved by grace, not by works. Then you were created for good works. The works come after. They are fruit, not payment. An apple tree does not grow apples to become an apple tree. The apples show what it already is.

Paul put it plainly: "But God demonstrates His own love toward us, in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8).

He loved you at your worst. Your best cannot buy more of what He already gave freely.

## The elder brother

Jesus told a story about a man who never left home and never disobeyed. The elder brother in Luke 15 worked for years, and he was angry when his younger brother came home.

"Lo, these many years I have been serving you; I never transgressed your commandment at any time" (Luke 15:29).

"These many years I have been serving you." That is the language of a hired worker. He was close to the house but far from his father's heart.

The father answered: "Son, you are always with me, and all that I have is yours" (Luke 15:31).

He forgot the real treasure, which was being with his father. A man can do all the right religious things and still be working for approval.

## How do I stop trying to earn God's love?

Two signs tell you that you are working for approval: striving and hiding.

Striving means proving your worth by output. A perfect streak. Extra Bible reading to make up for a fall. Serving until you are empty so you can feel accepted.

Hiding means covering up your struggles so you look fine. Nobody knows when you fell.

Both come from fear. A son who knows he is loved can be honest. He can say, "I am weak today," without losing his place.

Stop by believing what your heavenly Father has already said. Say it back to Him before you have a clean week to show Him.

## What is validation from God?

Validation means being confirmed as accepted. With people it usually follows performance. Do well, and they approve.

Your heavenly Father does it the other way. His word about who you are came before your performance and holds through your failure. Jesus heard "My beloved Son" before any ministry. You are told you are accepted in the Beloved (Ephesians 1:6).

When something is a fact, you do not chase it. You live from it.

## What does it mean to work from rest?

Jesus said:

"Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For My yoke is easy and My burden is light" (Matthew 11:28-30).

A yoke is a wooden frame fitted over two oxen so they pull a load together. It is still for work. So rest is not laziness. It is working beside your heavenly Father, with Him carrying the weight.

Hebrews adds: "There remains therefore a rest for the people of God. For he who has entered His rest has himself also ceased from his works as God did from His" (Hebrews 4:9-10).

Rest is entered by trusting. It is not achieved by finishing enough tasks.

In practice it looks like this:

- Receive before you do. Read one verse about who you are before you open your to-do list or your phone.
- After a fall, come back right away. Do not wait three days to feel clean enough.
- Keep your disciplines, like prayer, Bible reading, workouts and phone limits. Do them because you are loved, not to get loved.
- Ask one question of your motive: "Am I doing this to be loved, or because I am loved?"

## Why this matters for your habits

If your worth depends on your streak, one fall feels like losing everything. Shame then drives you back to the habit. You hide, you look for comfort in the very thing you were fighting, and the streak becomes the thing your worth rests on.

Working from rest does not mean you stop fighting. You fight from a place of being loved, not to get loved. You fight from victory, not for it. In plain words: Jesus already won on the cross. You are not fighting to get that win. You are fighting from it.

"There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1).

That is not permission to keep falling. It is the ground you stand on while you get up again.

## Working for approval or from approval

| Working for approval | Working from approval |
| --- | --- |
| After a fall, you stay away until you feel worthy again. | After a fall, you come back right away and tell the truth. |
| You read the Bible to pay down guilt. | You read the Bible to hear from your heavenly Father, who already loves you. |
| A good week makes you feel close to Him. | A good week is fruit, not payment. Your standing did not move. |
| A bad day sends you into hiding. | A bad day is brought to Him the same hour. |
| You serve others to earn points. | You serve others because you are already full. |
