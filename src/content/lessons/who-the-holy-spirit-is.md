Ask ten Christians who the heavenly Father is, and most can give you an answer. Ask about Jesus, and you will hear even more. Ask who the Holy Spirit is, and many people go quiet. Some say "a power." Some say "a feeling in church." Some say "I'm not really sure."

That is where this course starts.

## Where we are in this course

This is lesson 1 of 6. Before you can walk with someone, you need to know who they are. So this lesson is about the Holy Spirit Himself. The rest of the course builds on it: what He does, how to have fellowship with Him, how to hear Him, how He corrects, and how to keep being filled.

## Who is the Holy Spirit?

The Holy Spirit is God. He is the third Person of the Trinity, together with the Father and the Son. There is one God, who exists eternally as three Persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Jesus sent His followers to baptize "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19). One name, three Persons.

He is not a junior partner or a lesser part of God. He was there at creation, when "the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters" (Genesis 1:2). He is everywhere at once. David asked, "Where can I go from Your Spirit? Or where can I flee from Your presence?" (Psalm 139:7). He is called "the eternal Spirit" (Hebrews 9:14). He knows the deep things of God.

The phrase "Holy Spirit" is more a description than a personal name. That fits who He is. He does not push Himself forward. He points to Jesus.

## Is the Holy Spirit a person or a force?

He is a Person. Not a person with a body like yours, but a Person with a mind, a will, and feelings. A force cannot do the things the Bible says He does.

- **He knows.** "For the Spirit searches all things, yes, the deep things of God" (1 Corinthians 2:10).
- **He decides.** He gives spiritual gifts "to each one individually as He wills" (1 Corinthians 12:11).
- **He prays.** "The Spirit Himself makes intercession for us" (Romans 8:26).
- **He teaches and speaks.** Jesus said He "will teach you all things" (John 14:26).
- **He can be hurt.** "And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God" (Ephesians 4:30). You cannot grieve electricity. You can only grieve someone who loves you.

Jesus never called the Spirit "it." He said "He." That matters. You can use a force. You can only know a Person.

## Is the Holy Spirit God?

Yes. One clear example is in Acts 5. A man named Ananias lied about money he gave to the church. Peter asked him, "Why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit," and then said, "You have not lied to men but to God" (Acts 5:3-4). Lying to the Holy Spirit is lying to God.

The early church said the same thing in the Nicene Creed: the Holy Spirit is "the Lord, the giver of life," who "with the Father and the Son is worshiped and glorified." So it is right to honor Him, thank Him, and talk to Him. Honoring the Spirit does not take anything away from Jesus. The Father, the Son, and the Spirit are never in competition.

## Why did Jesus send the Holy Spirit?

On His last night with the disciples, Jesus told them He was leaving. They were afraid. Then He made a promise: "I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you" (John 14:18).

He kept that promise by sending the Holy Spirit. Jesus even said it was better for them: "It is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you" (John 16:7). While Jesus walked the earth, He could be in one place at a time. The Spirit lives in every believer, in every place, all at once.

Under the old covenant, the Spirit came upon prophets, priests, and kings for a task, and then lifted. At the Jordan, John saw the Spirit come down like a dove on Jesus, and he said, "He remained upon Him" (John 1:32). Jesus was the first Man the Spirit could rest on and stay. Now, because you are in Christ, He stays with you too. Not because you are perfect, but because Jesus is.

## What is the Holy Spirit's main job?

Jesus put it simply: "He will glorify Me, for He will take of what is Mine and declare it to you" (John 16:14).

There is an old picture of this in Genesis 24. Abraham sent his trusted servant to find a bride for his son Isaac. The servant traveled far, found Rebekah, and gave her gifts from his master's house. He told her family about his master and about Isaac, the son who would receive everything. Rebekah agreed to go and marry a man she had never seen, because of what the servant showed and told her about him.

That is what the Holy Spirit does. He makes Jesus real and beautiful to you, though you have not seen Him. Peter wrote of Jesus, "whom having not seen you love" (1 Peter 1:8). That love is the Spirit's work. If someone claims to be led by the Spirit, but everything ends up pointing to himself, something is wrong. The real Spirit always points to Jesus.

## Can the Holy Spirit leave you?

If you belong to Jesus, the Spirit lives in you. Paul wrote, "if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he is not His" (Romans 8:9). And Jesus promised He would "abide with you forever" (John 14:16).

You can grieve Him. You can ignore Him. Your awareness of His presence can go up and down, often because of what you feed your heart. But He does not pack up and leave every time you fail. If there is distance, He has not moved. Turn back to Him, confess what needs confessing, and keep walking. Lesson 5 goes deeper into this.
