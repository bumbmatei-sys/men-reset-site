There is a moment in most mornings that decides more than we think. It is the first time you sit down.

You wake up, you walk to the kitchen, and you sit. On the couch, at the table, on the edge of the bed. And when you sit, your hand looks for something to hold. Usually the phone, or the remote.

Ten minutes later you are still sitting. The day has started without you.

## Where we are in this course

[In the first lesson](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/a-wake-time-you-can-keep) you picked a wake time you can hit four days this week.

[In the second lesson](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/the-phone-sleeps-in-another-room) the phone moved out of the bedroom, so it cannot grab you before you are awake.

Now your feet are on the floor and the phone is in the kitchen. This lesson is about what you do next.

## Stand up, then do one thing

Here is the rule. Before you sit down anywhere, do one real chore.

Make the bed. Empty the dishwasher. Take out the trash. Feed the dog. Wipe the kitchen counter. Pick one, and only one.

It should take about two minutes. Not a cleaning project. Not a list. One thing that is real and finished.

Then you can sit. And when you do, you sit as someone who has already done something good today.

## Why make your bed every morning?

You have probably heard people say that making your bed changes your life. I would not go that far. A made bed will not fix your marriage or your habits.

But here is what it does. It turns the first act of your day into something done, instead of something avoided. It tells your body and your mind that the day has started. And it is a small promise to yourself that you kept before anyone was watching.

If you share a bed and your spouse is still in it, pick a different chore. The dishwasher works just as well. The point is not the bed. The point is starting with one thing done.

## Faithful in little

Jesus said, "He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much; and he who is unjust in what is least is unjust also in much" (Luke 16:10).

We often want to be faithful in big things. A big calling, a big change, a big breakthrough. But your heavenly Father usually grows us in small things first. Small things done every day, when nobody sees, shape who we become.

"For who has despised the day of small things?" (Zechariah 4:10). A two-minute chore is a small thing. Do not despise it.

## Is making your bed every morning good for you?

For most people, yes, as long as it stays small.

The risk is turning it into another thing to feel guilty about. If you miss a day, the bed is not a test of your worth. Your heavenly Father is not keeping a chart.

What helps is that a chore is something you can do while you are still groggy. You do not need focus or a good mood. Your hands can do it while your mind is waking up. That is why it works so well right after your feet hit the floor.

## Do it as unto the Lord

Paul wrote, "And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men" (Colossians 3:23).

That includes the dishes. You are not just tidying a kitchen. You are serving your family, your roommates or your future self. And you are doing it with your heavenly Father, not to impress Him.

So when you finish, say one simple line. "Thank You for this day. I am starting it with You." That is the bridge to the next lesson, where we sit down with His word.

## Pick your chore

| Chore | About how long | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Make the bed | 2 minutes | Anyone with their own bed or a partner already up |
| Empty the dishwasher | 3 minutes | Busy kitchens and families |
| Take out the trash or recycling | 2 minutes | Getting outside for a moment of light |
| Wipe the kitchen counter | 1 minute | Small homes and early starts |
| Feed or walk the pet | 5 minutes | Anyone with an animal that needs you anyway |

Pick one. Use the same one every day this week, so you do not have to decide in the morning.

## What if I am already running late?

Then do the shortest version. Pull the blanket up. Put three dishes away. It still counts.

The habit you are building is not "a clean house". It is "I do one thing before I sit". Even thirty seconds keeps that habit alive.
