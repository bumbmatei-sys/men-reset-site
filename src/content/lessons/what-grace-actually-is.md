Most men who grew up around church have heard the word grace a thousand times. It is in songs, in sermons, in the prayer before dinner. But if someone asked you to explain it in one plain sentence, could you?

This course starts here because everything else rests on it. Faith, the Kingdom, how you live, what you do after a fall. If you get grace wrong, you will get the rest wrong too.

## What does "saved by grace through faith" mean?

Paul wrote, "For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast" (Ephesians 2:8-9).

There are two words to hold on to. **Grace** is what God does. **Faith** is how you receive it.

The word Paul uses for grace, charis, carries the idea of a gift freely given. It is favor you did not deserve and cannot pay back. Faith is not a second payment on top. It is the open hand. A gift is not yours until you take it, but taking it does not make you the giver.

Picture a man in court. He is guilty, and the fine is far more than he could ever pay. The judge cannot pretend the crime did not happen. That would not be justice. Then someone steps forward and pays the whole fine. The law is satisfied, and the man walks out free. He did not pay. He did not even help pay. He received.

That is what happened at the cross. "All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" (Romans 3:23-24). Freely for you. Not cheaply for Him.

## Can you earn grace?

No. And this is where many men quietly go wrong. They say grace with their mouth and live by wages with their hands.

Paul is blunt about it: "Now to him who works, the wages are not counted as grace but as debt" (Romans 4:4). A wage is owed. A gift is not. If God's favor were something you could earn by praying enough, serving enough, or staying clean long enough, it would be payment, not grace. "And if by grace, then it is no longer of works; otherwise grace is no longer grace" (Romans 11:6).

Think of a man who cannot swim, going under in deep water. Grace is not someone shouting instructions from the shore. It is someone diving in and carrying him out. The man does not get credit for the rescue. All he does is stop fighting the hands that hold him. Afterward he can only say thank you.

If you have been trying to earn your way back into the Father's favor, [Know His Love](/tracks/know-gods-love) goes slowly through how He loved you before you did anything. This lesson is about the word itself, so you can recognize the real thing.

## What is cheap grace?

The German pastor Dietrich Bonhoeffer used the phrase "cheap grace" in his book The Cost of Discipleship (1937). He meant forgiveness preached with no call to follow Jesus. Grace without discipleship. Grace that says, "You are covered, so nothing needs to change."

Paul saw this coming centuries earlier. "What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it?" (Romans 6:1-2).

Cheap grace is not too much grace. It is a fake version. Real grace is free, but it is never cheap. It cost the Son of God His life, and it brings you into a new life with Him.

## Is grace a license to sin?

No. Grace is a teacher.

"For the grace of God that brings salvation has appeared to all men, teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly in the present age" (Titus 2:11-12).

Notice the order. Grace comes first and saves. Then the same grace starts teaching. It does not say, "Stop sinning and then I will love you." It says, "You are loved, so let me teach you how to live." Paul even says, "Sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace" (Romans 6:14). Grace is the reason sin loses its grip, not the reason you can keep it.

If you want the longer study of grace as power to change, read [What Is Grace in the Bible?](/teachings/what-is-grace-2026) This course builds on it.

## Is grace just God overlooking sin?

No. God does not shrug at sin. He dealt with it at the cross. That is why a forgiven man can stand before Him without fear. Grace does not mean your sin was small. It means the payment was big enough.

## Grace or earning: a simple table

Most men do not drift into earning on purpose. It sneaks in through ordinary habits. Use this table to see where.

| What I do | Earning says | Grace says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pray in the morning | "Now God will be pleased with me today." | "I get to talk with my Father, who already loves me." |
| Stay clean for a week | "I have earned my way back." | "He kept me. I am grateful, and I keep walking." |
| Fall again | "I have to wait until I have proven I am sorry." | "I come back now. The price is already paid." |
| Serve at church | "This makes up for last month." | "I serve because I have been given so much." |

## The one question that sorts it

When you are not sure whether you are living by grace or by earning, ask yourself one thing: **Am I doing this to be loved, or because I am loved?**

The action might look the same either way. The heart behind it is completely different. One man is a hired worker hoping to be paid. The other is a son at home.
