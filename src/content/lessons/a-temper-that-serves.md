This is the last lesson in the course. So far you have named your anger, learned your early signs, built a moment plan, learned to repair the same day, and looked at what is underneath. If you skipped ahead, start at [Anger Is Not the Same as Sin](/tracks/anger-that-doesnt-destroy/is-it-a-sin-to-be-angry).

Now the long work. Not a breakthrough weekend. A steady practice that turns a temper that destroys into one that serves.

## How do you control anger permanently?

Not with a switch. Many men want one moment where anger is gone for good. Sometimes God does a deep work quickly, and you should ask Him for it. But for most men, change looks like a slow, real shift. You notice earlier. You blow up less often. The blowups are smaller. You repair faster. Your kids stop flinching.

That is not failure. That is how fruit grows.

"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control" (Galatians 5:22-23). Look at that list. Longsuffering, gentleness, and self-control are the opposite of a hot temper. And they are fruit. Fruit grows from a living connection, not from straining.

## Self-control is not gritting your teeth

Self-control in the Bible is not self controlling itself by willpower. It is self under the Spirit's control. That changes how you fight.

When you feel anger rising, you are not alone with it. You can turn to your heavenly Father in one sentence and lean on His strength instead of your own. Your part is to stay close to Him, keep the practices, and keep telling the truth. His part is to change you from the inside.

"Whoever has no rule over his own spirit Is like a city broken down, without walls" (Proverbs 25:28). A city without walls is open to every attack. The practices in this course are your walls, and the Spirit is the One who builds them with you.

## A weekly check-in

Pick one fixed time each week. Sunday evening, or a quiet morning. Fifteen minutes. Ask four questions.

1. **When did I get angry this week, and how did I handle it?** Look honestly, not harshly.
2. **Do I owe anyone a repair?** If yes, do it in the next day.
3. **What was under my anger most this week?** Fear, shame, pride, exhaustion, feeling unheard.
4. **What is one thing I will do differently this week?** Just one. Go to bed earlier. Step away sooner. Tell a brother.

Then pray. Thank your heavenly Father for every moment you stayed slow, even small ones. Ask Him for help with the one thing.

Tiredness feeds anger more than most men admit. If your evenings and mornings are a mess, [Discipline and a Morning Routine](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine) can help you build rest into the week.

## Get help if you keep hurting people

If you have worked through this course and you are still shouting, threatening, breaking things, or making your family afraid, you need more than a course. That is not a judgment. It is honesty.

Talk to your pastor. Find a licensed Christian counselor who works with anger. Ask about a men's group or a structured anger program. Tell one man everything, not just the cleaned-up version. Seeking help is wisdom, not weak faith. "Without counsel, plans go awry, But in the multitude of counselors they are established" (Proverbs 15:22).

If you have hurt, threatened, or frightened anyone, do not wait. Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at **1-800-799-7233** today. Their advocates talk with people who think they may be hurting their partner, and can point you to programs near you. If anyone is in danger right now, call **911**. Never ask your wife or children to keep it quiet. If you feel very low, out of control, or unsafe, call or text **988**, any time.

## What is righteous anger?

Righteous anger is real. Jesus was angry when His Father's house was turned into a marketplace. "He drove them all out of the temple" and said, "Do not make My Father's house a house of merchandise!" (John 2:15-16). He was angry at hard hearts that did not care about a hurting man (Mark 3:5).

Righteous anger has a few marks:

- It is aimed at real wrong, not at being inconvenienced.
- It protects the weak instead of frightening them.
- It stays in control and does not sin.
- It moves toward healing and justice, not revenge.

A father who stands up for his child who is being bullied is showing righteous anger. A father who shouts at his child for spilling milk is not. Be careful here. Most of the anger we feel at home is not righteous, even when we have a point. Only call it righteous when it protects someone else, not when it protects your pride.

## God is slow to anger with you

"The LORD is merciful and gracious, Slow to anger, and abounding in mercy" (Psalm 103:8). Your heavenly Father is patient with your temper. He is not waiting to explode at you. He is the model of what you are becoming. The more you know how patient He is with you, the more patience you have for the people in your home.
