When men meet, the first question is usually, "What do you do?"

We answer with our job. Then maybe what we own, or what we have built. Without saying it, we are telling the other man who we are.

Most of life teaches that order. Do things, so you can have things, so you can become someone. Do, have, be.

In Christ it runs the other way. I am, I have, I do.

## Living backwards

Do, have, be has a problem. It never ends.

There is always more to do before you have enough. There is always more to have before you feel like someone. And one bad night wipes out the "be". You fall, and the whole thing comes down. So you start again at "do", trying to earn your way back to being a man you can respect.

That is how many men live with porn, gaming and the phone. Every fall resets who they think they are.

Your heavenly Father never built you to live that way.

## I am: a child of God

The first word over your life is not about your work. It is about your name.

"But as many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God, to those who believe in His name" (John 1:12).

"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new" (2 Corinthians 5:17).

You did not earn this. You received it. And it came first.

Here is a simple line to hold on to. Who you are is your identity. What you do is your ministry. They are not the same thing. Your job, your streak and your results can change. Your name as His child does not.

## What do I have in Christ?

A son is not just loved. A son has an inheritance.

"Therefore you are no longer a slave but a son, and if a son, then an heir of God through Christ" (Galatians 4:7).

Paul says your heavenly Father "has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ" (Ephesians 1:3). Notice the tense. Has blessed. It is already given.

So what do you have?

- **The Spirit.** "The Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God" (Romans 8:16).
- **Access.** "For through Him we both have access by one Spirit to the Father" (Ephesians 2:18). You do not need an appointment.
- **Forgiveness.** "In Him we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of His grace" (Ephesians 1:7).
- **What you need to live right.** "His divine power has given to us all things that pertain to life and godliness" (2 Peter 1:3).

You are not a beggar asking for supplies. You are a son drawing on what is already yours.

## What does it mean to be complete in Christ?

"And you are complete in Him, who is the head of all principality and power" (Colossians 2:10).

Complete means nothing is missing from what He has given you. You do not need a second Jesus, a secret method or a better version of yourself before you are fully accepted.

But be honest. You still struggle. Both things are true. You are complete in Christ, and you are still a work in progress in your daily life.

"Being confident of this very thing, that He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ" (Philippians 1:6).

Your standing is finished. Your growth is not. He is not disappointed that you are still growing. He started it, and He will finish it.

## I do: fruit, not payment

Only now do we get to "do".

"I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit; for without Me you can do nothing" (John 15:5).

A branch does not strain to grow grapes. Its job is to stay on the vine. The life of the vine flows into it, and fruit shows up in time.

That is how your good works grow. Serving your family. Being honest at work. Turning off the screen at night. Saying no to the thing that keeps pulling you back. These are not the price of being His. They are what grows when you stay close to Him.

"I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me" (Philippians 4:13).

Through Christ. Not to get to Christ.

## How do I know who I am in Christ?

Not from your feelings, and not from your last week.

You know it from what your heavenly Father says in His word, and from the Spirit's witness inside you. Feelings move. His word does not.

That is why Paul writes, "And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind" (Romans 12:2). Renewing your mind means replacing old thoughts with what is true, again and again, until the truth feels normal.

He changes you through His love, not through a list of rules. And the more you walk with Him, the more grateful you become, and the less you want the things that used to hold you.

## Why is it important to know who we are in Christ?

Because temptation usually attacks the "I am" first.

In the wilderness the devil said to Jesus, "If You are the Son of God" (Matthew 4:3). We looked at this in lesson 5. The same voice speaks to you after a fall. "If you were really His, you would not have done that."

If your identity rests on what you do, that voice wins. If it rests on what your heavenly Father said, you can answer it.

So when you fall, do not start again at "do". Do not try to earn your way back. Go back to "I am". Then remember "I have". Then do the next right thing.

| I am | I have | I do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A child of God (John 1:12) | The Spirit who says I belong (Romans 8:16) | I come back to Him the same hour after a fall |
| A new creation (2 Corinthians 5:17) | Forgiveness of my sins (Ephesians 1:7) | I tell the truth instead of hiding |
| A son and an heir (Galatians 4:7) | Access to my heavenly Father (Ephesians 2:18) | I pray before I scroll |
| Complete in Him (Colossians 2:10) | All I need for life and godliness (2 Peter 1:3) | I serve others because I am already full |

## The end of this course

Over six lessons we have walked one path. You are not an orphan. You are adopted as a full heir. You can call Him Abba. You can lean on Him like a child. You work from His approval, not for it. And now you know the order: I am, I have, I do.

You will forget this order some days. Most of us do. When you notice, come back to the first line. It has not changed.
