You are washing the dishes, driving to work, or praying. And out of nowhere a thought shows up that you never asked for. An image. A cruel word about someone. A doubt about God. Something you would never say out loud.

Then the second thought comes, and it is usually worse. "What kind of Christian thinks that? Something must be wrong with me."

This first lesson is about that second thought. Because a lot of people lose the battle there, not at the first one.

## Where we are in this course

This course has six short lessons on how to renew your mind. We start with the most basic question: what is a thought, and when does it become a sin? If you get this wrong, every other step feels heavy, because you will feel guilty for things that are not yours to carry.

If you want the whole method on one page, read [Take Every Thought Captive](/teachings/take-every-thought-captive-2026). Here we go one step at a time.

## Are bad thoughts a sin?

Not when they first arrive.

A thought that shows up uninvited is temptation. Temptation is not sin. Hebrews says Jesus "was in all points tempted as we are, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15). In the wilderness, the devil put real offers in front of Him: bread, a shortcut, the kingdoms of the world (Matthew 4:1-11). Jesus heard them. He did not sin by hearing them.

So if Jesus could be tempted and stay sinless, a thought crossing your mind does not make you guilty.

## Where the line really is

James gives us the order. "Each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own desires and enticed. Then, when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin" (James 1:14-15).

Notice the steps. First you are drawn, then enticed, then desire conceives, then sin is born. Something has to be conceived. That is the moment you say yes on the inside. You stop treating the thought as a visitor and start treating it as a plan. You replay it on purpose. You add details. You go looking for more.

Jesus said the same about lust. "Whoever looks at a woman to lust for her has already committed adultery with her in his heart" (Matthew 5:28). The words "to lust" matter. It is the look with a purpose. The first glance that catches you is not the same as the second look you choose.

So the question is not, "Did this thought come?" The question is, "What did I do with it once I noticed it?"

## Why guilt at the first flash is a trap

Here is how the trap works.

A thought arrives. You feel instantly dirty. You tell yourself you have already failed. And once you feel like you have already failed, giving in does not seem like a big step anymore. "I am already guilty, so why fight?"

Do you see it? The guilt did not protect you. It pushed you toward the thing you did not want.

Your heavenly Father does not work that way. "He knows our frame; He remembers that we are dust" (Psalm 103:14). He is not shocked by what passes through your mind. He knows you live in a fallen world, with a body, a memory, and an enemy who likes to throw things at you. He is not waiting to condemn you for a thought you did not choose. He is waiting for you to turn to Him with it.

## Can Christians have intrusive thoughts?

Yes. Many sincere believers have thoughts that horrify them. Violent pictures, sexual images, blasphemous words about God. Often these thoughts stick precisely because you hate them.

Here is something to hold on to. The fact that a thought upsets you is often a sign that you do not agree with it. Someone who wants a thing does not usually lie awake afraid of it.

If thoughts like this come all day, if you spend hours checking, confessing the same thing again and again, or trying to feel sure you did not mean it, please talk to a doctor or a counselor as well as your pastor. Thoughts like that can be part of anxiety or OCD. Getting help is not a lack of faith. It is wisdom, and your heavenly Father can work through it.

## What to do the moment a thought arrives

Keep it simple. You do not need to win a debate.

1. **Name it.** "That is a thought, not me." You are not your thoughts. You are a child of God who just had one.
2. **Hand it over.** "Father, I hand this to You." That is a real prayer. It takes two seconds.
3. **Turn to the next thing.** Go back to the dishes, the road, the person in front of you. You do not have to stare at the thought to defeat it.

Trying hard not to think about something often keeps it in front of you. Turning your attention somewhere else works better than pushing.

## Knocking or agreeing

| What is happening | Knocking (temptation) | Agreeing (sin starting) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it arrived | Uninvited, out of nowhere | I went looking for it |
| What I did next | Noticed it and turned away | Replayed it and added details |
| How I feel about it | I do not want it | I am enjoying it and want more |
| What to do | Hand it to the Father, move on | Confess it, receive forgiveness, move on |

If you find yourself in the right column, do not despair. "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). Confess it once, honestly, and move on. We will talk much more about that voice in lesson 3.

## Do this today

The next time an unwanted thought shows up today, do not punish yourself for it. Say quietly, "That is a thought, not me." Then say, "Father, I hand this to You," and turn your attention to the next thing in front of you. Tonight, write down how many times you did it.
