Many men who carry trauma live with a quiet rule: do not tell anyone. Some learned it as boys. "What happens in this house stays in this house." Some told once, were not believed, and never tried again. Some are afraid that if they open the door even a little, everything will come pouring out.

So they carry it alone. And it gets heavier.

This lesson is about finding the right people, and going at the right pace.

## Where we are in this course

So far you have separated what happened from who you are, made a plan for triggers, learned to lament, and seen what forgiveness is and is not. Every one of those steps goes better with someone beside you.

## Who can I talk to about trauma?

There are a few kinds of people who can help, and each plays a different part.

**A trained counselor.** Someone who is trained to help people with trauma can help you work through memories at a safe pace, find patterns in your triggers, and learn tools for your body and mind. Look for someone licensed, and ask whether they have experience with trauma. Many counselors are Christians, and many who are not will still respect your faith. If your harm happened in a church setting, you may prefer someone outside that world. That is fine.

**Your doctor.** If you are not sleeping, have constant tension or pain, cannot eat, or are using something to numb yourself, tell your doctor. Your body is part of this.

**A pastor or mature believer.** Someone who can pray with you, remind you what God says, and walk with you over months. A good pastor will also tell you when you need more help than they can give.

**One or two close friends.** People who know you, can sit with you, and can check in on a bad day.

"In the multitude of counselors there is safety" (Proverbs 11:14). Notice that it says counselors, plural. No single person has to carry everything.

## Who are safe people?

Not everyone who loves you is safe to talk to about this. Some people mean well and still do damage. Here are some signs to look for over time.

Safe people usually:

- Listen more than they talk.
- Keep what you share private.
- Believe you, and do not argue with your experience.
- Do not rush you to forgive, get over it, or confront anyone.
- Do not make your story about themselves.
- Can hear hard things without panic or disgust.
- Are the same person a week later.

Be careful with people who gossip, minimize ("it was not that bad"), blame ("why did you not leave?"), give quick spiritual fixes, or need you to take care of their feelings about your story.

"He who walks with wise men will be wise" (Proverbs 13:20). You get to choose who walks with you.

## How to talk about trauma without dumping

Some people hold everything in for years, and then, in one late-night conversation, tell someone everything. Afterward they feel exposed and ashamed, and the other person often does not know what to do. The friendship can get strained.

There is a better way. Think of it as opening a door a little at a time.

- **Start small.** "I went through some hard things as a kid, and it still affects me sometimes." That is enough for a first conversation.
- **Watch how they respond.** Did they listen? Did they keep it private? If so, you can share a little more next time.
- **Save the deep work for a counselor.** Friends are for walking with you. A counselor is trained to help you work through the memories themselves.
- **Ask for what you need.** "I do not need advice. I just need you to know." Or, "Can you check on me Thursday? That is a hard day."

You never owe anyone the details of what happened to you. Not friends, not church, not even your spouse, unless and until you are ready.

## Can talking about trauma make it worse?

Sometimes, yes, if it is done too fast, with the wrong person, or without support. Going over a painful memory again and again without help can stir it up rather than settle it.

That is one reason a trained counselor matters. They know how to go at a pace your body and mind can handle. With the right help, talking usually helps much more than silence does.

## Jesus chose who stayed close

On the worst night of His life, Jesus took Peter, James and John a little further into the garden than the others. He told them, "My soul is exceedingly sorrowful, even to death. Stay here and watch" (Mark 14:34).

He did not tell the crowd. He did not tell everyone. He chose a few close friends and asked them to stay near. Even the Son of God did not walk through His darkest night completely alone.

"Two are better than one ... For if they fall, one will lift up his companion" (Ecclesiastes 4:9-10).
