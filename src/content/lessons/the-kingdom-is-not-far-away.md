So far you have seen that grace is a gift and that faith is trust, not a feeling. Now we come to the word Jesus used more than almost any other. He talked about the Kingdom constantly. In the parables, in the Sermon on the Mount, in the prayer He taught His disciples.

Yet many Christians could not tell you what He meant. Some think it means heaven after you die. Some think it means the church building. Some think it is a political goal. Let's look at what He actually said.

## What does "kingdom of God" mean?

At its root, a kingdom is the rule of a king. The Greek word in the New Testament, basileia, first means reign or royal rule. The territory and the people come second. They exist because the king rules.

So the kingdom of God is, first of all, God reigning as King. "The LORD has established His throne in heaven, And His kingdom rules over all" (Psalm 103:19). "Your kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, And Your dominion endures throughout all generations" (Psalm 145:13).

When Jesus talks about entering the Kingdom, He means coming under that rule. Not a new address. A new King.

## What does "the kingdom of God is at hand" mean?

The first thing Jesus preached was this: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand. Repent, and believe in the gospel" (Mark 1:15).

At hand means near, within reach. The long wait was over, because the King Himself had arrived. When Jesus healed the sick and set people free, He said it plainly: "If I cast out demons by the Spirit of God, surely the kingdom of God has come upon you" (Matthew 12:28).

His call was not, "Try harder to be good." It was, "Turn around and trust Me. The King is here."

## Is the kingdom of God here now, or someday?

Both. The Pharisees asked Jesus when the Kingdom would come. He answered, "The kingdom of God does not come with observation; nor will they say, 'See here!' or 'See there!' For indeed, the kingdom of God is within you" (Luke 17:20-21). Some translations say "in your midst." He was standing right there among them.

So the Kingdom has begun. Paul says the Father "has delivered us from the power of darkness and conveyed us into the kingdom of the Son of His love" (Colossians 1:13). Has delivered. Already. If you belong to Jesus, you are already under His rule.

But it is not finished. Jesus taught us to pray, "Your kingdom come. Your will be done On earth as it is in heaven" (Matthew 6:10). We still pray that because His will is not yet done everywhere. When Jesus returns, His rule will be complete. "Of the increase of His government and peace There will be no end" (Isaiah 9:7).

Now, and not yet. Started, and growing.

## Is the kingdom of God the same as the kingdom of heaven?

Yes. Matthew, writing mostly to Jewish readers, usually says "kingdom of heaven." Mark and Luke usually say "kingdom of God." You can see it in the same teaching. Matthew records, "Blessed are the poor in spirit, For theirs is the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 5:3). Luke records, "Blessed are you poor, For yours is the kingdom of God" (Luke 6:20). Same Kingdom, two ways of saying it.

## Is the kingdom of God just heaven?

Heaven is part of it, but the Kingdom is bigger than where you go when you die. Jesus said, "Unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God" (John 3:3). New birth is how you enter, and it starts now, in this life.

Paul describes what the Kingdom looks like in a person: "For the kingdom of God is not eating and drinking, but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit" (Romans 14:17). Those are things you can have on a Tuesday afternoon.

## It starts small

Jesus said the Kingdom "is like a mustard seed, which a man took and sowed in his field, which indeed is the least of all the seeds; but when it is grown it is greater than the herbs and becomes a tree" (Matthew 13:31-32).

That is how it often works in a man's life too. Not a dramatic overnight change. A small seed. One area handed over. Then another. Over time, something grows that you could not have built.

## Not of this world

Jesus told Pilate, "My kingdom is not of this world" (John 18:36). His Kingdom does not come by a political party, an election, or a culture war. Christians can and should care about their country and vote their conscience. But the Kingdom of God is not on any ballot. It comes when people bow to the King.

That is good news for you. You do not have to wait for the world to change before you can live under His rule. You can start today, wherever you are.
