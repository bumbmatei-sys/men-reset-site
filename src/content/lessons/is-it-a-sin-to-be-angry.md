Many Christian men carry a quiet confusion about anger. Some think every flash of anger is a sin, so they push it down until it leaks out sideways. Others think their anger is always justified, because they are right about the problem. Both end up in the same place: a home that walks on eggshells.

This course is not about never feeling angry. It is about anger that does not destroy your home, your marriage, or your own heart.

## Is it a sin to be angry?

No, not by itself. Paul writes, "Be angry, and do not sin" (Ephesians 4:26). He is quoting Psalm 4:4. The Bible assumes you will feel anger. It tells you what not to do with it.

Jesus was angry. When religious leaders cared more about their rules than about a man with a withered hand, "He had looked around at them with anger, being grieved by the hardness of their hearts" (Mark 3:5). Then He healed the man. His anger moved Him toward the hurting person, not against him.

So the feeling is not the problem. The problem is what the feeling does next.

## When does anger become sin?

Anger becomes sin through what you do with it, how long you keep it, and why you feel it.

**What you do with it.** A few verses later Paul names the fruit of sinful anger: "bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, and evil speaking" (Ephesians 4:31). Clamor is shouting. Evil speaking is words meant to wound. Add threats, slammed doors, punching walls, contempt, and the silent treatment used as punishment. If people in your home are afraid of you, your anger has crossed a line.

**How long you keep it.** "Do not let the sun go down on your wrath" (Ephesians 4:26). Anger kept overnight turns into a grudge. A grudge kept for weeks turns into bitterness. Paul says it gives "place to the devil" (Ephesians 4:27). A foothold is a small space. It does not stay small.

**Why you feel it.** Much of our anger is not about justice at all. It is about being inconvenienced, embarrassed, or not getting our way. James says it plainly: "the wrath of man does not produce the righteousness of God" (James 1:20). We will look at what is underneath in lesson 5.

## Is it a sin to go to bed angry?

Ephesians 4:26 is not a rule that you must solve every argument before midnight. Some conversations need rest and daylight. It means you do not keep your anger. Before you sleep, you hand it to your heavenly Father, you own your part, and if you need to, you say, "I want to finish this tomorrow, and I am not leaving it." That is very different from rolling over with your back turned and planning your case.

## Anger can be a signal

Anger often shows up because something matters. Someone was treated unfairly. Something you value was ignored. A line was crossed. That is why it can be useful. It tells you to pay attention.

But a signal is not a command. A smoke alarm tells you to check the kitchen. It does not tell you to break the windows. When anger says "something is wrong here," your job is to slow down and find out what, not to unload it on whoever is closest.

## Anger can also destroy

Here is the hard part. The people most likely to receive a man's anger are the people he loves most. His wife. His kids. The ones who cannot easily leave the room.

"He who is slow to wrath has great understanding, But he who is impulsive exalts folly" (Proverbs 14:29). One sharp sentence can take a minute to say and a year to heal. Your children learn what a man is by watching what you do when you are frustrated. That is not meant to crush you. It is meant to wake you up.

## Can a Christian have anger issues?

Yes. Being a believer does not mean you no longer struggle. It means you no longer struggle alone. Many good men who love God have a temper that hurts their family. The way forward is not to hide it but to bring it into the light, to your heavenly Father and to someone you trust. If you do not have that person yet, [How to Find a Christian Accountability Partner](/teachings/christian-accountability-partner) walks you through it.

## When to get more help

If your anger has led you to hurt, threaten, or frighten anyone in your home, get help today. Talk to a licensed Christian counselor and your pastor, and ask about programs for men who want to stop hurting their families. If anyone is in danger right now, get emergency help. Do not ask your wife or kids to keep it quiet. If you feel very low or out of control, tell someone today. A licensed Christian counselor is wisdom, not weak faith.
