You noticed the rise. Your jaw is tight, your voice is getting louder, and you are at a 5 or 6. Now what?

This lesson is about the next ten minutes. Not the whole problem. Just the next ten minutes.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 1 you named what your anger looks like. In lesson 2 you learned your early signs and where anger usually rises at home. Now you need a plan for the moment itself, so you are not making it up while your heart is pounding.

## How do you control anger in the moment?

In the moment your only job is to not make it worse. That is it. You do not have to win, explain yourself, or solve the problem right now. Most damage in a marriage is not done by the problem. It is done by what was said while trying to solve it angry.

"So then, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath" (James 1:19). Slow to speak is the first move. Close your mouth before you open it.

## Step away without disappearing

Leaving the room is often the wisest thing you can do. But how you leave matters.

Some men leave by storming out, slamming a door, or going silent for two days. That is not a pause. That is punishment, and the people left behind feel it. Others leave and never come back to the conversation. That is not peace. It is avoidance.

A good step-away has three parts:

1. **Say it calmly.** "I'm getting too angry to talk well. I'm going to take twenty minutes, and I will come back."
2. **Go somewhere calm.** Another room, the porch, a walk around the block.
3. **Come back when you said.** If you need longer, come back and say so: "I need another half hour. I have not forgotten."

Tell your wife your step-away sentence on a calm day, so when you use it she knows it means "I'm protecting us," not "I'm done with you."

## Use the pause well

A pause only helps if you use it to settle down, not to wind yourself up further.

- **Breathe.** Slow breath in, longer breath out. Do it ten times.
- **Pray one sentence.** "Set a guard, O LORD, over my mouth" (Psalm 141:3). Or simply, "Father, help me. I don't want to hurt them."
- **Move.** Walk, carry something, do push-ups, wash a dish. Your body has energy to burn.
- **Stop rehearsing.** If you spend the pause replaying her words and building your case, you will come back angrier. When you catch yourself, say, "Not now," and go back to breathing or praying.
- **Do not drive, drink, or text.** Driving angry is dangerous. Alcohol lowers the guard you need. Angry texts say what you would never say to her face.

## A word you will not say

Some words do damage that takes years to undo. Decide now, while you are calm, which ones you will not say no matter how angry you are. Write them down.

Common ones men choose:

- Name-calling of any kind
- "You always" and "You never"
- "You made me do this"
- Threats to leave, divorce, or take the kids, said to win a fight
- Cursing at her or the children
- Bringing up old failures to win today's argument

"Let no corrupt word proceed out of your mouth" (Ephesians 4:29) is not about being polite. It is about protecting the people God gave you.

## A soft answer

Sometimes you cannot step away. You are in the car, or a child needs you right now. Then lower your voice on purpose.

"A soft answer turns away wrath, But a harsh word stirs up anger" (Proverbs 15:1). When you lower your voice, the room usually follows. When you raise it, the room usually follows too.

Try a simple, honest sentence: "I'm frustrated. I need a minute before I answer." That is a soft answer. It is true, and it does not wound anyone.

## What if she doesn't want me to leave?

Some wives feel abandoned when a husband walks away mid-conversation, especially if leaving has meant days of silence before. That is why the sentence and the return matter so much. If she still finds it hard, agree on the plan together on a calm day. If she does not want to continue the conversation when you come back, respect that, and set a time later. A pause is for both of you, not a tool to control when she must talk.

## When to get more help

If you ever feel like you might hurt someone, leave right away and call someone. If you have hurt, threatened, or frightened anyone in your home, get help today. Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at **1-800-799-7233**, and call **911** if anyone is in danger. Do not ask anyone to keep it quiet. If you feel very low, out of control, or unsafe, call or text **988**, any time. A licensed Christian counselor can help you build a plan that holds. That is wisdom, not weak faith. For more on praying when you keep falling short, read [How to Pray When You Keep Falling](/teachings/how-to-pray-when-you-keep-falling).
