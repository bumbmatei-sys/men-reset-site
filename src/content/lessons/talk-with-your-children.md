Many men are afraid of the big talks. Faith, sex, money, the hard questions. So they put them off, and then one day the child is fifteen and the door is shut.

Here is the good news. The big talks are much easier when there have been a thousand small ones first.

## Where we are in this course

You have looked at what leading means, your own walk, being present, and loving your wife. Now you turn to your children. This is for fathers of little ones, teenagers, and grown children, and for the man who will be a father one day.

## How do I be a Christian father?

Start with what your children see. Long before they understand theology, they form a picture of God from the way their father treats them. "As a father pities his children, So the Lord pities those who fear Him" (Psalm 103:13).

You will not be a perfect picture. No earthly father is. But you can point them to the real thing by being patient, being there, and telling the truth.

In the story of the prodigal son, the father "saw him and had compassion, and ran and fell on his neck and kissed him" (Luke 15:20). That is the Father you are trying to show them.

## What does "do not provoke your children" mean?

"And you, fathers, do not provoke your children to wrath, but bring them up in the training and admonition of the Lord" (Ephesians 6:4). Paul says it again in another letter: "Fathers, do not provoke your children, lest they become discouraged" (Colossians 3:21).

Provoking looks like harshness, sarcasm, constant correction, mocking, rules with no reasons, promises you do not keep, and anger that comes out of nowhere. Over time a child stops trying and stops talking.

Training looks like patience, clear and fair limits, explaining why, and following through calmly. "A soft answer turns away wrath, But a harsh word stirs up anger" (Proverbs 15:1).

If your temper is the main thing in the way, the [Anger That Doesn't Destroy](/tracks/anger-that-doesnt-destroy) course walks through it step by step.

## How do I get my kids to talk to me?

Some ways that help:

- **Talk side by side, not face to face.** Kids, especially boys, open up in the car, on a walk, or while fixing something together.
- **Ask questions that cannot be answered with "fine."** "What made you laugh today?" "What was hard?"
- **Listen to the whole answer.** Do not jump in with a lesson the moment they share something.
- **Stay calm when they tell you hard things.** If you explode, they learn not to tell you next time.
- **Say "thank you for telling me."** Even when what they said worries you.

## How do I talk to my kids about faith?

Keep it real and keep it short. Pray out loud at meals and at bedtime. Read a short Bible story with little ones, or a few verses with older kids, and ask, "What does this tell us about God?" Tell them how God has helped you. Let them see you pray when things go wrong.

You do not need a perfect family devotion. A simple, faithful habit says more than a long, rare one.

## What about the hard talks?

Some talks feel too big. Sex, porn, safety online, money, death. Do not hand these to the internet. Your child needs to hear about them from a father who loves them.

If you have a son and you do not know how to start the talk about porn, read [How to Talk to Your Son About Porn](/teachings/talk-to-your-son-about-porn). It gives you words to use and what to avoid.

## Should I tell my kids about my own mistakes?

Yes, wisely and at their level. You do not need to share every detail. But a father who says, "I got that wrong, I'm sorry," teaches a child that faith is real life, that grace is real, and that it is safe to be honest. Children who never see their father admit a mistake often learn to hide their own.

## When a child is not safe

If a child tells you about harm, abuse, or thoughts of hurting themselves, believe them and get help. Call **911** if they are in danger right now. Call or text **988** if they or you are in crisis. If anyone in your home has been hurt, threatened, or frightened, including by you, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at **1-800-799-7233** today, and do not ask anyone to keep it quiet. A Christian counselor is wisdom, not weak faith.
