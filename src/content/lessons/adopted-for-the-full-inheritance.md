A lot of people hear "adopted" and think of second place. Like you got into the family, but the real kids come first.

That is not how the Bible uses the word. When God adopts you, you are a full heir. In Christ you are a son or daughter, with everything that comes with it.

## From slave to son

Galatians 4 starts with a picture from normal life. A father owns everything, and his young son will inherit it one day. But while the boy is still small, he is told when to eat, when to sleep, what to do. He is an heir, and he does not live like one yet. Paul says he "does not differ at all from a slave" (Galatians 4:1).

But the father sets a day when that changes (Galatians 4:2). For us, that day came when God sent His Son, so we could receive the adoption as sons (Galatians 4:4-5). God did not slowly promote us. He declared it. "You are no longer a slave but a son, and if a son, then an heir of God through Christ" (Galatians 4:7).

So the question is not whether you will one day be good enough to get something. The question is whether you believe what God already said about you.

## What a joint heir means

Romans 8:17 says: "and if children, then heirs, heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ."

Let me explain "joint heir" in plain words. When two people inherit together, one does not get the big half and the other a small one. They share the same thing. Whatever belongs to Jesus, He shares with you.

Now let's be honest about the rest of the verse. It says "if indeed we suffer with Him, that we may also be glorified together." Following Jesus will cost you something in this life. Not everyone will understand your choices. But the same verse says the glory is shared too. You do not walk the hard part alone, and you do not miss the good part.

## So what do I have?

So what do I actually have? Basically, everything Jesus paid for.

Ephesians 1:3 says the heavenly Father has *already* blessed us with every spiritual blessing in Christ. Not "will bless." Already blessed. And 2 Peter 1:3 says His divine power has given us all things that pertain to life and godliness.

"Already" is the key word. God is not waiting for you to qualify before He hands it over. Jesus did the work, and it is already in your name.

## What is in the inheritance

You have forgiveness and a clean record. Peace with God. The Holy Spirit living in you. Open access to your heavenly Father any time, day or night: "Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace" (Hebrews 4:16). A new nature, not the old one patched up. And eternal life that cannot fade: "an inheritance incorruptible, undefiled, that does not fade away, reserved in heaven for you" (1 Peter 1:4).

"Reserved" means it is kept for you. Nobody can take it or spend it before you get there.

## The guarantee in you

Ephesians 1:13-14 says the Holy Spirit is the guarantee of our inheritance.

Think about a down payment on a house. The money you hand over at the start is real money. It is part of the full price. And it promises the rest is coming. That is the Holy Spirit in you. He is already part of the full amount, and He is the promise that the rest is on the way.

In the next lesson we will look at what it sounds like to call your heavenly Father "Abba". For now, just know that the guarantee lives in you.

## Robe, ring and sandals

[In the last lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/you-are-not-an-orphan) we saw the son come home hoping to be a hired servant. Before he finished his speech, the father put three things on him.

A robe. Sandals. A ring.

In that culture, hired servants went barefoot. Sandals marked you as a son of the house. The ring was a sign of family authority. So the father was not just dressing him up for the party. He was answering the speech. "You are not my hired servant. You are my son."

It is the same with you. You may still talk to God like a servant asking for a job. He answers by treating you as His son or daughter.

## Rich on paper, living poor

Many believers are rich on paper and live poor. It is like having money in an account you never check. The balance does not change because you stopped looking at it. But your life does not change either.

The inheritance is received by faith and patience. Hebrews 6:12 says we should imitate those who "through faith and patience inherit the promises." Not by rushing. Not by begging. Faith is our positive response to what He already did. It is not a lever to make God do something He did not want to do. But without faith, the promise just sits there, unused.

And patience matters, because you do not see everything at once. God's grace is unlimited from day one. It does not grow. What grows is how much of it you experience and walk in as you grow. If you want more on that, read [What Is Grace](/teachings/what-is-grace-2026).

## Learning to live as an heir

A small child only asks for things. A grown son or daughter learns to take care of what was given to them. That is what growing up looks like here. You move from begging for blessings to living from what you already have, and you look after it well.

## The lie and the truth

Here are thoughts that keep people living poor, and what God says back.

| The lie | The truth |
| --- | --- |
| "I have to be good enough before God gives me anything." | He has already blessed us with every spiritual blessing in Christ. (Ephesians 1:3) |
| "Other Christians got more than me." | You are a joint heir with Christ. What is His, He shares with you. (Romans 8:17) |
| "I can only come to God on a good day." | Come boldly to the throne of grace, to find help in time of need. (Hebrews 4:16) |
| "I am still just a slave waiting for something." | You are no longer a slave but a son or daughter, and an heir through Christ. (Galatians 4:7) |
| "What if God holds back the good things?" | He who did not spare His own Son, how shall He not with Him also freely give us all things? (Romans 8:32) |

## Do this today

Today, write down three things this lesson says you have in Christ. Put the note where you will see it tomorrow morning.

Then each morning this week, read one of the verses above out loud and say, "This is already mine in Jesus." Pay attention to the one that is hardest to say. That is where the lie is still living.
