When people try to start reading the Bible in the morning, they often aim too high. A whole hour. Three chapters. A long journal entry.

It lasts a week. Then one busy morning breaks it. Then guilt comes in. And soon you are avoiding your Bible because it reminds you of what you did not do.

That is not what your heavenly Father wants for you. He is not waiting with a stopwatch. He wants to be with you.

## How do I start reading the Bible in the morning?

Start with a few minutes. Five is a good number. If you have more, wonderful. If you have less, still sit down for one passage.

Then make one decision the night before: what you will read. Do not wake up and wonder where to open. That small question is enough to send you back to the phone.

Here is a simple place to start. The Gospel of Mark. It is short, it moves fast, and it is all about Jesus. Read a little each morning, from where you stopped the day before. When you finish Mark, go to John. Or go through the Psalms, one each morning.

Leave your Bible open on the table where you will sit. When you come in from your chore, it is already waiting.

## Read until one line stands out

You do not have to finish a chapter. Read slowly. Out loud if you can. Paul wrote, "So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God" (Romans 10:17). Hearing your own voice read His word helps it land.

When one line catches you, stop there. That is your line for today.

Then say it back to your heavenly Father in your own words. If you read, "Follow Me, and I will make you become fishers of men" (Mark 1:17), you might say, "Father, I want to follow You today, at work too." That is prayer. It does not need to be long or fancy.

Then ask Him for one thing for your day. Patience with your kids. Courage at work. Strength in the hour that is usually hard. One thing.

## What Bible verses should I read in the morning?

Any part of His word can feed you. But some passages were written for mornings, and they are good to come back to.

"Through the LORD's mercies we are not consumed, Because His compassions fail not. They are new every morning; Great is Your faithfulness" (Lamentations 3:22-23).

"Cause me to hear Your lovingkindness in the morning, For in You do I trust; Cause me to know the way in which I should walk, For I lift up my soul to You" (Psalm 143:8).

"Your word is a lamp to my feet And a light to my path" (Psalm 119:105).

Notice the order in Psalm 143:8. First, His love. Then, the way to walk. We often want directions first. He gives His love first, and then the directions make sense.

## Daily bread, not a weekly stock

When Israel was in the desert, God gave them manna. "And the people shall go out and gather a certain quota every day" (Exodus 16:4). They could not store it for the week. It had to be gathered each morning.

Jesus said, "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God" (Matthew 4:4).

That is why a few minutes every day matters more than a long session once a week. You are not filling a tank. You are eating today's bread.

## It is time with a person, not a streak

Bible apps love streaks. Streaks can help. But be careful. A streak can turn your time with your heavenly Father into a scoreboard. And when the number breaks, it feels like the whole thing broke.

It did not. His love did not reset because you missed Tuesday. Just open the Bible again on Wednesday, from where you were.

## What if it feels dry?

Some mornings you will read and feel nothing. That is normal.

Here is something I learned. A son is driven by delight. But on the days he does not feel like praying, he uses discipline to get back to delight. Discipline is not the opposite of love. It is the road back to it on the dry days.

So read anyway. Tell Him the truth: "Father, I feel nothing this morning, but I am here." That is one of the most honest prayers there is. Holiness grows from meeting Him, not from feeling a certain way.

## A five-minute plan

| Minute | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Sit down. Say, "Father, I am here. Speak to me." |
| 2 to 3 | Read slowly from where you stopped yesterday, until one line stands out |
| 4 | Say that line back to Him in your own words |
| 5 | Ask Him for one thing for your day, and thank Him |

If you have more time, read longer or write your line on a card to carry with you. For a fuller twenty-minute version, see [A Christian Morning Routine for Men With a Job](/teachings/christian-morning-routine-men-2026).
