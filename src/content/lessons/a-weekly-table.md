Friendship needs time. Church gives you a family. But most loneliness still happens on an ordinary weeknight, at home, after work. This lesson is about that evening.

The answer is not a big event. It is one regular table.

## Why one weekly table works

Think back to lesson 2. Friendship grows from repeated time together. One great evening is nice. The same evening, every week, is how friends are made.

A weekly table is simple. Same day. Same time. A meal, a coffee, or a walk. Two to six people. It happens whether you feel social or not.

The beauty of it is that nobody has to ask, "When should we meet up?" Everyone already knows. Tuesday night is soup at your place. Saturday at eight is coffee and a walk. Over a few months, a table like that turns acquaintances into friends.

## Jesus at the table

Much of Jesus' ministry happened over food. He ate at Levi's house with "many tax collectors and sinners" (Mark 2:15). He told Zacchaeus, "Zacchaeus, make haste and come down, for today I must stay at your house" (Luke 19:5). After He rose, two disciples on the road to Emmaus recognized Him at a table. "He took bread, blessed and broke it, and gave it to them. Then their eyes were opened and they knew Him" (Luke 24:30-31).

The early church did the same. They were "breaking bread from house to house" and eating "with gladness and simplicity of heart" (Acts 2:46). Simplicity. Not a show.

## What is Christian hospitality?

It is making room in your life and your home for other people, because God made room for you.

Peter wrote, "Be hospitable to one another without grumbling" (1 Peter 4:9). Paul listed it right next to helping people in need: "distributing to the needs of the saints, given to hospitality" (Romans 12:13).

Notice what is not in those verses. Nothing about a big house, good cooking, or a clean garage. Christian hospitality is an open door and a simple meal. Some people have a gift for it and could cook for fifty with joy. Most of us do not, and that is fine. You do not need a gift to set out a few bowls of soup.

Jesus also said to invite people who would not usually get invited. "When you give a feast, invite the poor, the maimed, the lame, the blind. And you will be blessed, because they cannot repay you" (Luke 14:13-14). Somewhere near you is a widower, a new guy at work, a single dad, or a student far from home who would love to be asked.

## How to practice Christian hospitality

**Pick the evening.** Choose the night that is usually the loneliest for you. That is the one to fill.

**Pick the format.** It can be a meal at your place, breakfast at a diner, coffee after church, a walk on Saturday morning, or a game night. If your place is small or messy, meet somewhere else. A table at a cheap diner works.

**Pick the people.** Two or three to start. A friend from lesson 2, someone from your church group, a neighbor, one person who is often left out.

**Keep it cheap and simple.** Chili, pasta, pizza, paper plates. If it costs too much or takes too much work, you will stop. You can also take turns bringing food.

**Make it the same time every week.** Send one text the day before: "Still on for Tuesday at 7?" That is it.

**Pray, briefly.** Thank your heavenly Father for the food and the people before you eat. One sentence. You are not running a Bible study, just inviting Him to the table.

**Do not cancel because only one person can come.** A table for two is still a table. Some of the best conversations happen when only one person shows up.

## What if I am married?

A weekly table is good for married men too. Many married men are lonely, with no friends outside the house. Talk with your wife first, and plan it together. Maybe you invite another couple once a week, or you meet a brother for breakfast before work while she has her own time with friends. It should add to your home, not take away from it.

## What if I do not have anyone to invite yet?

Then start with the people you have. Your parents, a sibling, one coworker. Or join someone else's table. Ask your church if there is a group that eats together, and go. Hospitality can start with saying yes to an invitation, too.
