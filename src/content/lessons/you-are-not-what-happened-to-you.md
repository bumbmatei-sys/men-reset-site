Some things that happen to a person do not stay in the past. A childhood where you were never safe. A beating. A betrayal by someone you trusted. An accident, a war, a sudden death. Sexual abuse. Years later, you can be a grown man with a job and a family, and part of you is still standing in that room.

If that is you, start here. You do not have to tell anyone your story to take this course. You do not have to relive it. This first lesson is about one thing: what happened to you is not who you are.

## What does the Bible say about trauma?

The word "trauma" is not in most English Bibles, but the thing itself is everywhere in it. Joseph was thrown in a pit and sold by his own brothers. David spent years running from a king who wanted him dead. Tamar was violated and left desolate. Jeremiah was beaten and thrown into a cistern. Jesus Himself was betrayed, abandoned, beaten and killed.

The Bible does not hide any of that. It also never tells hurt people to smile and move on. It says, "The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart" (Psalm 34:18). It says, "He heals the brokenhearted And binds up their wounds" (Psalm 147:3).

When Jesus stood up in His hometown to read about His mission, He chose these words: "He has sent Me to heal the brokenhearted, To proclaim liberty to the captives ... To set at liberty those who are oppressed" (Luke 4:18). Healing a broken heart was not a side project for Jesus. It was part of why He came.

So if you are carrying something heavy, you are not a problem case. You are exactly the kind of person Jesus said He came for.

## What happened is real. It is not your name

Here is what trauma often does. It does not just leave a memory. It leaves a sentence.

A boy who was hit every time he made a mistake can grow into a man who hears, "One mistake and you are worthless." A child who was abused can grow up believing, "I am dirty," even though he did nothing wrong. Someone who was betrayed can carry, "Nobody can be trusted. I am on my own." A man who could not stop something terrible from happening can carry, "It was my fault."

These sentences were formed in moments when you could not think clearly, could not get away, and could not make sense of it. They are not conclusions you reached calmly. They are scars that learned to talk.

Some people also made quiet promises in those moments. "I will never cry again." "I will never need anyone." "I do not need a father." Those promises kept you going once. Today they may be keeping you alone.

None of those sentences is what God says about you. "Behold what manner of love the Father has bestowed on us, that we should be called children of God!" (1 John 3:1). Your name, in His house, is son.

And if anyone harmed you, the shame of what they did belongs to them, not to you. Being hurt is not a sin.

## A word about your heavenly Father

If the person who hurt you was your father, or a man who should have protected you, the word "Father" may make you tense up. That is not a lack of faith. That is what it feels like when someone damaged a word that should have been safe.

Your heavenly Father is not like that person. He is not a bigger, stronger version of what happened to you. David wrote, "When my father and my mother forsake me, Then the LORD will take care of me" (Psalm 27:10). The Father Jesus showed us runs toward His son, does not hit him, does not use him, and does not leave.

You do not have to feel that yet. You can let it be true while your feelings catch up. The lesson [You Are Not an Orphan](/tracks/know-who-you-are/you-are-not-an-orphan) goes further into this, if the father wound is a big one for you.

## Can you heal from trauma?

Yes. Many people who have been through terrible things do get better, especially with good help and people around them. But healing is usually slower and less tidy than we want.

Healing does not mean you forget. It does not mean it never hurts again. It usually looks more like this: the memory slowly loses some of its power over today. You react a little less. You recover a little faster. You start to believe, a little at a time, what God says about you instead of what the hurt said.

Feeling better is not the test of whether God is working. Some weeks you will feel worse, especially when you start paying attention to things you had pushed down. That does not mean you are going backward.

## Healing is not a test of faith

Some Christians were told that if they just had more faith, the memories and reactions would go away. When they did not, they felt like failures twice over.

That is not what the Bible teaches. God uses people to heal people. "In the multitude of counselors there is safety" (Proverbs 11:14). Going to a trained counselor, especially one who understands trauma, is wisdom. So is talking to your doctor if you are not sleeping, cannot eat, or your body feels on alert all the time. We will come back to finding safe people in lesson 5.

## Your identity table

Fill this in for yourself. Start with just one row if that is all you can do today.

| What it left behind (in its exact words) | Where that sentence came from | What my heavenly Father says | A verse to stand on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "I am dirty." | | "You are My child." | 1 John 3:1 |
| "I am on my own." | | "I am near to the brokenhearted." | Psalm 34:18 |
| "It was my fault." | | "I bind up your wounds. What they did is not your shame." | Psalm 147:3 |
| | | | |

You do not need to write details of what happened in the middle column. A few words are enough, like "age nine" or "the army" or "after the divorce." Keep the paper somewhere private.

If an old sentence keeps coming back even after you answer it, the lesson [When the Same Thought Keeps Coming Back](/tracks/renew-your-mind/when-the-same-thought-keeps-coming-back) will help. And if a low or unsafe moment comes while you do this, stop. In the US you can call or text 988 at any hour.

## Do this today

Today, write down one sentence that what happened to you left behind, the way it sounds in your head. Next to it, write what your heavenly Father says about you instead, using one verse from this lesson. Read the second line out loud once in the morning and once at night.

## Reflect

If what happened to you had a voice, what would it say your name is? What does your heavenly Father call you instead?
