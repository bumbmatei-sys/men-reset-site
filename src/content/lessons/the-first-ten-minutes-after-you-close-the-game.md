In the last lesson you set a hard stop. A clock time, decided before you start, with an alarm across the room. If you have not tried it yet, start with [Set a Hard Stop](/tracks/quit-gaming-and-the-phone/set-a-hard-stop).

Here is what many people find out the first night they keep it. The alarm rings. They close the game. They feel good for about thirty seconds. Then they sit there, and the pull starts.

"Just check the patch notes." "Just watch one clip." "Maybe one quick match, I stopped early anyway." Or the phone comes out, and an hour goes in a different way.

This lesson is about those first ten minutes.

## Why the pull is strongest right after you stop

When you close a game, your mind is still running at game speed. You were busy, focused, reacting. Then it is suddenly quiet. That quiet can feel empty, even a little uncomfortable.

So your mind goes looking for the next thing that feels the same. The game is right there. The phone is right there. Both take one second to open.

That is not a sign you have failed. It is just how habits work. The end of one thing is the most likely moment to start the next. The good news is that this moment is short. If you get through the first ten minutes, the pull usually drops a lot.

## How do I stop playing video games when I want to keep going?

You leave.

Not later. Not after you scroll for a minute. When the stop comes, close the game, stand up, and walk out of the room where you play. Go to the kitchen, the hallway, outside, anywhere that is not that chair.

Distance does a lot of the work for you. Sitting in front of a dark screen with the controller in reach, you are arguing with yourself the whole time. Standing in the kitchen, the game is no longer one button away. It is a walk, a sit-down, and a login away. That extra effort is often enough.

Paul wrote that with every temptation, your heavenly Father "will also make the way of escape, that you may be able to bear it" (1 Corinthians 10:13). Sometimes the way of escape is very plain. It is the door of the room.

## Give your hands something to do

Once you are out of the room, do one physical thing. Something with your hands or your body.

Wash the dishes. Take out the trash. Make tomorrow's lunch. Lay out tomorrow's clothes. Do ten pushups. Walk around the block. Fold laundry. Water the plants.

It does not matter what it is, as long as it is real and it uses your hands. Your hands were busy for two hours. If they suddenly have nothing, they reach for the phone. Give them something else first.

This also helps your mind come down slowly. A few minutes of simple work lets your thoughts slow from game speed to evening speed.

## Pick it before the stop

The one thing has to be chosen before you start playing, not after you stop.

If you wait until the alarm rings to think about what to do next, the game wins. You are tired, your mind is still in the match, and "nothing" feels like the easiest answer. Nothing turns into the phone.

So when you write your stop time on the sticky note, write your one thing under it. "Stop 9:45. Then dishes." That is the whole plan.

## Do it before you negotiate

You will hear yourself start to bargain. "I stopped on time, I earned one more." "It is only a quick one." "Tomorrow I will be stricter."

Here is the rule. You do not have that conversation until your one thing is done. Hands first, then thinking.

Most of the time, by the time the dishes are done, you do not want to argue anymore. The urge has passed or become small. If it is still loud, do a second thing, or go talk to someone in the house.

If you have worked through [Break Free](/tracks/break-free), you will know this pattern from [How to Deal With an Urge in the First Ten Minutes](/tracks/break-free/how-to-deal-with-porn-urges). Name it, leave the room, and do one physical thing before you negotiate. It works for a game the same way it works for any other pull.

## Let your hands be busy and your heart talk

While your hands are working, your heart is free.

Say something simple to your heavenly Father. "Thank You that I stopped. Help me not to go back." Or tell Him what you are feeling. "I am restless. I wanted to keep playing. Be with me in this."

Self-control in the Bible is part of the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23). It is not you clenching your jaw until the urge goes away. It is you, under the Spirit's care, choosing the next good thing. Every time the pull shows up, it can become a reminder to turn to Him. "Lord, I need You right now." That is not a weak prayer. It is the right one.

## What about the people in the house?

Sometimes the best one thing is a person.

Go find your wife, your husband, a kid, your roommate. Ask how their day was. Sit with them for ten minutes, phone in another room. They have probably had the leftover version of you for a while. Ten minutes of you, fully present, means more than you think.
