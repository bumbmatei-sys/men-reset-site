In the first weeks after quitting, your days were built around not doing the thing. Every hour had a fight in it.

That cannot last forever, and it should not. Over time, freedom needs to become less about the fight and more about a life. Not an exciting life. A plain, steady one, with a shape that holds even when you are tired, busy, or bored.

This lesson is about building that shape, one week at a time.

## How do you maintain long-term sobriety?

When people ask this, they often expect a secret. There is not one. Most people who stay free for years describe something very ordinary. They sleep. They work. They stay close to God in small daily ways. They have people who know them. And they keep doing those things after the excitement fades.

Jesus said, "He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much" (Luke 16:10). In ministry school, one of my teachers kept coming back to this. God is not looking first for the brightest or the most gifted. He is looking for faithfulness. Small things, done again and again, are how the big things get built.

A plain week kept for a year will do more for you than a perfect week kept for three days.

## Think in weeks, not only days

The [Discipline and a Morning Routine](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine) course is about how to start each day well. This lesson zooms out. Some things that keep you free do not happen every day. You see a friend once or twice a week. You go to church on Sunday. You rest on one evening or one afternoon.

Thinking in weeks also protects you from all-or-nothing thinking. If Tuesday went badly, the week still has a shape. You do not throw away Wednesday through Sunday because of one day. You just pick up the next anchor.

## The five anchors

You do not need twenty habits. Five anchors carry most of the load.

**1. Sleep.** Late, tired nights are where a lot of falls happen. "It is vain for you to rise up early, To sit up late, To eat the bread of sorrows; For so He gives His beloved sleep" (Psalm 127:2). Pick a bedtime and a wake time you can keep on most nights. [A Wake Time You Can Keep](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/a-wake-time-you-can-keep) and [The Phone Sleeps in Another Room](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/the-phone-sleeps-in-another-room) cover the details.

**2. Honest work.** Work gives your days structure and your mind something good to chew on. Do it well and stop on time. Endless work leads to exhaustion, and exhaustion is a warning sign.

**3. A few minutes with Scripture.** Not an hour you will skip. A few minutes you will keep. Jesus "in the morning, having risen a long while before daylight, He went out and departed to a solitary place; and there He prayed" (Mark 1:35). Start small. [A Few Minutes With Scripture](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/a-few-minutes-with-scripture) shows how.

**4. One friend.** One person you see or call every week, who knows what you are staying free from. "Two are better than one... For if they fall, one will lift up his companion. But woe to him who is alone when he falls, For he has no one to help him up" (Ecclesiastes 4:9-10). Put it in the calendar like a meeting.

**5. Church.** Gathering with other believers on Sunday keeps you from drifting into a private faith. It is also where you meet the people who can become your one friend.

## Plan rest on purpose

Here is the anchor most people forget. If your week is all duty, the old habit will start to look like rest. That is often what it was. A reward at the end of a hard day.

So plan real rest. An evening with no work. A walk, a meal with people, a hobby with your hands, a game with your kids, a long nap on a Sunday afternoon. Rest is not a reward you earn by being good all week. It is something your heavenly Father built into the week from the very beginning.

## The ten-minute weekly check

Once a week, on Sunday evening or whenever suits you, sit down for ten minutes. Ask three questions.

- Which anchors held this week?
- Which ones slipped, and why?
- What is one line I will change for next week?

Then look at the warning signs from [the drift lesson](/tracks/staying-free/the-quiet-drift-before-a-fall). If two or more have shown up, act on them now, not next week.

End with thanks. "Father, thank You for this week. Thank You for every free day. Lead me in the next one."

## When the week falls apart

Some weeks will fall apart. A sick child, a work crisis, travel, a death in the family. On those weeks, do not try to keep all five anchors. Keep two. Sleep as well as you can, and tell your one friend what is going on. That is enough to hold the shape until life settles.

If the late evening is the hardest part of your week, the lesson [Building a New Default](/tracks/break-free/porn-addiction-recovery-for-men) has a plan for the hour you come home, and [What to Do With the Empty Evening](/tracks/quit-gaming-and-the-phone/what-to-do-with-the-empty-evening) helps you fill it.
