If you were badly hurt, you may have heard some version of this: "You just need to forgive and move on." Maybe it came from a pastor, a parent, or a well-meaning friend. Maybe it came while you were still bleeding.

For a lot of people, that sentence made things worse. It sounded like, "What happened to you does not matter. Stop making it a problem." Or worse, "Go back and act like nothing happened."

That is not what the Bible means by forgiveness. This lesson is about what forgiveness is, what it is not, and how to start, slowly, without pretending.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 1 we separated what happened from who you are. In lesson 2 we made a plan for triggers. In lesson 3 we learned to pour out the pain to God. Grieving comes first for a reason. You cannot forgive something you are not allowed to name.

## Do I have to forgive my abuser?

The Bible does call followers of Jesus to forgive. "Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God in Christ forgave you" (Ephesians 4:32). Jesus on the cross prayed, "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do" (Luke 23:34).

But look closely at what forgiveness is in Scripture. It is releasing a debt. It means you give up your right to make them pay, and you hand the case to God.

Paul puts it this way: "Do not avenge yourselves, but rather give place to wrath; for it is written, 'Vengeance is Mine, I will repay,' says the Lord" (Romans 12:19). That verse does not say the wrong does not matter. It says it matters so much that God Himself will deal with it. Forgiveness is trusting Him with justice instead of carrying it yourself.

So yes, forgiveness is part of following Jesus. But it is not what many people were told it was.

## Forgiveness is not saying it was fine

What happened to you was wrong. If it was not wrong, there would be nothing to forgive. Forgiveness starts by calling it what it was.

You can say, "What he did to me was evil. It damaged me. I am still paying for it. And I am choosing to hand the debt to God." All of that is true at the same time.

## Forgiveness is not the same as trust or reconciliation

Forgiveness happens between you and God. You can do it alone, even if the other person never says sorry, has moved away, or has died.

Reconciliation is different. It means rebuilding a relationship, and it takes two people. It needs real repentance and real change from the person who did harm, shown over time. You cannot make that happen on your own, and you are not required to pretend it has happened when it has not.

David is a good example. King Saul tried to kill him again and again. When David had the chance to kill Saul, he refused. He said, "Let the LORD judge between you and me ... But my hand shall not be against you" (1 Samuel 24:12). That is forgiveness. He did not take revenge.

But David did not move back into Saul's house. He did not go back to the palace. He kept his distance, because Saul was still dangerous. Forgiving Saul and staying away from Saul were both wise.

## Staying safe is not unforgiveness

If someone is still hurting you, or could, you are allowed to protect yourself and the people in your care. "A prudent man foresees evil and hides himself" (Proverbs 22:3). Jesus Himself walked away from crowds who wanted to throw Him off a cliff.

Leaving an unsafe home, keeping your children away from someone, refusing to be alone with a person, going to the police when a crime was committed: none of that is a lack of forgiveness. Forgiveness deals with your heart toward the debt. Safety deals with what happens next. Both can be true at once.

If you are in danger where you live right now, get emergency help and tell one trusted person today. Your safety comes first. If you are in a low or unsafe place in your own mind, do not carry it alone. Tell someone and get help right away.

## The distinctions table

This table is the heart of the lesson. Read it slowly.

| | What it is | What it is not | Does it need the other person? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Forgiving** | Handing the debt to God and giving up revenge | Saying it was fine, or forgetting it | No |
| **Excusing** | Saying it did not matter or was not really wrong | Something God asks of you | No, and you should not do it |
| **Trusting** | Believing someone is safe again, based on changed behavior over time | Automatic once you forgive | Yes, and it must be earned |
| **Reconciling** | Rebuilding the relationship | Required for forgiveness | Yes, with real repentance and change |
| **Staying safe** | Distance, limits, protection, reporting a crime | Unforgiveness or bitterness | No |
| **Grieving** | Naming the loss and feeling the pain | Weakness or lack of faith | No |

## How do you forgive an abuser?

Slowly, and usually more than once.

1. **Name the debt.** Write down what was taken from you. Your safety, your childhood, your trust, years of your life. You do not need to write details of the harm itself. Just the cost.
2. **Grieve it.** Bring it to God as a lament, like in lesson 3. Do not skip this.
3. **Hand it over.** Pray something simple: "Father, this is what they owe me. I am giving the debt to You. I will not take revenge. You judge this rightly."
4. **Choose it again.** When the memory comes back and the anger rises, you have not failed. Say again, "I gave that to You."
5. **Keep your limits.** Forgiving does not change who is safe to be around. Your boundaries stay.

Saying "I forgive them" while you still feel angry is not fake. It is a choice of the will, and feelings often follow much later. Some people find it takes years.

Nobody should rush you, including you. If someone pressures you to forgive quickly so things can go back to normal, that is a sign to slow down and talk to a counselor or a wise pastor who understands abuse.

## What about forgiving yourself?

Many people who were hurt also blame themselves. "I should have stopped it." "I should have told someone." If you were a child, or overpowered, or tricked, it was not your fault. There is nothing there to forgive yourself for. The shame belongs to the one who did it.

If there are things you yourself have done since then that you regret, that is a different conversation, and God's mercy covers it fully.
