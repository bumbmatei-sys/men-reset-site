You have named the pattern, decided what stops with you, and looked at the father you had. Now we get practical. Where does the old pattern show up in you, today, at home?

For most men it shows up in a hard moment. The kids are loud. Your wife says something that stings. Money is short. You are tired. And without thinking, you do what you saw growing up.

## The three old moves

Most men fall back on one of these:

- **Anger.** Raising your voice, a sharp word, slamming something, a look that makes everyone go quiet.
- **Silence.** Shutting down. Going cold for hours or days. Answering in one word.
- **Leaving.** Walking out, driving off, disappearing into the garage, the phone, the bar, or work.

Many men swore they would never yell like their father, and they do not. But they shut down like their mother did, or leave like their father did. The move changes shape. The pattern is the same: when it gets hard, someone ends up alone.

## Why am I angry at home?

Home is where you are tired, where you let your guard down, and where the people you love most can hurt you most. It is also where your old pattern was learned. So it makes sense that the old move comes out there first.

Anger is not always sin. Jesus was angry in the temple. Paul wrote, "Be angry, and do not sin: do not let the sun go down on your wrath" (Ephesians 4:26). The problem is not feeling it. The problem is letting it rule what you say and do. "The wrath of man does not produce the righteousness of God" (James 1:20).

Often anger sits on top of something else. Fear, shame, feeling disrespected, feeling like a failure. If you can name what is underneath, you are halfway to a better response.

## Is yelling at kids normal?

It is common. That does not make it harmless. Children read your voice and face long before they understand your words. Paul wrote to fathers in particular: "Fathers, do not provoke your children, lest they become discouraged" (Colossians 3:21). A child who is yelled at often learns to fear, to hide, or to yell back. You know this. You may have been that child.

The good news is that children also learn from repair. A father who says, "I was wrong to yell. I am sorry," teaches them something many men never saw. We will come back to that in lesson 6.

## How to control anger immediately at home

You will not think clearly in the heat of the moment. So decide now.

1. **Notice the signal.** Tight jaw, hot face, clenched hands, a fast heartbeat. That is your warning light.
2. **Pause.** Stop talking. Take a few slow breaths. A short silent prayer works: "Father, help me."
3. **Say your calm sentence.** Something like, "I am getting angry and I don't want to say something I regret. Give me ten minutes."
4. **Step away, and say when you are coming back.** This is not the old move of leaving. Leaving is disappearing. A pause has a return time.
5. **Come back.** Talk when you are calm. Listen first. "Let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath" (James 1:19).

"A soft answer turns away wrath, But a harsh word stirs up anger" (Proverbs 15:1). A calm voice is not weakness. It is strength under control.

## Silence and leaving need a plan too

If your old move is silence, your new move is a sentence. "I need a bit of time to think, but I want to talk about this tonight." Then talk tonight.

If your old move is leaving, your new move is staying in the room for one more minute, or leaving with a clear return time. "I'm going for a walk. I'll be back at seven and we can finish this." Then be back at seven.

## When to get help today

This lesson is for ordinary hard moments. Some things are not ordinary.

If your anger has ever turned into hitting, pushing, breaking things near someone, threatening, or making anyone at home afraid of you, get help today, not after the course. Talk to your pastor and a licensed counselor. The **National Domestic Violence Hotline** (**1-800-799-7233**, or text START to 88788) also talks with people who are worried about their own behavior. If anyone is in immediate danger, call **911**.

If you are the one who is not safe at home, the same hotline is for you too.

If you feel very low, hopeless, or unsafe with yourself after a hard moment, call or text **988** any time.

Getting help is not a failure of faith. It is what a man does to protect the people he loves.
