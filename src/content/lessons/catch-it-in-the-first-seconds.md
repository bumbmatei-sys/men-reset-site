Most of us notice a thought far too late. We only realize what we were thinking after we have already acted on it. Then we wonder how it happened so fast.

It did not happen fast. It happened in steps. We just were not watching the first ones.

## Where we are in this course

So far you have learned that a thought is not yet a sin, that renewing your mind is a daily process, and how to tell accusation from conviction. Now we work on timing. When do you deal with a thought? The answer is as early as you can.

## How a thought becomes an action

Most temptations follow a chain. Not every time, but often enough that it helps to know it.

1. **A thought.** "I am bored." "She looked at me." "I deserve a break."
2. **A picture.** Your mind fills in the details. A scene, a memory, a screen.
3. **A feeling.** Desire, tension, a pull in your chest or stomach.
4. **Your body.** Your hand reaches for the phone. You shift in your chair. You close the door.
5. **An action.** The thing you did not want to do.

Each step makes the next one easier. At step one, a thought is light. You can set it down. By step four, your body is already moving, and stopping feels like slamming on the brakes.

So the goal is simple. Move your catch point earlier. If you usually notice at step five, aim for step four. Then three. Over time you will catch more at step one.

## How do I control my thoughts as a Christian?

You do not control every thought that arrives. You control what you do in the first seconds after you notice it.

Solomon wrote, "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life" (Proverbs 4:23). Keeping your heart is not about never feeling a pull. It is about guarding the door early, when it is easy.

## David and Joseph

Look at two men in the Bible.

One evening King David walked on his roof and "saw a woman bathing" (2 Samuel 11:2). Seeing her was not yet the sin. But he kept looking. Then he asked who she was. Then he sent for her. Each step was a chance to stop. He did not take it, and the end was adultery and murder.

Joseph faced a stronger push. His master's wife tried to seduce him day after day. When she grabbed his garment, "he left his garment in her hand, and fled and ran outside" (Genesis 39:12). He did not stay to argue with himself. He used his feet.

Paul tells Timothy the same thing. "Flee also youthful lusts" (2 Timothy 2:22). Flee is a body word. Sometimes the most spiritual thing you can do is stand up and leave the room.

## Change what your body is doing

Your mind and body are connected. A thought gets stronger when your body is in the setting where you usually give in. So when a thought is pulling hard, change the setting.

- Stand up if you are sitting. Sit up if you are lying down.
- Leave the room. Go to a room with other people or with the lights on.
- Put the phone down, face down, or in another room.
- Go outside for two minutes. Cold air helps.
- Do something with your hands. Wash a dish, do ten pushups, write a line on paper.

You are not running from God. You are running toward a place where it is easier to hear Him. For more on setting up your space ahead of time, see [Dismantling Triggers](/tracks/break-free/dismantling-triggers).

## The "I already blew it" lie

This one deserves its own section, because it does so much damage.

You slip. You clicked, you looked, you said the harsh word, you ate the thing. And then a thought says, "Well, you already blew it. Tonight is ruined. You might as well keep going and start fresh tomorrow."

That is a lie. One slip is one slip. It does not have to become a whole night. The thought wants you to treat a small fall as a reason for a big one.

Catch this one in the first seconds too. The moment you notice you have slipped, stop right there. Say, "Father, I stop here. I am Yours." Then change what your body is doing, right away. A slip you stop after ten seconds is a very different evening from one you keep going with for two hours.

"For a righteous man may fall seven times And rise again" (Proverbs 24:16). Rising again starts in the next minute, not tomorrow morning.

## Your first seconds plan

Fill this in. Keep it short enough to remember under pressure.

| The thought I most need to catch | Where it usually finds me | My body move | My one sentence to my heavenly Father |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example: "Just one look." | In bed with my phone | Get up, phone on the kitchen counter | "Father, I am Yours. Help me." |
| Example: "I already blew it." | After a slip | Stand up, leave the room | "I stop here." |
| | | | |
| | | | |

Write your plan on a card and put it where the thought usually finds you. On the nightstand, by the computer, in your wallet.

## Do this today

Write your first seconds plan on a card: the thought you most need to catch, the one body move you will make, and the one sentence you will say to your heavenly Father. Put the card where that thought usually finds you. The next time it shows up, do the plan before you think about it.
