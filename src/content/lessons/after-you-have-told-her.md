You told her. Maybe it took years to get the words out. Maybe she found something first and you finally stopped denying it. Either way, the secret is out, and something in your house has changed.

A lot of men feel two things at once in these first days. Relief, because the hiding is over. And fear, because now she knows, and you have no idea what happens next.

This lesson is about what happens next. If you have not told her yet, go back and read [How to Tell Your Wife About Porn](/teachings/how-to-tell-your-wife-about-porn) first. This course starts on the other side of that conversation.

## Telling her was the beginning

Proverbs 28:13 says, "He who covers his sins will not prosper, But whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy."

Notice the two verbs. Confesses and forsakes. You have done the first one. The second one is the rest of your life. Telling her was the right thing, and it was brave. But it was not the repair. It was the door into the repair.

That matters because many men expect the confession to settle things. You said it. She cried. You hugged. Now you want to move on. But for her, the confession may be where the pain starts. She is not at the end of something. She is at the beginning.

## How do I rebuild trust with my wife?

You rebuild it the same way it was broken: through what you do when she cannot see you, repeated over time.

Trust is not a feeling you can ask her to have. It is a judgment she makes about you, based on evidence. Right now, the evidence says you hid something from her. The only way to change her judgment is to give her new evidence, day after day, for a long time.

That sounds slow. It is. But it also means you are not powerless. You cannot make her trust you. You can become a man who is trustworthy. That part is yours.

## What does she need in the first days?

Every wife is different, and the best way to know is to ask her gently and believe her answer. But here are some things many women need after this kind of news.

**Safety.** She needs to know you will not explode, blame her, or punish her for how she reacts. If she asks a hard question, you answer without anger.

**Honesty.** She needs to know the truth is finally on the table. If there is more she has not heard, it is better to tell her now, ideally with a counselor's help, than for her to find it later. Drip-feeding the truth is one of the fastest ways to destroy what little trust is left.

**Space, if she asks for it.** She may need a night alone, a walk, or a few days with fewer words. That is not rejection. Give it without sulking.

**Steadiness.** She needs to see that you are the same man on Tuesday that you were on Sunday. Not a flood of tears one day and cold silence the next.

## What not to do after you tell her

Some of these come from a good place. They still hurt.

**Do not rush her.** "How long is this going to take?" puts a clock on her pain. She did not choose this. Let it take what it takes.

**Do not demand forgiveness.** Forgiveness is her choice before God, not a debt she owes you. If she forgives quickly, thank God. If she cannot yet, do not push.

**Do not compare.** "At least I never touched anyone." "Most guys do worse." Comparisons tell her you are still defending yourself.

**Do not make her your counselor.** If you need to cry about your shame, cry to your heavenly Father and to a brother. Lesson 5 goes deep on this.

**Do not perform.** Flowers, a big speech, and a sudden burst of romance can feel to her like you are trying to buy your way out. Small and steady beats big and sudden.

## Offer before she asks

One of the most powerful things you can do early is to offer openness without being asked.

Hand her your phone. Tell her your passwords. Put a filter and accountability software on your devices, and let her know it is there. The article [Accountability Apps Compared](/teachings/accountability-apps-compared-2026) can help you choose. Tell her where you are going and when you will be back, then be there.

Some wives want to check. Some do not want to be the police and would rather a trusted man does it. Ask her what helps. Either way, the offer itself says something. It says, "I am not hiding anymore. You do not have to dig."

## Get your own help now

You cannot rebuild this alone, and she should not be your only help.

Tell one brother you trust. If you do not have one, read [How to Find a Christian Accountability Partner](/teachings/christian-accountability-partner). Talk to your pastor. And think seriously about a counselor, ideally one experienced with couples and betrayal. Many couples meet with someone together and the husband also meets with someone on his own. A counselor is wisdom, not weak faith.

If she is in danger in your home, or there is harm going on that is bigger than this, her safety comes first. In the US she can call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233, or 911 in an emergency. Lesson 3 says more about this. If you or she is in a dark place emotionally, call or text **988**, any time.

## Coming up next

Now that the first days are behind you, the question becomes what proves you have changed. Not your words. Your pattern. "Consistency Over Promises."
