Some nights go badly.

You could not sleep. Or you stayed up scrolling until 1 a.m. Or a kid was sick. Or you went back to the thing you promised yourself you were done with.

And then the alarm rings. And a voice says, "What is the point? You already ruined it. Skip the routine. Start again Monday."

That voice is lying. This lesson is about the morning after.

## Where we are in this course

Over five lessons we built a simple morning.

[A wake time you can keep](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/a-wake-time-you-can-keep). [The phone sleeps in another room](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/the-phone-sleeps-in-another-room). [One chore before you sit](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/one-chore-before-you-sit). [A few minutes with Scripture](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/a-few-minutes-with-scripture). [Out the door without a scroll](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/out-the-door-without-a-scroll).

That works on a normal morning. But the real test of a routine is the bad morning. If it only works when life is easy, it is not discipline yet.

## A bad night does not cancel the morning

Here is the most important line in this lesson. The morning is not ruined by the night. The morning is where you get back up.

"Do not rejoice over me, my enemy; When I fall, I will arise; When I sit in darkness, The LORD will be a light to me" (Micah 7:8).

Notice the words. When I fall. Not if. The prophet expected falls. But he did not stay down, and he did not sit in the dark alone. The LORD was his light right there.

"For a righteous man may fall seven times And rise again" (Proverbs 24:16). The righteous man is not the one who never has a bad night. He is the one who gets up the next morning.

## What should you do after a bad night's sleep?

If the night was bad because you slept badly, be kind to your body.

Get up at your normal time if you can. It is tempting to sleep in for two or three hours. But that usually makes it harder to fall asleep the next night, and the problem rolls on. The [National Sleep Foundation's sleep timing panel](https://www.sleephealthjournal.org/article/S2352-7218(23)00166-3/fulltext) recommends keeping sleep timing as regular as you can, and says short naps can help when you are short on sleep.

So get up, open the curtains, and drink a glass of water. Eat a real breakfast. If you can, take a short rest later in the day. And go to bed a little earlier tonight.

When Elijah was worn out and wanted to give up, God did not lecture him. "Then as he lay and slept under a broom tree, suddenly an angel touched him, and said to him, 'Arise and eat'" (1 Kings 19:5). Sleep and food came first. The next step of the journey came after.

## If the night went badly because you fell

This is the harder one. You scrolled into something you should not have seen. You watched porn. You played until 3 a.m. and lied about it.

The morning after, shame says, "Stay away from God until you have made up for it." That is the orphan voice from [You Are Not an Orphan](/tracks/know-who-you-are/you-are-not-an-orphan), in the Know Who You Are course.

Your heavenly Father says something else. "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9).

So come to Him the same hour. Not after a clean week. Now. Tell Him the truth, plainly. "Father, I fell last night. I am sorry. I am still Yours. Help me today." Then get up and do the short version of your morning.

"There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1). That is not permission to keep falling. It is the ground you stand on while you get up.

## The short version

On a bad morning, you do not need the full routine. You need the smallest version that keeps the habit alive.

| Full morning | Short version after a bad night |
| --- | --- |
| Up at your wake time | Up at your wake time, or as close as you can |
| Phone in another room | Phone stays in another room until you are dressed |
| One chore before you sit | Pull the blanket up or put three dishes away |
| A few minutes with Scripture | One verse, read out loud |
| Pray for your day | One honest line to your heavenly Father |
| Out the door without a scroll | Bag by the door, feeds closed until you arrive |

If you did the short version, the morning counts. Write that down. You kept it.

## New mercy for today

"Through the LORD's mercies we are not consumed, Because His compassions fail not. They are new every morning; Great is Your faithfulness" (Lamentations 3:22-23).

New every morning. Including this one. Including the one after your worst night.

Jesus said, "Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about its own things. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble" (Matthew 6:34). You get mercy for today, not for next week. So you do not need to fix your whole life this morning. Just this morning.

## Look at the night tonight, not now

In the morning, you just start. You do not sit and analyze what went wrong. That usually turns into an hour of shame.

But tonight, take five minutes. What made last night hard? The phone came back into the bedroom. You were alone and bored. You went to bed angry. Change one thing for tonight.

## The end of this course

Over six lessons we built one simple morning. A wake time you can keep. A phone that sleeps in another room. One chore before you sit. A few minutes with your heavenly Father in His word. Out the door without a scroll. And a short version for the mornings after a bad night.

None of these is big. That is the point. Small things, done most days, with Him.

You will miss days. Most of us do. When you notice, do not start over on Monday. Start over at the next morning, with the short version if you need it.
