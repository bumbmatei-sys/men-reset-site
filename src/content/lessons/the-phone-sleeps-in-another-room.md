Here is how most mornings go wrong. The alarm rings on the phone. You reach over to turn it off. And while it is in your hand, you check one thing.

Twenty minutes later you are still in bed. You have read the news, seen a few videos and answered a message. Your mind is already full of other people's day, and you have not even stood up.

That did not start in the morning. It started the night before, when the phone went to bed with you.

## Where we are in this course

[In the first lesson](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/a-wake-time-you-can-keep) you picked a wake time you can hit four days this week. You put the alarm across the room, and you made feet on the floor the win.

This lesson protects that wake time. Because the easiest way to lose your morning is to let the phone sleep next to you.

## Willpower is not the plan

When are you weakest? Late at night, when you are tired. And early in the morning, when you are half awake.

Those are the two times most of us plan to "just be strong" with the phone. It rarely works. Not because you are a bad person, but because tired people make tired choices.

Jesus told His friends, "Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation. The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak" (Matthew 26:41). He did not shame them. He was honest about how people work.

So we do not plan to be strong at 6 a.m. We set things up at 6 p.m., while we are strong, so the morning does not need willpower.

Paul put it this way: "But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to fulfill its lusts" (Romans 13:14). Make no provision. Do not leave the door open and hope you will not walk through it.

## Should you sleep with your phone in another room?

Yes, if you can. It is one of the simplest changes in this whole course, and one of the strongest.

When the phone is across the house, three things change. You cannot scroll in bed at night. You cannot grab it the moment you wake. And if you wake at 3 a.m., there is nothing glowing next to you asking for attention.

A lot of people worry about emergencies. That is fair. If you need to be reachable, turn the ringer on for calls only from your favorite contacts and leave the phone in the hallway. You will hear it. What you will not do is lie there scrolling.

## Give the phone a home

Phones end up in the bedroom because the charger is there. So move the charger.

Pick one spot outside your bedroom. The kitchen counter, a shelf in the hallway, a desk in the living room. That is where the phone sleeps from now on.

Then pick a time it goes to bed. Maybe 9:30 or 10:00. When that time comes, you plug it in and walk away. Same place, same time, every night. After a week or two, you do not have to decide anymore. It is just what you do.

## What goes in its place

If you only take something away, you leave a hole. The phone did a few jobs in your bedroom. So give each job to something else.

| What the phone did | What does it now |
| --- | --- |
| Alarm clock | A simple alarm clock across the room |
| Something to read before sleep | A paper book or your Bible |
| The last thought of the day | A short prayer to your heavenly Father |
| The first thing you touch in the morning | Your feet on the floor, then the curtains |
| Something to do when you cannot sleep | Lie still, breathe slowly, and pray for one person by name |

A basic alarm clock costs very little. It is one of the best small purchases you can make for your mornings.

## What if I live alone, or share a room?

If you live alone, this is fully up to you. That makes it easier and harder. Easier because nobody else is involved. Harder because nobody notices. So tell one friend about the rule. Just saying it out loud helps.

If you share a bedroom, talk about it together. You may find you talk more at night once both phones are out of the room. If your spouse is not ready, you can still move yours. Lead by doing, not by lecturing.

## This is about your first and last thought

I am not against phones. I use mine every day for good things.

But the first and last thought of the day matter. At night, you hand your mind to something as you fall asleep. In the morning, the first thing you look at often sets the tone for the next hour.

David wrote, "I will set nothing wicked before my eyes" (Psalm 101:3). Not everything on your phone is wicked. But much of it is noise. And you want the first voice you hear in the morning to be your heavenly Father's, not a feed.
