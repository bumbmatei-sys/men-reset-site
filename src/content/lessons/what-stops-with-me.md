In the last lesson you looked at the pattern you grew up in. Some of it you want to keep. Some of it you do not want to pass on.

Now comes the decision. Not a speech at the next family dinner. Not a promise you shout when you are angry. A quiet decision before God about what stops with you.

## The son who sees and does not do likewise

Ezekiel 18 has a short story in it that fits every man in this course. A father lives wrongly. Then the father "begets a son Who sees all the sins which his father has done, And considers but does not do likewise" (Ezekiel 18:14). God's verdict over that son: "He shall not die for the iniquity of his father; He shall surely live!" (Ezekiel 18:17).

Look at the three verbs. He **sees**. He **considers**. He **does not do likewise**. That is the whole course in one verse. You saw it in lesson 1. Now you consider it, and decide.

## What does breaking generational curses mean?

People mean different things by it. Here is a plain way to say it. Breaking a generational curse means the harmful pattern that ran through your family stops running through you. Not because you said the right prayer once, but because you turned away from it and God gave you something better to walk in.

As we saw last lesson, if you belong to Jesus you are not living under a curse. "Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us" (Galatians 3:13). The work is not to fear an invisible force. The work is to stop practicing what you learned.

## How to break generational curses, step by step

A simple biblical way that many pastors teach:

**1. Confess your own part.** Not your father's sins. Yours. Where have you already repeated it? Nehemiah prayed, "Both my father's house and I have sinned" (Nehemiah 1:6). He did not blame his family and excuse himself. He owned what was his. "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9).

**2. Forgive what was done to you.** Forgiveness is not saying it was fine. It is not rushing yourself to feel better. It is not staying near someone who is still dangerous. It is handing the debt to God so it stops ruling you. We will come back to this in lesson 3.

**3. Turn away from the old way.** Say it plainly: "I am done with this. It does not belong in my home." Then remove what feeds it. If the pattern was drinking, do not keep it in the house. If it was rage, find out what sets you off.

**4. Ask for a new heart.** You cannot manufacture patience you never saw. God promised, "I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you" (Ezekiel 36:26). Ask for it every day.

**5. Practice the opposite.** This is where it gets real. Every time the old move comes up, you do the new one, even badly. More on that in lessons 4 and 5.

## Why is breaking generational curses so hard?

Because the old pattern is automatic, and the new one is not yet. You practiced the old way for years, maybe without knowing it. Under stress, tired, or hungry, the body goes back to what it knows.

That is why willpower alone fails. You need three things together: the Holy Spirit changing you from the inside, a plan made before the hard moment, and other men who know what you are working on. A brother can see what you cannot. If you do not have one yet, read [How to Find a Christian Accountability Partner](/teachings/christian-accountability-partner).

## Be specific

"I won't be like my dad" is not a plan. It is a feeling.

A plan sounds like this:

- "My father drank every night. I will not keep alcohol in the house, and I will be home and sober at dinner."
- "Nobody in my house apologized. I will say sorry to my kids when I am wrong, the same day."
- "My dad was never at anything. I will be at the game, the recital, the school meeting."
- "My parents fought with silence. I will talk it out, even when it is awkward."

Notice each one has two halves. What stops, and what you give instead. You cannot just remove the old thing. Something good has to take its place.

## What if I don't have kids yet?

Do this anyway. The pattern lives in you, not in your children. How you treat a girlfriend, a wife, a roommate, a coworker, and yourself is where it shows up first. If you settle it now, you bring a different man into the home you build later.

## A decision you will keep making

You will make this decision once, and then make it again tomorrow, and the day after. That is not failure. That is how a new pattern is built. Your heavenly Father is patient with that process. "He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ" (Philippians 1:6).

If you notice the old pattern is not just a habit but something that harms or frightens people in your home, do not wait for this course to fix it. Talk to your pastor and a licensed counselor this week.
