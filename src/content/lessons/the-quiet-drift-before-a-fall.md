A lot of people describe a fall the same way. "It came out of nowhere. One minute I was fine, the next minute I was back in it."

It almost never comes out of nowhere. It feels sudden because the last step was sudden. But the road to that night usually started weeks earlier, with small things nobody noticed. Not even you.

That is good news. If the fall starts early, you can turn back early.

## A fall starts before the night it happens

The writer of Hebrews uses a boat word. "Therefore we must give the more earnest heed to the things we have heard, lest we drift away" (Hebrews 2:1). A boat that drifts does not crash on purpose. Nobody decides to drift. The rope just slips a little, then a little more, and one day you look up and the shore is far away.

Doctors who work in addiction recovery say much the same thing. Dr. Steven Melemis, in a paper on relapse prevention in the Yale Journal of Biology and Medicine, writes that relapse is a gradual process that can begin weeks or months before someone actually uses again. The habit you left might be different from alcohol, but the drift looks very similar.

## What are the three stages of relapse?

Melemis describes three stages. They are a helpful map.

1. **Emotional.** You are not thinking about the habit at all. You remember the last fall and do not want to go back. But you are tired, bottled up, pulling away from people, and not taking care of yourself.
2. **Mental.** Now part of you wants it and part of you does not. You think about it more. You start bargaining, minimizing, or looking for a time when nobody would know.
3. **Physical.** You do it.

Most people only fight at stage three. By then it is the hardest fight. The earlier you notice, the easier it is to turn.

## What are the early warning signs of relapse?

The earliest signs do not look like temptation. They look like an ordinary week going a little wrong.

- You are sleeping badly or staying up late with no reason.
- You have stopped telling anyone how it is really going.
- You skip the short time with Scripture, then skip it again, and it stops bothering you.
- You say no to people and yes to being alone with a screen.
- You are irritable with your family or coworkers and do not know why.
- You keep busy with everyone else's problems so you do not have to look at your own.
- You tell small lies. "I'm good." "Nothing much." "Just tired."

Later signs are closer to the habit itself:

- You start remembering the old habit as better than it was.
- You loosen one boundary. The filter goes off "for work." The phone comes back into the bedroom.
- You notice when the house will be empty.
- You think, "Just this once," or, "I have been good for months, I can handle it."

## The first temptation is deciding alone

In ministry school I heard a teaching that stayed with me. There are two levels of temptation. The second level is the one we all know: choosing between right and wrong in the moment. The first level is easier to miss. It is the decision to run your own life without asking the Father at all.

That is where most drift starts. You stop praying about your evenings. You stop asking, "Father, what do You want for this weekend?" You just plan it yourself. Nothing sinful has happened yet. But you are already steering the boat alone.

So part of staying free is simple. Keep asking Him before, not only after. "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life" (Proverbs 4:23).

## "Just this once" is a warning sign, not a conclusion

One of the loudest warning signs is a thought that sounds reasonable. "I have been free for three months. I am strong now. One look will not undo anything."

Paul wrote, "Therefore let him who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall" (1 Corinthians 10:12). That is not meant to scare you. It is meant to keep you humble and awake. Feeling strong is a gift. Feeling so strong that you stop guarding the door is a sign of drift.

When you catch yourself bargaining, do not argue with it for an hour. Name it. "That is the drift talking." Then do something from the checklist below. For how to answer a specific lie, the lesson [Answer With What Is Written](/tracks/renew-your-mind/answer-with-what-is-written) goes deeper.

## Your warning-signs checklist

Go through this honestly. Mark every sign you have seen in the last two weeks.

| Sign | What it looks like for me | One small fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poor sleep | Late nights, waking tired | A bedtime I tell someone about |
| Pulling away | Skipping church, calls, invitations | Say yes to one thing this week |
| Bottling up | Not telling anyone how I feel | One honest sentence to one person |
| Skipped habits | No Scripture, no prayer, no walk | Two minutes, not twenty |
| Irritability | Snapping at people | Name what is really bothering me |
| Small lies | "I'm fine" when I am not | Tell the truth once today |
| Loosened boundary | Filter off, phone in the bedroom | Put it back tonight |
| Glamorizing | Remembering the habit as good | Write down what it really cost |
| Bargaining | "Just this once," "I can handle it" | Say it out loud to someone |
| Planning | Noticing when I will be alone | Change the plan for that time now |

## What to do when you see two or more

One sign on its own may just be a hard week. Two or more in the same week is your cue to act, today.

- **Tell someone.** Not a full confession, just the truth. "I have been isolating and sleeping badly. Can you ask me about it on Friday?" "But exhort one another daily, while it is called 'Today,' lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin" (Hebrews 3:13).
- **Fix one boundary.** Whatever loosened, put it back tonight while you are calm.
- **Get your body back.** Sleep, real meals, a walk. Melemis calls poor self-care the common thread in early relapse.
- **Go back to the Father.** Not with a long apology. Just, "Father, I have been steering alone. I want You to lead again."

If loneliness is the biggest sign for you, read [Loneliness and Temptation](/teachings/loneliness-and-temptation). If you notice it is always the same trigger, [Dismantling Triggers](/tracks/break-free/dismantling-triggers) from the Break Free course will help.
