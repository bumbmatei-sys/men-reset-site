You might be the kind of husband who feels terrible about what you did. Good. That shows you understand it hurt her.

But there is a trap here that many sincere men fall into. Your shame gets so loud that it fills the room. She came to talk about her pain, and twenty minutes later she is comforting you. That is not repair. That is a second weight on her shoulders.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 4, [Patience Without Disappearing](/tracks/rebuilding-trust-after-porn/patience-without-disappearing), we saw how shame can make a man hide. This lesson looks at the other side. Sometimes shame does not make us hide. It makes us hand our pain to her.

## Remorse looks at her. Shame looks at you.

There is a kind of sorrow she needs from you. It sounds like, "I hurt you. I see what this has done to you. I am so sorry." That is remorse. It keeps its eyes on her.

There is another kind of sorrow that slowly turns toward yourself. "I am disgusting. I ruined everything. I do not deserve you. How can you even look at me?" It feels like repentance. But notice who it is about. It is about you. And it often leaves her feeling she has to reassure you that you are not that bad.

Paul named this difference. "Godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, not to be regretted; but the sorrow of the world produces death" (2 Corinthians 7:10). Godly sorrow moves you to change and to care for the person you hurt. Worldly sorrow keeps circling back to your own pain.

If you have not read it yet, [How to Forgive Yourself After Sexual Sin](/teachings/forgive-yourself-after-sexual-sin) walks through the verdict God has already given. This lesson is about where to carry the weight while you are learning to believe it.

## How do I stop feeling ashamed of my past?

Not by pretending it did not happen, and not by asking her to tell you it is fine. You bring it to the One who can actually deal with it.

David wrote, "I acknowledged my sin to You, And my iniquity I have not hidden. I said, 'I will confess my transgressions to the Lord,' And You forgave the iniquity of my sin" (Psalm 32:5).

He went to God first. Not to a crowd. Not to someone who would make him feel better. To the One who could forgive. And God forgave.

Your heavenly Father does not need your wife's approval to forgive you. He does not wait until she is healed. When you confess, He forgives you fully, "as far as the east is from the west" (Psalm 103:12). Her trust may take years. His forgiveness does not.

That matters because once you know you are forgiven by God, you stop needing her to forgive you right now so that you can feel okay. That frees her. She can take her time, because your peace does not depend on her.

If the old voice keeps telling you that you are condemned, the Renew Your Mind lesson [Accusation or Conviction](/tracks/renew-your-mind/accusation-or-conviction) will help you tell the two voices apart.

## Carry it in the right place

Galatians 6 says two things that seem to conflict. "Bear one another's burdens" (Galatians 6:2). And a few lines later, "each one shall bear his own load" (Galatians 6:5).

Both are true. There are things you must carry yourself, like your choices and your work to change. And there are heavy things you are not meant to carry alone. The question is who helps you carry them. Right now, it should not mainly be her. She is already carrying her own wound.

James wrote, "Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed" (James 5:16). That is why you need a brother. A man who knows the whole story, who will hear your shame at 11 p.m. and pray with you, so she does not have to. If you do not have that man yet, read [How to Find a Christian Accountability Partner](/teachings/christian-accountability-partner) this week.

And a counselor can help you look at the parts that are too tangled for a friend. That is wisdom, not weak faith.

## Do not only mop the floor

Confession is not the end of the work. If water keeps pouring out of a faucet, mopping the floor every night does not fix it. You have to find the faucet.

John wrote, "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). Forgiveness and cleansing. The first clears the debt. The second deals with what is underneath.

With your brother or a counselor, look at the roots. When does the pull come? What were you running from? Loneliness, stress, anger, an old wound? The Break Free lesson [Why Do I Keep Going Back?](/tracks/break-free/why-you-keep-going-back-to-porn) helps with this. Dealing with the root is one of the kindest things you can do for your wife, because it makes the change real.
