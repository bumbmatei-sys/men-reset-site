A man can provide for his wife, stay faithful to her, and still leave her feeling alone. Loving a wife well is more than not doing harm. It is a daily choice to give.

## Where we are in this course

You have seen that leading means serving, that it starts with your own walk, and that it needs your presence. Now we come to the first person you are called to love in your home.

## How do you love your wife as Christ loved the church?

"Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her" (Ephesians 5:25).

That verse sets the bar, and it is high on purpose. Christ did not love the church because she was perfect. He did not love her only when she responded well. He came toward her, served her, and gave His life for her good.

So loving your wife like Christ is not a feeling you wait for. It is a direction you choose. Toward her. For her good. At your cost.

## Serve her

Jesus took a towel and washed feet. Your version is less dramatic, and it still counts. Do the job she usually does. Take the kids so she can rest. Handle the appointment she has been dreading. Notice what wears her down and quietly carry some of it.

Do it without announcing it, and without keeping a tally. "Let each of you look out not only for his own interests, but also for the interests of others" (Philippians 2:4).

## Listen to her

Most men listen to fix. She tells you about a hard day, and you give her three solutions before she finishes. Sometimes she wants a solution. Often she wants to be known.

Peter tells husbands to "dwell with them with understanding" (1 Peter 3:7). Understanding takes time and questions. "What was that like for you?" "What would help?" "Tell me more." Then let there be silence while she thinks.

## Pray for her

Pray for her by name every day. Pray for her body, her work, her friendships, her walk with God, and whatever is weighing on her. Ask her what she would like prayer for, and then actually pray it.

If she wants to pray together, start short. A one-minute prayer at night is enough. If she does not want to pray together right now, do not push. Keep praying for her on your own. That is still leading.

## Protect her dignity

Talk about her with honor, at home and in public. Do not make her the joke. Do not complain about her to your friends. Do not correct her in front of the kids. "Let no corrupt word proceed out of your mouth, but what is good for necessary edification, that it may impart grace to the hearers" (Ephesians 4:29).

Respect her mind. Ask her opinion and let it change yours. She is a fellow heir "of the grace of life" (1 Peter 3:7), not a project to manage.

## Can I use the Bible to tell my wife what to do?

No. This matters. Some men read Ephesians 5 and skip to the verses about wives. They use Scripture as a weapon to win arguments or demand compliance. That is not leading. It is control dressed up in religious words.

Your job is to obey the verses written to you. "Husbands, love your wives and do not be bitter toward them" (Colossians 3:19). Live those, and let God speak to her about the rest.

## What if things are hard between us right now?

Some marriages are tired. Some have been hurt by broken trust, harsh words, or years of distance. If that is your home, know this: closeness, trust, and forgiveness are hers to give, at her own pace. They are not something you are owed because you are trying now.

Do not pressure her to be fine. Do not keep score of your good deeds. Do not quote verses about forgiveness at her. Keep serving, keep listening, keep praying, and let her see change over time.

Seeing a Christian marriage counselor together, or on your own, is wisdom, not weak faith.

## What if I have hurt or frightened her?

Yes. If you have hurt, threatened, or frightened your wife, love means taking responsibility, not asking her to forget it. Get help today. Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at **1-800-799-7233**, and call **911** if anyone is in danger. Do not ask her or anyone to keep it quiet. If she needs space or safety, respect it. If you feel very low or unsafe yourself, call or text **988**.
