John wrote one of the four Gospels, and he never once uses his own name in it. Instead, five times he calls himself "the disciple whom Jesus loved."

Some people read that and think it sounds proud. Like John was saying he was the favorite. But that is not what was going on, and the real meaning is good news for you.

## Who was the disciple Jesus loved?

Most Bible teachers, going back to the early church, agree it was John, the son of Zebedee. He was one of the twelve, a fisherman, and the brother of James. He was there at the Last Supper, at the cross, at the empty tomb, and on the shore when the risen Jesus cooked breakfast.

At the end of his Gospel he tells us plainly that the disciple Jesus loved is the one who wrote these things down (John 21:24).

## Not loved more, just believed it more

Here is the key. Jesus did not love John more than Peter, or Thomas, or you. Jesus loved all twelve. John 13:1 says He loved His own to the end.

The difference was that John believed it. He let it become his name. When he thought about who he was, the first thing that came to mind was not "fisherman" or "apostle" or "writer." It was "I am the one Jesus loves."

That is not pride. Pride says, "Look at what I do." John was saying, "Look at what He does." His identity was not built on his performance. It was built on being loved.

Every one of the disciples could have written the same line. Peter could have. You could. The love was there for all of them. John just took it personally.

## Close enough to ask

Look at the scene in John 13. Jesus tells the twelve that one of them will betray Him. The room goes tense. Everyone wants to know who.

Peter, who was usually the first to speak, does not ask Jesus directly. He motions to John. Why? Because John was leaning on Jesus' chest. He was close enough to just ask.

So John leaned back and said, "Lord, who is it?" And Jesus told him (John 13:25-26).

The others were wondering. John got the answer. Not because he was smarter or more spiritual. Because he was close. And he was close because he believed he was welcome there.

That is how it works. People who think God is disappointed in them keep their distance. They pray politely from across the room. People who believe they are loved lean in, and they hear things the others only guess at.

## Love changed him

It is easy to picture John as gentle all along. He wasn't. Jesus nicknamed John and his brother the Sons of Thunder (Mark 3:17). Once, when a village refused to welcome Jesus, they asked if they should call fire down from heaven on it (Luke 9:54). Another time they tried to grab the best seats in His kingdom (Mark 10:37).

That is a hot temper and a lot of ambition. Not exactly the gentle face from the paintings.

But years of leaning on Jesus did something. The same man later wrote, "Beloved, let us love one another, for love is of God" (1 John 4:7). The Son of Thunder became the apostle of love.

He did not get there by trying harder to be nice. He got there by being loved, and believing it, for a long time. Received love is what changes people.

## Faithful when others ran

When Jesus was arrested, the disciples scattered. At the cross, John is the one disciple the Gospels place right there, standing near Jesus' mother. And Jesus, while dying, trusted her to him (John 19:26-27).

Love made John brave. Fear runs. Love stays. When you know you are loved, you do not have to protect yourself the same way, so you can stand where others won't.

## Your place at His side

So what does "the disciple whom Jesus loved" mean for you? It means that seat was never reserved for one man. It is an invitation.

You can take the same name. Not because you earned it, but because it is true. And when you start believing it, you will start coming closer, asking honest questions, and hearing His voice more clearly.

If you are not sure who you are apart from what you do, read [Identity in Christ](/teachings/identity-in-christ-2026). And if something in you says you are the exception, the one He could not love, [Shame vs Guilt](/teachings/shame-vs-guilt-jesus-2026) will help you see where that voice comes from.

## Do this today

Each morning this week, before you check your phone, say out loud, "I am the one Jesus loves." It may feel awkward. Say it anyway.

Then bring Him one real question you have been carrying, the kind you usually only wonder about. Lean in, ask it, and wait a few minutes in quiet before you move on with your day.

## Reflect

If you truly believed you were the one Jesus loves, what would you stop doing, and what would you start bringing to Him?
