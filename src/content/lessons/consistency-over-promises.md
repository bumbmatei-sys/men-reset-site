When trust is broken, most men reach for words. "I promise it will never happen again." "I am a different man now." "You have to believe me."

The words are often sincere. The trouble is that she has heard sincere words before. Maybe from you, maybe the last time you said you were done. Words were part of how she got hurt. So words, right now, are worth very little.

That is not a reason to despair. It is a reason to change tools.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 1, [After You Have Told Her](/tracks/rebuilding-trust-after-porn/after-you-have-told-her), we looked at the first days: be safe, be honest, offer openness, and get your own help. Now we look at what actually rebuilds trust over the weeks that follow.

## What actions rebuild trust?

Here is the short answer. Small actions, done when no one is checking, repeated until they are a pattern.

Jesus said, "He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much" (Luke 16:10). That verse is about money and stewardship, but the principle runs through all of life. Faithfulness in small things is how anyone learns whether you can be trusted with big ones.

Think about how trust works with anyone. You do not trust a coworker because he gave a great speech about reliability. You trust him because, for two years, when he said he would send the report on Friday, it came on Friday. That is all trust is. A long record of words and actions that match.

Your wife is now watching for that record. Not because she is suspicious by nature, but because she has to be, for a while, to stay sane. Give her a record worth reading.

## Why big promises can backfire

A big promise feels strong when you make it. But it puts a heavy weight on one sentence. If you slip even once, the whole promise falls, and she is back at the beginning.

Smaller commitments are more honest. "I will tell you where I am when I leave the house." "I will hand you my phone any time you ask." "I will tell my brother the truth on our call every Thursday." These are things you can actually do every day. And when you do them, every day, they add up to something no speech can match.

John wrote, "My little children, let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth" (1 John 3:18). Love that stays in your mouth does not reach her. Love that shows up in your actions does.

## Tell her before she has to ask

There is a big difference between truth she has to dig for and truth you volunteer.

If she finds something on your phone and then you explain, she learns you only tell the truth when caught. If you tell her first, "I was tempted tonight on the way home, I called my brother and came straight here," she learns something new: you are willing to be known, even when it costs you.

Christian counselors, such as those writing for Focus on the Family, keep coming back to this kind of openness. Not perfection. Openness. Talk with her, ideally with a counselor, about what she wants to hear about and when. Some wives want to know about every temptation. Some would rather a brother hears the daily details and she hears about any fall. Let her choose what is helpful for her.

## Your character is seen at home

Here is something worth saying plainly. Character is not mostly seen on Sunday morning, at work, or online. It is seen at home. In how you speak when you are tired. In whether you do the dishes without being asked. In whether you look at your phone or at her when she is talking.

Rebuilding trust is not only about porn. It is about the whole man. If you are faithful with your screens but careless with your words, she will still feel unsafe. If you are honest about temptation but never show up for the children's bedtime, she will wonder what else you are absent from.

Ask yourself where your words and your actions do not match yet. Start there.

## Is it working yet?

You will want to know how long this takes. Lesson 4 looks at that question honestly. For now, here is what matters most: you cannot control the length. You can control the direction. Every faithful day points the same way. And every hidden day, even a small one, points back. Lesson 4 talks about the patience this takes.

## Your weekly consistency table

Fill this in for the coming week. Keep every line small enough to do every single day.

| Area | What I will do this week | Did I do it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screens | My phone stays open to her, my filter stays on | |
| Where I am | I tell her when I leave and when plans change | |
| Honesty | I tell her or my brother about temptation before anyone asks | |
| Home | One task she usually carries, every day | |
| Words | No promise I cannot keep today | |
| My heavenly Father | A few honest minutes with Him each morning | |
| My brother | One honest check-in this week | |

At the end of the week, look only at the third column. Not at what you meant to do. At what you did. Then change one line for next week.

If you need help with the morning time with God, the course [Discipline and a Morning Routine](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine) walks through it step by step.

## When you fall short

You will not do this perfectly. When you miss a line, tell the truth about it. "I said I would call when I was running late, and I did not. I am sorry." That kind of honesty about a small failure builds more trust than pretending you are fine.

If you fall back into porn, do not hide it. Tell her as you agreed, tell your brother the same day, and read [What to Do After a Porn Relapse](/teachings/what-to-do-after-porn-relapse). A hidden fall does far more damage than a confessed one.
