You have seen that grace is a gift, faith is trust, and the Kingdom is the reign of the King. Now the practical question. If I am saved by grace and not by works, does it matter what I do?

It matters a lot. Just not for the reason many men think.

## What does "faith without works is dead" mean?

James wrote, "What does it profit, my brethren, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? Can faith save him? If a brother or sister is naked and destitute of daily food, and one of you says to them, 'Depart in peace, be warmed and filled,' but you do not give them the things which are needed for the body, what does it profit?" (James 2:14-16).

Then he says it plainly: "Thus also faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead" (James 2:17). And again, "For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also" (James 2:26).

James is not saying you need faith plus a pile of good deeds to get in. He is saying a faith that never does anything is not real faith. It is words. If you truly trust that God is good, that trust will move your hands. A man who says he believes the house is on fire, but keeps sitting on the couch, does not believe it.

## Do Paul and James disagree?

It can look that way. Paul says we are saved "not of works" (Ephesians 2:9). James says faith without works is dead. But read the very next verse in Ephesians: "For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them" (Ephesians 2:10).

Not saved by works. Saved for works.

Paul is fighting people who think they can earn their standing with God by keeping rules. James is fighting people who think they can say the right words and change nothing. They are attacking two different errors from two sides. Both of them would say the same thing: you are saved by grace through faith, and real faith goes to work.

Paul put both together in one line: "faith working through love" (Galatians 5:6).

## Is faith a work?

No. Faith is not something you do to earn salvation. It is receiving. A man who accepts a gift has not earned it by holding out his hand. In lesson 1 we saw that grace is like being carried out of deep water. Faith is not swimming. It is letting yourself be carried.

## Fruit, not wages

Jesus gave the picture. "I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit; for without Me you can do nothing" (John 15:5).

A branch does not strain to produce grapes. It stays connected, and fruit grows. Paul lists that fruit: "love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control" (Galatians 5:22-23).

Here is the test. It is not how good the work looks. Even good activities can be done in the flesh, to be seen, to prove something, to pay off guilt. The question is where it comes from. Is this the fruit of staying close to Jesus, or is it you grinding to stay on God's good side?

Paul says you work it out "for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure" (Philippians 2:13). He gives the desire and the strength. You show up and do it.

## Hired hand or son?

In the story of the lost son, the younger son plans to come home as a hired servant. The father will not hear of it. Then the older brother complains that he has worked for years and never been rewarded. The father answers, "Son, you are always with me, and all that I have is yours" (Luke 15:31).

Both sons thought in wages. The father thought in sonship. "You are no longer a slave but a son, and if a son, then an heir of God through Christ" (Galatians 4:7).

If you went through [Know Who You Are](/tracks/know-who-you-are), you saw how much of the Christian life is living from that identity. [I Am, I Have, I Do](/tracks/know-who-you-are/i-am-i-have-i-do) puts who you are before what you do. Here we take one step further. Once you know you are a son, what does a son actually do this week?

## There is no secular job

"And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men, knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance; for you serve the Lord Christ" (Colossians 3:23-24).

In the Kingdom, there is no line between holy work and ordinary work. Jesus called fishermen and a tax collector. Paul made tents. If you belong to the King, your job, your shift, your yard work, and the way you speak to your kids are all done for Him. A son working in his father's house is never doing unimportant work.
