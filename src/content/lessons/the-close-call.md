You were alone. It was late. The urge was strong, and the old way felt very close. You picked up the phone, or you opened the laptop, and you were one click away.

And then you didn't. Maybe you closed it. Maybe you walked outside. Maybe you texted someone. Your heart was still pounding, but you did not fall.

That was a close call. This lesson is about what to do in that hour, and what to do the next morning.

## A close call is not a failure

Many people come out of a close call feeling dirty. "I was so close. Something must be wrong with me." So they hide it, and the shame sets up the next fall.

Look at it the other way. "God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make the way of escape, that you may be able to bear it" (1 Corinthians 10:13). A close call is that verse coming true. There was a way of escape, and you took it. That is not failure. That is what faithfulness looks like on a hard night.

Being tempted is not a sin. If that still trips you up, read [A Thought Is Not Yet a Sin](/tracks/renew-your-mind/a-thought-is-not-yet-a-sin).

## How do you stop a relapse before it happens?

The Break Free course already has a plan for [the first ten minutes of an urge](/tracks/break-free/how-to-deal-with-porn-urges). A close call is often what happens when the urge has been building for hours and you are much nearer the edge. You need something even simpler.

### 1. Move first, think second

Do not sit there and debate. Your body is already on its way. Change rooms. Go outside. Stand in the kitchen with the light on. Joseph did not stay to explain himself to Potiphar's wife. "He left his garment in her hand, and fled and ran outside" (Genesis 39:12). He moved first.

"Flee also youthful lusts; but pursue righteousness, faith, love, peace with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart" (2 Timothy 2:22). Flee is a body word.

### 2. Tell someone while it is happening

This is the step most people skip. They wait until it passes, and then they tell no one because "nothing happened."

Send the message during the close call, not after. It can be very short. "Rough night. Close to falling. Praying. Can you pray too?" You do not need an answer. Sending it breaks the secret, and the habit lives on secrets. Pick the person now, in daylight, so you do not have to think about it at 1 a.m.

### 3. Answer the lie with one plain truth

A close call usually has a sentence behind it. "You deserve this tonight." "Nobody will know." "You already blew it by looking."

Answer it once, out loud if you can, and stop arguing. "I am a child of God, and I do not need this." Then go back to what you are doing in the other room. "Therefore submit to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you" (James 4:7).

### 4. Let it pass

Urges rise and then they fall. They feel like they will last all night, but in practice they usually ease if you stay out of the room and keep your hands busy. Make tea. Wash the dishes. Take a shower. Go to bed in a different spot if you need to. You do not have to win forever tonight. You only have to get through this hour.

## The morning after a close call

Most people want to forget a close call as fast as possible. Do the opposite, briefly.

First, thank your heavenly Father. Really thank Him. He gave you the way out and helped you take it.

Then take five minutes and write down what led there. Use the warning signs from [the last lesson](/tracks/staying-free/the-quiet-drift-before-a-fall). Were you tired? Alone all weekend? Did you loosen a boundary? Had you been bargaining for days?

A close call shows you exactly where your guard is thin, without the damage of a fall. That makes it one of the most useful nights you will have. Fix the one gap it showed you, and tell your person how it ended. "I made it. Thanks for praying. I think the problem was the weekend alone."

## What if close calls keep happening?

One close call is a win. Close calls every few days are a sign that something bigger needs to change. Maybe a device needs stronger limits, maybe your evenings need a plan, maybe you need to talk to someone more often.

Do not wait for a fall to take that seriously. Tell a pastor or a mature Christian friend. If the pull is constant and you cannot get ahead of it, a Christian counselor can help you find what is underneath. Asking for help is wisdom, not weakness.
