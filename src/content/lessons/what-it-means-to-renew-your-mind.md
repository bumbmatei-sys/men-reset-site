Most of us have heard the phrase "renew your mind" so many times that it has stopped meaning anything. It sounds like something good Christians do, but nobody tells you what it actually looks like on a Tuesday.

So let us slow down and look at what Paul really wrote.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 1 we saw that a thought is not yet a sin. A thought knocks. Sin starts when you agree with it. Now we zoom out. If agreeing is where things go wrong, then renewing your mind is learning to agree with something else.

## What does it mean to renew your mind?

Here is the verse. "Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God" (Romans 12:2).

To renew your mind means bringing the way you think into agreement with what God says is true.

That matters, because if you are in Christ, a lot has already changed. You are a new creation (2 Corinthians 5:17). Paul even says, "we have the mind of Christ" (1 Corinthians 2:16). The change happened on the inside, in your spirit. But your thinking, your habits of thought, the things you assume without noticing, did not all change overnight.

James says God's word is like a mirror (James 1:23-24). You cannot see your new self by looking at your feelings or your track record. You see it by looking into what He says. Renewing your mind is looking in that mirror long enough that you start to believe what you see there.

## Be transformed, not transform yourself

Look closely at the words. Paul does not say "transform yourself." He says "be transformed." It is something done to you and in you.

In another letter he describes it like this. "We all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord" (2 Corinthians 3:18).

"Are being transformed." Ongoing. By the Spirit. While we behold Him.

So you are not doing this by willpower alone. Your part is to keep looking at Him. His part is to change you. That takes a lot of pressure off. You do not have to fix your own brain. You have to keep coming back to the One who does.

## How a stronghold forms

Paul calls a fixed way of wrong thinking a stronghold (2 Corinthians 10:4). It helps to know how one gets built, because it comes down the same way.

Nobody wakes up with a stronghold. It forms in steps.

- **A thought arrives.** "Nobody really wants you around."
- **You agree with it once.** Maybe on a hard day, when it seemed true.
- **You agree again.** Each time something happens that fits, the thought gets a little stronger.
- **It becomes normal.** You stop noticing it. It is not a thought anymore. It is just "how things are."
- **You live from it.** You pull back from people before they can reject you. Then you feel alone, which seems to prove the thought right.

Think of a path worn across a lawn. Nobody planned it. People just kept walking the same way until the grass gave up. A stronghold is a path in your mind that you have walked so many times that your thoughts run down it on their own.

## Why do we need to renew our minds?

Because you will live out of whatever you believe, whether it is true or not.

Paul says you renew your mind "that you may prove" God's will. A renewed mind starts to see life the way your heavenly Father sees it. Decisions get clearer. Fear has less say. Temptation loses some of its shine, because you can see where it goes.

Paul puts it another way in Ephesians. "Put off ... the old man ... and be renewed in the spirit of your mind, and ... put on the new man which was created according to God, in true righteousness and holiness" (Ephesians 4:22-24). Put off, be renewed, put on. You do not just pull out the weed. You plant something in its place.

## How long does it take to renew your mind?

Honestly, the rest of your life. That is not bad news.

You will notice real change much sooner. Some thoughts lose their grip in weeks. Old ones, the ones you have agreed with for years, usually take longer. We will talk about those in lesson 6.

What matters is not speed. It is direction. Every day you agree with your heavenly Father a little more, you are walking a new path across the lawn. And the old one slowly grows over.

## A daily rhythm that works

You do not need an hour. You need something you can keep. Here is a simple start.

| When | What you do | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning | Read a short passage until one line stands out. Write it down. | 5 to 10 minutes |
| Lunch | Read your line again. Ask, "What does this say about You, Father?" | 1 minute |
| Before bed | Read it again. Tell Him one thing it showed you. | 2 minutes |

If you are not sure where to read, start in the Gospel of John or in Psalms. If mornings are hard, [A Few Minutes With Scripture](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/a-few-minutes-with-scripture) shows how to keep a short time with Him that survives a busy week.
