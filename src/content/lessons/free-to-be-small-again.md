In the world, growing up means needing people less. You move out, pay your own bills and handle things yourself. Independence is the goal.

With God it runs the other way. Maturity means leaning on your heavenly Father more, not less. That is what childlike faith means, and it is what this lesson is about.

## Where we are in this course

[In the first lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/you-are-not-an-orphan) we saw the difference between living like an orphan and living like a child of God. An orphan has to look after himself. A child has a home.

[In the second lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/adopted-for-the-full-inheritance) we saw that you are a full heir, not a second-class member of the family.

[In the third lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/crying-abba) we learned to call your heavenly Father "Abba". The Holy Spirit in you already cries out "Abba, Father", and you get to join in.

At the end of that lesson I said that in the Kingdom we grow younger, not older. Let's see what that means.

## Who is the greatest in the Kingdom?

One day the disciples argued about which of them was the greatest. These were grown men who had followed Jesus for a while, and they still wanted to know who was on top.

Jesus did not give them a talk about ranking. He called a little child, stood him in the middle of them, and said:

"Assuredly, I say to you, unless you are converted and become as little children, you will by no means enter the kingdom of heaven. Therefore whoever humbles himself as this little child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 18:3-4).

Not a king, not a scholar, not a soldier. A child. In that culture a child had no status and nothing to offer. He depended on his parents for food, safety and everything else. And he did not pretend otherwise.

Jesus said that is what greatness looks like in His Kingdom. In plain words: the way up is down. The men who grow the most are the ones who stop trying to look strong and let their heavenly Father carry them.

## What does childlike faith mean?

Childlike faith is honest, humble, trusting and teachable.

It is honest. When a little boy falls and scrapes his knee, he does not sit there acting tough. He runs to his dad.

It is humble. A child does not earn his place at the table. He belongs because he is a son.

It is trusting. A child jumps into his dad's arms at the pool, not because he worked out the odds, but because he knows his dad.

It is teachable. A child asks a lot of questions. He does not know everything, and he is fine with that.

## Is childlike faith biblical?

Yes. Jesus said it plainly in Matthew 18, and again when He said the kingdom must be received "as a little child" (Mark 10:15).

But childlike is not childish. Jesus is not telling you to switch off your mind, be naive or skip your responsibilities. In lesson two we said a grown son learns to look after what he was given. That is still true. You grow in responsibility and you grow in dependence at the same time. A man who leads his home well and still runs to his heavenly Father like a small boy is living the way Jesus described.

## Jesus lived this way

If anyone could have stood on His own strength, it was Jesus. Listen to how He talked about Himself:

"Most assuredly, I say to you, the Son can do nothing of Himself, but what He sees the Father do" (John 5:19).

"I can of Myself do nothing" (John 5:30).

The Son of God lived in constant dependence on His Father. So dependence is not weakness. It is how the Son lives. And He invites you into the same life: "I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit; for without Me you can do nothing" (John 15:5).

## Power in weakness

Paul had a "thorn in the flesh". We do not know exactly what it was, but it hurt, and he asked the Lord three times to take it away. God gave him something else instead:

"And He said to me, 'My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness.' Therefore most gladly I will rather boast in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me" (2 Corinthians 12:9).

Weakness you bring to your heavenly Father becomes the place His power shows up. You do not have to be strong enough. You have to be honest enough to say you are not.

## The danger of "I got this"

Here is where many of us get stuck. If you are fighting porn, gaming or endless scrolling, you know the pattern. The habit grows when you are alone and tell yourself, "I've got this."

So you try to beat it with willpower alone and tell no one. You grit your teeth for a few days, you fall, you hide, and the shame gets heavier.

Pride says, "I will fix this first, then I will come to God." But a child does not clean himself up before he runs to his dad. He runs first. The strongest step you can take is often not a bigger effort. It is coming to your heavenly Father with open hands and honest words.

## Resting, not straining

"Surely I have calmed and quieted my soul, like a weaned child with his mother; like a weaned child is my soul within me" (Psalm 131:2).

A weaned child is not crying for milk anymore. He is not working for anything. He is just resting close to his mother. Dependence is not only for emergencies. It is a quiet trust all through the day.

Jesus said, "Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow" (Matthew 6:28). A lily grows by receiving water, sun and good soil. It does not strain. Growth comes from receiving.

## What does dependence on God look like?

Keep it simple.

- Start the day by saying, "I can't do today without You," before you check your phone.
- When the urge hits, tell your heavenly Father the truth instead of hiding it. "I feel weak right now. Help me."
- Ask for help before the hard moment, not only after you fall.
- Let one person you trust know what you are carrying. Habits grow in hiding.

Short, honest prayers count. A boy who calls "Dad" does not give a speech.

## The lie and the truth

| The lie | The truth |
| --- | --- |
| "I should be strong enough to handle this by now." | My strength is made perfect in weakness. (2 Corinthians 12:9) |
| "Needing God is for beginners." | Whoever humbles himself as this little child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven. (Matthew 18:4) |
| "I can manage most things without God." | Without Me you can do nothing. (John 15:5) |
| "I have to fix myself before I come to Him." | Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need. (Hebrews 4:16) |
| "I have to fight this alone." | Woe to him who is alone when he falls, for he has no one to help him up. (Ecclesiastes 4:10) |
