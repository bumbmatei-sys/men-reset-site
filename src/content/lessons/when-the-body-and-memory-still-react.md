You are having a normal day. Then a door slams, or someone raises their voice, or you smell a certain aftershave, or you drive past a building. Suddenly your heart is pounding, your chest is tight, and you want to either hit something or get out. Or you go strangely blank and far away.

A minute ago everything was fine. Now nothing feels safe. And afterward you feel embarrassed, or angry at yourself. "Why can I not just get over this?"

This lesson is about that moment. In lesson 1 we saw that what happened to you is not who you are. Now we get practical about what to do when your body still acts like it is happening again.

## What happens when trauma is triggered?

When something terrible happens, your body learns to watch for danger. It remembers the sounds, smells, places and tones that came with it. Later, when something similar shows up, your body can react before your mind has time to think. It is trying to protect you.

That is why a trigger can feel so out of proportion. Your head knows you are in your own kitchen. Your body thinks you are back there.

We are not doctors, and this is not a medical explanation. But it helps to know this much: reacting does not mean you are weak, crazy, or faithless. It means a body that once needed to be on guard has not yet learned that it is allowed to rest.

## What does a trauma trigger feel like?

It is different for different people. Some common ones:

- Your heart races, your breathing gets fast or shallow, your muscles go tight.
- You feel a sudden rush of anger that is much bigger than the moment.
- You want to run, leave the room, or get in the car and drive.
- You go numb, blank, or feel far away from your own body.
- A picture or feeling from the past pushes into your mind.
- Later you feel exhausted, ashamed, or irritable for hours.

Men often notice the anger first. If you snap at your wife or kids out of nowhere and then wonder where it came from, it may be worth asking whether something reminded your body of an old danger.

## Why can a normal day suddenly feel unsafe?

Because triggers do not wait for a good time. They show up at the grocery store, in a church service, during an argument, on an anniversary you forgot was coming, or in bed at night.

Some are easy to spot. Others take time to notice. A counselor can help you find patterns you would not see alone. You can also keep a few notes after a hard moment: where you were, what you heard or saw, what time of day it was. Over weeks, a pattern often shows up.

You do not have to avoid your whole life to stay safe. But you also do not have to walk straight into every reminder to prove you are strong. Wisdom is somewhere in between, and a counselor can help you find it.

## God is not disappointed in your body

The prophet Elijah once ran for his life and collapsed under a tree, so worn out he asked to die. God did not lecture him. An angel woke him and gave him food and water. Then the angel came again and said, "Arise and eat, because the journey is too great for you" (1 Kings 19:7).

God cared for Elijah's body first. Sleep, food, water. Then He spoke to him.

Jesus Himself, the night before He died, said, "My soul is exceedingly sorrowful, even to death" (Mark 14:34). He was "a Man of sorrows and acquainted with grief" (Isaiah 53:3). He knows what it is like when your whole body is under strain. He does not look down on you for it.

## A simple "when it hits" plan

Make this plan now, on a calm day. In the moment you will not be able to think clearly, so you want the steps already decided.

| Step | What I do | My version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Name it | Say to myself, "This is a trigger. I am safe right now." | |
| 2. Ground my body | Feet flat on the floor. Notice five things I can see and three I can hear. | |
| 3. Slow my breathing | Breathe out slowly, longer than I breathe in, five times. | |
| 4. Pray one line | "Father, I am here with You. You are here with me." | |
| 5. Step away if needed | Tell the people near me, "I need ten minutes," and go somewhere quiet. | |
| 6. One ordinary thing | Drink water, wash my face, walk around the block, then go back. | |
| 7. Note it later | Where was I, what set it off, what helped? | |

Fill in the "My version" column with what fits you. Maybe your one line is shorter. Maybe walking helps more than sitting. Keep it simple enough to remember.

Tell your wife or a close friend about step 5 ahead of time. "When I say I need ten minutes, it is not about you. I am coming back." That one conversation can save a lot of hurt.

## Do trauma triggers ever go away?

For many people they become less frequent and less strong over time, especially with good help. Some may never fully disappear. What usually changes is how long they last, how much they control you, and how quickly you come back.

That is real progress, even if it does not feel dramatic. "In the multitude of my anxieties within me, Your comforts delight my soul" (Psalm 94:19). The anxieties are still there in that verse. So is the comfort.

## When to get more help

Please talk to a trained counselor and your doctor if any of these are true:

- Triggers happen often, or they are getting worse.
- You are not sleeping, or you have nightmares most nights.
- You are using alcohol, porn, gaming, or anything else to numb it.
- Your anger is hurting your family.
- You feel far away from yourself for long stretches.

That is not giving up on God. It is using the help He has put around you.

If at any point you feel unsafe, or have thoughts of ending your life, call or text **988** in the US, any time of day. If you are in immediate danger, call 911.
