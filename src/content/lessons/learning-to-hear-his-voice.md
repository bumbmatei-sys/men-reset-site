"I just don't hear God." Many sincere Christians say this. They read about people in the Bible who heard God speak, and they wonder what is wrong with them.

Usually nothing is wrong. They are often hearing more than they think. They just have not learned to recognize it.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 3 you learned how to [fellowship with the Holy Spirit](/tracks/walking-with-the-holy-spirit/fellowship-with-the-holy-spirit) through the day. Fellowship goes both ways. This lesson is about the other side: how He speaks to you, and how to test what you hear.

## Can the Holy Spirit talk to you?

Yes. Jesus said, "My sheep hear My voice, and I know them, and they follow Me" (John 10:27). He did not say "some of My sheep." Hearing God is part of being His.

In the book of Acts, the Spirit speaks often. "As they ministered to the Lord and fasted, the Holy Spirit said, 'Now separate to Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them'" (Acts 13:2). Another time He stopped Paul from going where he had planned (Acts 16:6-7). The Spirit leads His people. "For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God" (Romans 8:14).

## How does the Holy Spirit speak to us?

Rarely with an audible voice. Usually it is much quieter. When God met Elijah, He was not in the wind, the earthquake, or the fire. Then came "a still small voice" (1 Kings 19:12).

Here are the most common ways He speaks:

- **Through Scripture.** This is the first and clearest way. A verse stands out and speaks straight into your situation. The Spirit takes the written Word and makes it alive for you today.
- **Through an inner nudge.** A thought you did not plan, often simple: call him, apologize, wait, give, say no. It tends to come quietly, not with pressure or panic.
- **Through peace, or the lack of it.** Sometimes you have a settled peace about one path and a growing unease about another.
- **Through other believers.** A friend, a pastor, a sermon that seems written for you. God often confirms through more than one voice.
- **Through circumstances.** Doors open or close. Paul tried to go one way, and the Spirit stopped him (Acts 16:6-7).

Most of the time it is not one dramatic moment. It is many small ones you learn to notice.

## How do I know if it is the Holy Spirit or my own thoughts?

Good question, and an important one. John wrote, "Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God" (1 John 4:1). Testing is not unbelief. It is wisdom.

Here are five simple tests:

1. **Does it agree with Scripture?** The Spirit wrote the Bible. He will never tell you to do something it forbids.
2. **Does it sound like Jesus?** The Spirit glorifies Jesus (John 16:14). His voice carries Jesus' character: truth and love together. It may correct you, but it does not crush you.
3. **What fruit does it lead to?** His leading moves you toward love, joy, peace, patience, and self-control (Galatians 5:22-23). Fear, pride, and pressure are warning signs.
4. **Do mature believers confirm it?** For anything big, talk to people who walk with God. Humility protects you.
5. **Does it still stand after you wait?** God is rarely in a rush. A true word usually becomes clearer with time, not weaker.

There is also the voice of the accuser, who condemns and pushes you away from God. That is not the Spirit's voice. Lesson 5 is about telling them apart. For more on sorting your thoughts, read [How to Take Every Thought Captive](/teachings/take-every-thought-captive-2026).

## Why am I not hearing the Holy Spirit?

A few common reasons:

- **Too much noise.** If every quiet moment gets filled with a screen, there is little room to listen. "Be still, and know that I am God" (Psalm 46:10).
- **Not asking.** Many people pray and then get up before listening. Ask, then wait a few minutes.
- **Not reading the Word.** He speaks most clearly through what He has already said. The more Scripture you know, the more He has to remind you of.
- **Unfinished business.** If you sense Him asking for something small and you keep saying no, it is hard to hear the next thing. Do the last thing He said.
- **Expecting only something dramatic.** He often speaks in a simple thought you almost dismissed.

And sometimes He is quiet for a season. That is not rejection. Keep walking in what you already know.

## How do you hear the Holy Spirit more clearly?

The same way you learn any voice: by spending time with Him. A mother can pick out her child's voice in a crowded room because she has heard it so often.

Practice "waiting on the Lord" the way a waiter waits at a table. Not sleeping in the corner, but attentive and ready to serve. Read, ask, wait, write it down, test it, and obey. Each time you obey something small and see it was Him, you learn His voice a little better.
