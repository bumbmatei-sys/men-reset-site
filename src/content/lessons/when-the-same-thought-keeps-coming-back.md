You did everything this course has taught. You caught the thought early. You answered it with a verse out loud. You felt free.

And the next morning, there it was again. Same words. Same pull.

It is easy to think, "This does not work," or "I am the exception." You are not. This last lesson is about what to do with the thought that keeps coming back.

## Where we are in this course

Over five lessons you have learned that a thought is not yet a sin, that renewing your mind is a daily process, how to tell accusation from conviction, how to catch a thought in the first seconds, and how to answer it with what is written. Now we put it all together for the long haul.

## Why do the same thoughts keep coming back?

There are a few honest reasons.

**It is an old path.** In lesson 2 we compared a stronghold to a path worn across a lawn. If you agreed with a thought for ten years, your mind will keep trying to walk that path for a while, even after you have decided to stop. The grass does not grow back the day you choose a new way.

**Some moments make it louder.** When you are tired, hungry, lonely, stressed, or bored, old thoughts get stronger. That does not mean you went backward. It means you are human. Notice the pattern and plan for those moments.

**The enemy tests what you have learned.** After Jesus answered every temptation in the wilderness, Luke writes, "Now when the devil had ended every temptation, he departed from Him until an opportune time" (Luke 4:13). Until an opportune time. Even Jesus faced a return. If it came back for Him, it will come back for you. That is not a sign you failed.

## A returning thought is not a failed answer

Here is a mistake many of us make. We think the goal is for the thought to never come again, so when it returns, we feel like we lost.

But the goal is not that the thought never knocks. The goal is that you answer it every time it knocks.

Think of sweeping leaves in autumn. You sweep the courtyard, and by afternoon more leaves have fallen. Did sweeping fail? No. The leaves fall because it is autumn. You sweep because it is your courtyard. One day the season changes and far fewer leaves come down.

Every time you answer a thought with the truth, you are walking the new path. Even if the thought comes back tomorrow, today's answer counted.

## Answer it every time

Practically, this means keeping it short and steady.

- Use the same verse for the same lie. You do not need a new answer each time. Repetition is how a new path gets worn.
- Do not argue. Answer and move on, like we practiced in lesson 4.
- Do not grade yourself by how often it came. Grade yourself by how often you answered.
- Tell one person you trust which thought keeps coming back. Saying it out loud to another believer takes some of its power. If you do not have that person yet, read [How to Find a Christian Accountability Partner](/teachings/christian-accountability-partner).

## Do not let despair have the last word

When a thought keeps returning, a second thought often comes with it. "Nothing is changing. You will always be like this."

That is just another lie. Answer it the same way.

Your heavenly Father's patience with you does not run out. "Through the LORD's mercies we are not consumed, Because His compassions fail not. They are new every morning; Great is Your faithfulness" (Lamentations 3:22-23). And the work is His, not only yours. "He who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ" (Philippians 1:6). He started it. He will finish it.

If a returning thought has led you to fall again, [What to Do After a Porn Relapse](/teachings/what-to-do-after-porn-relapse) walks through the next hour. If you find it hard to pray afterward, read [How to Pray When You Keep Falling](/teachings/how-to-pray-when-you-keep-falling).

## Answer from who you are

This is where the whole course lands.

You do not fight thoughts in order to become a child of God. You fight them because you already are one. In Know Who You Are we learned to live in this order: I am, then I have, then I do. It works here too.

- **I am.** "Behold what manner of love the Father has bestowed on us, that we should be called children of God!" (1 John 3:1). I am His child, even on the day the thought came back.
- **I have.** I have the mind of Christ (1 Corinthians 2:16). I have no condemnation (Romans 8:1). I have His word and His Spirit.
- **I do.** So I answer the thought again. Not to earn anything. Because that is what His children do.

If that order is new to you, read [I Am, I Have, I Do](/tracks/know-who-you-are/i-am-i-have-i-do) and [Working From Rest](/tracks/know-who-you-are/working-from-rest).
