Most family patterns run through a father. Maybe yours was there every day and hard to please. Maybe he left when you were small. Maybe he was in the house but never really with you. Or maybe he was a good man with one or two things that still hurt.

This lesson is not therapy, and it is not a trial of your dad. It is a look at the father you had, next to the Father you have.

## What is the father wound?

The father wound is the gap between what a boy needed from his father and what he actually got. Every boy needs to know a few things: you are wanted, you are safe, you have what it takes, and I am proud of you. When those were missing, or when the opposite was said, a wound forms.

It can come from:

- **Absence.** He left, died, was in prison, or was simply never around.
- **Harshness.** Anger, criticism, or fear in the house.
- **Distance.** He was present but closed. Provided, but never talked, never hugged, never said much.
- **Addiction.** His habit came first, and you learned to work around it.

None of this was your fault. A boy does not cause his father to leave or to drink.

## How does the father wound show up in men?

Often it looks like strength from the outside. Common signs:

- Working nonstop to prove you are enough.
- Anger that comes out fast, especially at home.
- Shutting down when someone gets close.
- Never asking for help.
- Struggling to believe God is pleased with you.
- Swearing you will never be like him, and catching yourself doing exactly what he did.

If you see yourself in that list, you are not broken beyond repair. You are a son who needs his Father.

## Your earthly father is not the picture of God

Many men look at God through the lens of their dad. If your father was absent, God feels far. If he was harsh, God feels angry. If he was hard to please, God feels disappointed.

Jesus came to fix that picture. He said, "He who has seen Me has seen the Father" (John 14:9). Look at Jesus with children, with sinners, with Peter after he failed. That is what your heavenly Father is like.

Then look at the father in the story Jesus told about the lost son. The son had wasted everything. "But when he was still a great way off, his father saw him and had compassion, and ran and fell on his neck and kissed him" (Luke 15:20). He ran. He did not wait for a speech. That is the Father you have.

David wrote, "When my father and my mother forsake me, Then the LORD will take care of me" (Psalm 27:10). And: "A father of the fatherless, a defender of widows, Is God in His holy habitation. God sets the solitary in families" (Psalm 68:5-6).

## You are a son, not an orphan

Paul wrote, "You did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, 'Abba, Father'" (Romans 8:15). If you belong to Jesus, you are not trying to earn a place in the family. You already have one.

That changes how you work and how you lead your home. You stop working to make the Father pleased with you, and start working as a son He is already pleased with. If you want more on this, read [You Are Not an Orphan](/tracks/know-who-you-are/you-are-not-an-orphan) in the Know Who You Are course, and [Know His Love](/tracks/know-gods-love).

## How to heal the father wound

Healing is usually slow, and it is usually done with others. A few plain steps:

1. **Tell God the truth.** What did you need that you did not get? Say it to Him. He can take it.
2. **Replace the lie.** If you believe "I will never be enough," find what God says instead and read it daily.
3. **Receive.** You cannot give your children what you never received. Spend time with your heavenly Father as a son, not a worker.
4. **Let other men in.** God often fathers us through older men in the church. Look for one.
5. **Get skilled help when it is heavy.** A licensed Christian counselor is wisdom, not weak faith.

## Should you forgive an absent father?

Jesus calls us to forgive (Ephesians 4:32). But be clear about what forgiveness is and is not.

- It is **not excusing** what he did. It was wrong.
- It is **not rushing**. You may need to forgive the same thing many times as new layers come up. Saying "I forgive him" while you still feel the anger is a real start, not a fake one.
- It is **not remaining in danger**. Forgiveness does not require you to let an unsafe person back into your home or near your children.
- It **is** handing the debt to God, so that it stops ruling your heart.

## Should I contact my absent father?

There is no single answer. Pray, talk to your pastor or a counselor, and think about safety first, especially if he is still drinking, using, or violent. Some men find a careful phone call or letter brings peace. Others find that distance is the wiser and more loving choice. "If it is possible, as much as depends on you, live peaceably with all men" (Romans 12:18). Sometimes it is not possible, and that is not your failure.
