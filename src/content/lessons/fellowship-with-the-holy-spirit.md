You can know a lot about someone and still not know them. You can read a man's biography, quote his speeches, and recognize his face, and never once have sat down with him for coffee.

Many believers relate to the Holy Spirit that way. They know the doctrine. They have never really talked with Him.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 1 you saw that the Holy Spirit is a Person. In lesson 2 you saw what He does as [Helper, Comforter, and Teacher](/tracks/walking-with-the-holy-spirit/your-helper-comforter-and-teacher). This lesson is about the relationship itself: what it means to have fellowship with Him every day.

## What does fellowship with the Holy Spirit mean?

Paul ended one of his letters with a blessing: "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Spirit be with you all" (2 Corinthians 13:14).

The Greek word for communion is koinonia. It means sharing, partnership, fellowship. A shared life. Notice what Paul connects to each Person. Grace with Jesus. Love with the Father. Communion, or fellowship, with the Holy Spirit.

So fellowship with the Holy Spirit is not a special experience for a few people. It is meant to be normal Christian life. He lives in you. "Do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you" (1 Corinthians 6:19). He is not a visitor you have to call down. He is a Friend who is already home.

## Is it okay to talk to the Holy Spirit?

Yes. Jesus taught us to pray "Our Father," and most of our prayers go to the Father in Jesus' name. That is good. But the Holy Spirit is God too, and the Persons of the Trinity are never jealous of each other. Talking with the Spirit, thanking Him, and welcoming Him is not a strange extra. It honors the Father and the Son, because He is the Spirit of Christ.

God looks at the heart more than the exact wording. You do not need to worry about getting the formula right. You need to show up.

## What I took from Good Morning, Holy Spirit

Benny Hinn's book Good Morning, Holy Spirit helped me see this. I recommend it. Here are a few things I took from it, in my own words.

- **He is someone, not something.** The book's main message is that the Holy Spirit is a Person you can know, not only a power you can use.
- **Start the day with Him.** The title comes from a simple habit: greeting the Holy Spirit first thing in the morning, the way you would greet someone you live with.
- **Talk with Him, then listen.** Fellowship goes both ways. You speak, and you also wait and let Him lead.
- **Guard the friendship.** He is gentle. You can grieve Him with what you choose, so keep short accounts.
- **He always leads you to Jesus.** Knowing the Spirit never takes you away from Christ. It makes Jesus more real.

For a short overview and a 10-minute morning template, read [How to Build a Relationship With the Holy Spirit](/teachings/relationship-with-the-holy-spirit). This lesson focuses on the rest of the day.

## How do you fellowship with the Holy Spirit daily?

Think of the people you are closest to. You did not get close through one long meeting a year. You got close through many small moments. Fellowship with the Spirit works the same way.

1. **Greet Him in the morning.** Before the phone, before the news. "Good morning, Holy Spirit. Thank You that You are here. I want to walk with You today." Then talk with Him about the day.
2. **Invite Him into ordinary things.** The commute, the email you dread, the dishes. "Holy Spirit, do this with me." You will be surprised how many small moments become places of prayer.
3. **Check in at midday.** Set a reminder if you need to. Stop for one minute and ask, "What are You doing here? What do You want me to see?"
4. **Worship Him.** Paul connects being filled with the Spirit to "singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord" (Ephesians 5:19). You do not need a band. A song in the car counts.
5. **Read the Word with Him.** Ask Him to open it to you before you start. He wrote it.
6. **Thank Him at night.** Look back over the day and name one thing He did. Thankfulness trains your eyes to see Him.

## What if I feel nothing?

That is normal, especially at first. Fellowship is not measured by goosebumps. Sometimes His presence is very tangible: peace, tears, a weight lifting. Many days it is quiet. Your feelings change; He does not.

His presence never leaves a believer, but your awareness of Him can rise and fall. Often what you feed your heart affects how clearly you sense Him. Turn the noise down. Give Him your attention. If there is distance, it is usually because we drifted, not because He did.

Spiritual thirst is not a problem to hide either. It is an invitation. David wrote from a dry place, "O God, You are my God; Early will I seek You; My soul thirsts for You" (Psalm 63:1). Let the thirst move you toward Him, not away.

## Do I have to earn His presence?

No. This is important. You are not trying to be good enough for the Holy Spirit to stay. He came to live in you because of Jesus, not because of your performance. Fellowship flows out of being loved.

If you have been carrying a picture of God as a hard, distant judge, read [What Kind of Father Is God?](/teachings/what-kind-of-father-is-god). The Spirit is the Spirit of that same Father.
