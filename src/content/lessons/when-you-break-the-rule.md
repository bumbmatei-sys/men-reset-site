This is the last lesson in the course. So far you have watched your evenings, set a hard stop, planned the first ten minutes, set up your phone, and filled the empty evening on purpose. If you skipped ahead, start at [Why the Session Never Stays Twenty Minutes](/tracks/quit-gaming-and-the-phone/why-the-session-never-stays-twenty-minutes).

Now the honest part. At some point you will break the rule. You will go past your stop. You will reinstall the game. You will take the phone into bed and lose two hours.

That does not mean the course did not work. It means you are a person. What matters is what you do next.

## How do you quit gaming for good?

Not by never slipping. By coming back faster every time.

Most people think "for good" means a perfect record. Then they break it once and decide the whole thing was a lie. "I knew I could not do it." So they go back to five hours a night, because if they already failed, why try.

That thinking is the real danger. One bad night costs you one night. Giving up costs you months.

"For a righteous man may fall seven times And rise again" (Proverbs 24:16). The verse does not describe a man who never falls. It describes a man who keeps getting up.

## Come back the same hour

When you notice you are past your stop, come back right then. Not tomorrow. Not Monday. Not the first of the month.

Close the game. Put the phone in its spot. Stand up and leave the room. Do your one thing, even if it is 1 a.m. and the one thing is just brushing your teeth and going to bed.

It will feel pointless. "I already lost the night, what difference does ten more minutes make?" A big one. Stopping at 1 a.m. instead of 3 a.m. is two hours of sleep. And it teaches you something important: you can stop even after you broke the rule. The rule is not all or nothing.

## Tell the truth to your heavenly Father

Then talk to Him. Plainly.

"Father, I said I would stop at 9:45. I played until 1. I am sorry. Thank You that You still love me. Help me tomorrow."

That is all. You do not need to beg or punish yourself. "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9).

Think of the son in Jesus' story who wasted everything and came home. "But when he was still a great way off, his father saw him and had compassion, and ran and fell on his neck and kissed him" (Luke 15:20). The father ran. That is what your heavenly Father is like when you turn back. Not arms crossed. Running.

If shame is what keeps you stuck after a bad night, read [Shame and the Loop](/tracks/break-free/break-the-porn-and-shame-cycle). The same loop shows up with games and phones.

## Find the one gap

The next day, in daylight, look at what happened. Not to beat yourself up. To learn.

Ask one question: what was the gap? Usually it is one of these:

- I did not set the stop or the alarm.
- The alarm was in reach and I swiped it.
- I sat back down before doing my one thing.
- The phone came into the bedroom.
- The evening had no plan, and I was tired.
- Something hard happened, and I wanted to escape.

Pick the one that fits. Fix only that one. If the alarm was in reach, move it. If you were tired with no plan, add a low energy option. Small fixes hold. Big new rules after a bad night usually do not.

## Do not make the next evening harsher

After a bad night, it is tempting to swing hard the other way. "No gaming for a year." "I am throwing the console out." "No phone after 6 p.m."

Sometimes a longer break really is wise. If you keep breaking your stop no matter what you change, think about quitting all at once for a set time, and tell someone. [How to Quit Gaming as a Christian](/teachings/quit-gaming-christian-2026) talks through that choice.

But do not decide it at 2 a.m. out of shame. Decide it in daylight, calmly, maybe with someone you trust. Most of the time, the best move is to run the same plan again tomorrow with one fix.

His mercies "are new every morning" (Lamentations 3:23). You do not have to earn a new start. It is already there.

## Your starting again card

| When I break the rule | What I will do |
| --- | --- |
| The moment I notice | Close it, stand up, leave the room |
| Within the hour | Do my one thing, or go to bed |
| Before I sleep | Tell my heavenly Father the truth, and thank Him |
| The next day | Find the one gap and write one fix |
| The next evening | Same stop, same one thing, same phone curfew |
| If it keeps happening | Tell someone, and think about a longer break |

Copy it onto a card and keep it next to your stop time sticky note.

## What this course was really about

This course was never only about games or phones. It was about your evenings. The hours where your sleep, your work, your family and your walk with your heavenly Father either get you, or get whatever is left.

You are not trying to become a person who never plays a game again or never looks at a screen. You are becoming a person whose evenings belong to what matters most. One stop, one walk out of the room, one phone on the kitchen counter, one plain evening at a time.

If you want to go deeper into who you are while you do this, the course [Know Who You Are](/tracks/know-who-you-are) is a good next step. You change from a place of being loved, not to earn it.

## Do this today

The next time you go past your stop, close the game or put the phone in its spot within the same hour. Tell your heavenly Father plainly what happened and thank Him for His mercy. Then write down the one gap that let it happen and one small fix for tomorrow evening. Use the tracker below for this week.
