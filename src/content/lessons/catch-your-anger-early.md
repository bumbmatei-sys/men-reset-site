Most men describe a blowup the same way. "It came out of nowhere." "I just snapped." "One second I was fine."

It almost never comes out of nowhere. It rises in steps. We just were not watching the first ones.

## Where we are in this course

In lesson 1 you learned that anger is not the same as sin, and you wrote down what your anger looks like. Now we work on timing. The earlier you notice anger, the more choices you still have.

## How does anger build?

Think of it as a scale from 1 to 10.

- **1 to 3.** Irritation. A small tightness. You notice the dishes, the noise, the tone.
- **4 to 6.** Frustration. Your voice gets sharper. You start building your case in your head.
- **7 to 8.** Anger. Your face is hot, your words come fast, you want to win.
- **9 to 10.** Rage. You are not thinking anymore. You are only reacting.

At a 3 you can still choose almost anything. You can breathe, ask a question, let it go, or name the problem calmly. At a 9 the part of you that weighs consequences is hardly in the room. That is why "just control yourself" at a 9 rarely works. The work happens earlier.

"The beginning of strife is like releasing water; Therefore stop contention before a quarrel starts" (Proverbs 17:14). A small leak is easy to close. A burst dam is not.

## Your body knows first

Before you say the sharp word, your body usually sends signals. Here are common ones. Circle the ones that are yours.

- Jaw clenching, teeth pressed together
- Face or neck getting hot
- Heart beating faster, breath getting short and high in the chest
- Shoulders lifting, hands tightening
- Voice getting louder or, for some men, very quiet and cold
- Talking faster, interrupting
- A thought like "Here we go again" or "I'm done"

These are not sins. They are warnings, like the rumble strip on the side of a highway. The goal is to feel the rumble strip and steer before you hit the ditch.

## How to control anger at home

Most anger at home shows up in the same few places. Not random places. Predictable ones. Look at your table from lesson 1 and you will probably see a pattern.

- **The first ten minutes after work.** You walk in tired and the house is loud.
- **Bedtime with kids.** Everyone is exhausted, and the routine falls apart.
- **Money talks.** Fear about bills comes out as blame.
- **Mess and noise.** The thing you asked to be done is not done.
- **Being interrupted or corrected.** Especially in front of others.
- **Driving.** Late, traffic, someone cuts you off, and the family is in the car.

When you know your hot spots, you can walk into them awake instead of asleep. That one change does a lot.

## Why am I so angry at my wife?

Many husbands ask this quietly. Often the honest answer is not about her at all. You may be exhausted. You may be carrying fear about work or money. You may feel unheard or disrespected, and instead of saying so plainly, it comes out as irritation. The person closest to you gets what has built up all day.

This is not her fault, and it is not a reason to blame yourself into the ground either. It is information. "Husbands, love your wives and do not be bitter toward them" (Colossians 3:19). Paul names bitterness because it is a real temptation in marriage. We will look at what is underneath in lesson 5. For now, notice when it rises and where.

## Slow is strong

"He who is slow to anger is better than the mighty, And he who rules his spirit than he who takes a city" (Proverbs 16:32). The world often treats a quick temper as strength. The Bible says the stronger man is the one who can rule his own spirit.

"A fool vents all his feelings, But a wise man holds them back" (Proverbs 29:11). Holding back here does not mean stuffing it down forever. It means not letting it out on people in the heat of the moment. You will still deal with it. Just not by venting it on your family.

## What if I only notice at a 9?

That is normal at the start. Do not give up. Each time, look back afterward and ask, "What was the first sign I missed?" Write it down. Over a few weeks your catch point moves earlier. A man who used to notice at a 9 starts noticing at a 7, then a 5. That is real progress.

## When to get more help

If your anger often goes from 1 to 10 in seconds, if you have ever hurt, threatened, or frightened someone, or if anyone at home is afraid of you, get help today. Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at **1-800-799-7233**, and call **911** if anyone is in danger now. Do not ask anyone to keep it quiet. If you feel very low or out of control, call or text **988**. A licensed Christian counselor can help you learn your signs faster. That is wisdom, not weak faith.
