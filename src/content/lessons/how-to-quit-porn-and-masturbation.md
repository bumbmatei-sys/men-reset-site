**How to quit porn and masturbation** gets fuzzy when you treat them as separate projects. For a lot of men they are one sequence. The video starts it, or the habit starts it and the video finishes it. If you ban the site and keep the rest, you have renamed the problem.

This is adult, direct teaching. It is not a dare to be reckless, and it is not a claim that every married sexual life looks the same. It is about the private habit that runs on fantasy and a screen.

## Close the loophole

Write the rule while you are not aroused. “I do not masturbate to porn or to a replay of porn.” If you are single and your conviction goes further, write that rule too, in your own words, and keep it specific. Vague rules get lawyered at midnight.

Fleeing, in the verse for this lesson, is physical. You leave. You do not stand in the doorway and negotiate a cleaner version of the same act. Fantasy that replays the video is the video with the screen off. Call it that, so you stop congratulating yourself for a technicality.

## Expect arousal and move

Arousal is not an emergency and it is not a command. Your body can be loud and still not get a vote on the plan you wrote in daylight. The move is the same as the urge plan in [how to deal with porn urges](/tracks/break-free-from-porn/how-to-deal-with-porn-urges): change rooms, change posture, do something that uses your body without feeding the image.

Cold water on your face, a walk, a set of pushups, a shower with the door unlocked if you live with people and the locked bathroom is the danger. Pick one in advance. Deciding during the urge is how the loophole wins.

If you are married, do not use your spouse as a tool to “get it out of your system” with a head full of someone else. That is not intimacy. If sex in your marriage is broken or absent, that is a real problem, and porn will offer a counterfeit. The counterfeit makes the real problem worse. Say the truth to your wife if you safely can, or to a pastor or counselor who will not wink.

## Watch the helpers that are not helpers

Alcohol lowers the argument you were going to make. So does exhaustion, and so does a long game session that already trained you to chase the next hit. If those are part of your slide, name them in the rule. “No drink on nights I am alone.” “Games off at 10.” The [gaming track](/tracks/quit-gaming-and-the-phone) is not written yet. The boundary can still start.

Do not replace porn with a feed that is “not quite porn.” You know the difference in your gut. If you would hide the screen when someone walks in, it counts.

## The day after you want it and do not

You may feel restless, irritable, or convinced that you need the release to sleep or to focus. That feeling is withdrawal from a habit, not proof that the rule is wrong. Go to bed anyway. Get up and do the first task of the morning. Eat. The feeling is allowed to be unpleasant.

## The places the rule usually dies

The rule dies in three places more than anywhere else. Know yours.

The bed. You are horizontal, the day is over, and the phone is in your hand “for the alarm.” The alarm can live across the room. If you need a sound in your ear, use a separate clock. Do not argue that you are a special case who can hold the phone and not use it. The data of your last month is the argument.

The bathroom. A lock, a few minutes, a phone. Leave the phone on the sink outside, or on the kitchen counter, before you go in. It feels silly. Silly is cheaper than the loop.

The car or the office with the door shut. If you are early to work, do not spend the extra minutes in the parking lot with a browser. Walk in. If the office door is the cue, prop it open when you are not on a call, or do the risky admin tasks in a shared space.

Write the place into the one-line rule. “I do not take the phone to the bathroom.” A rule that names the room is harder to lawyer than a rule about “purity.”

## What you tell your body afterward

The urge to “finish what you started” is the habit trying to complete a script. You can stop mid-script. It will feel unfinished. Unfinished is allowed. Put clothes on, leave the room, and do something with your hands that is not private. The unfinished feeling fades faster than the shame of a second round.

If you are shaking, angry, or wide awake, do not treat that as a sign you need the release to sleep. Get up, drink water, and read two pages of something slow, or pray in a sentence, or fold laundry. Then go back to bed without the phone. Sleep may come late. A late sleep is still better than training the script to the end.

If you break the rule, do not add a second session to “make the failure worth it.” Stop, tell the truth in your note, and return to the plan the same night if you can, or the next morning if you cannot.
