A glass of water can be full on Monday and half empty by Friday. Not because the water was bad, but because life pours it out. Walking with the Holy Spirit is a lot like that. You are not filled once for life. You keep coming back to the source.

## Where we are in this course

This is the last lesson. You have learned [who the Holy Spirit is](/tracks/walking-with-the-holy-spirit/who-the-holy-spirit-is), what He does, how to fellowship with Him, how to hear Him, and how [He convicts without condemning](/tracks/walking-with-the-holy-spirit/he-convicts-he-never-condemns). Now you will put it together: being filled with the Spirit every day and walking in step with Him.

## What does it mean to be filled with the Holy Spirit?

Every believer has the Holy Spirit. "If anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he is not His" (Romans 8:9). So being filled is not about getting a Spirit you do not have. It is about Him having more of you.

Paul wrote, "And do not be drunk with wine, in which is dissipation; but be filled with the Spirit" (Ephesians 5:18). A man who is drunk is under the influence of what he drank. A believer who is filled with the Spirit is under the influence of the Spirit: his words, his reactions, his choices.

Many Christians also speak of a time when the Spirit came on them in power, often called the baptism in the Holy Spirit. Jesus promised, "you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you" (Acts 1:8). Churches describe this in different ways, and good believers disagree on the details. What the whole Bible makes clear is that God wants you full, not running on empty.

## Can you be filled with the Holy Spirit more than once?

Yes. Look at the book of Acts. At Pentecost, "they were all filled with the Holy Spirit" (Acts 2:4). A short time later, the same group prayed after being threatened, and "they were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they spoke the word of God with boldness" (Acts 4:31). Same people, filled again.

In Ephesians 5:18, "be filled" is an ongoing command. It carries the sense of keep on being filled. Being filled is not a badge you earned years ago. It is a daily way of living.

## How do you get filled with the Holy Spirit?

It is simpler than many people make it.

1. **Ask.** Jesus said, "how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!" (Luke 11:13). You are not twisting the arm of a reluctant God. You are asking a generous Father.
2. **Come thirsty.** Jesus said, "He who believes in Me, as the Scripture has said, out of his heart will flow rivers of living water" (John 7:38). John explains that He was speaking of the Spirit (John 7:39). Rivers, not a small cup.
3. **Yield.** Instead of only asking, "How do I get more of the Holy Spirit?" pray, "Holy Spirit, take more of me." Give Him your plans, your mouth, your evenings, your fears.
4. **Clear the way.** If He has convicted you of something, deal with it. Confession keeps the channel open.
5. **Worship and stay in the Word.** Paul connects being filled to singing, thankfulness, and Scripture (Ephesians 5:19-20). These keep your heart open to Him.

You may feel something strong. You may feel only peace. Do not chase the feeling. Trust the Father's promise.

## What does walking in the Spirit look like?

"I say then: Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh" (Galatians 5:16). "If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit" (Galatians 5:25).

Walking is not dramatic. It is one step, then another. Walking in the Spirit means keeping in step with Him in ordinary choices: what you say when you are tired, what you do when you are alone, how you treat the person who annoys you, whether you obey that small nudge.

The path is not always a straight line. The Spirit often leads in ways you would not have planned. Your part is to keep your faith active, even when you do not know exactly where He is taking you. And when you get out of step, you do not quit. You confess, receive, and take the next step with Him.

## How does the fruit of the Spirit grow?

"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. Against such there is no law" (Galatians 5:22-23).

Notice it is fruit, not works. A branch does not strain to make grapes. It stays connected to the vine. Jesus said, "He who abides in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit; for without Me you can do nothing" (John 15:5).

So you do not grow patience by gritting your teeth harder. You grow it by staying close to the One who is patient, and by letting Him live through you in the moments that test you. Over time, people around you notice a difference before you do.

## What about the gifts of the Holy Spirit?

The Spirit gives gifts to believers "for the profit of all" (1 Corinthians 12:7). Paul tells us to "pursue love, and desire spiritual gifts" (1 Corinthians 14:1). The gifts are real, and they are for today.

But notice the order. Pursue love first. Paul also warned that even great gifts without love leave you with nothing: "though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, but have not love, I am nothing" (1 Corinthians 13:2).

So this course puts intimacy before activations. The gifts are not techniques you switch on, or a way to look spiritual. They flow out of a close friendship with the Holy Spirit. Remember the servant in Genesis 24 from lesson 1. The gifts he gave Rebekah belonged to his master, and they were meant to make her love Isaac, not the servant. Spiritual gifts work the same way. Real gifts make people fall in love with Jesus. If a gift keeps drawing attention to the person using it, something has gone wrong.

Stay close to Him. Ask Him to use you to bless others. Then be ready for Him to do it in ordinary places: a word of encouragement at work, a prayer for a sick friend, wisdom for a hard situation.
