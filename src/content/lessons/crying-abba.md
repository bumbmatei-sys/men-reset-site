Most men can say "God" without any trouble. Saying "Father" is harder. And saying it like a son who knows he is welcome is harder still.

That is what this lesson is about.

## Where we are in this course

[In the first lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/you-are-not-an-orphan) we looked at the difference between living like an orphan and living like a child of God. An orphan has to look after himself. A child has a home.

[In the second lesson](/tracks/know-who-you-are/adopted-for-the-full-inheritance) we saw that you are not a second-class member of the family. You are a full heir, and the Holy Spirit in you is the guarantee of that.

At the end of that lesson I said we would look at what it sounds like to call your heavenly Father "Abba". So let's do that now.

## The Spirit who cries "Abba"

Paul writes this in Romans 8:15: "For you did not receive the spirit of bondage again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption by whom we cry out, 'Abba, Father.'"

He says almost the same thing in Galatians 4:6: "And because you are sons, God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into your hearts, crying out, 'Abba, Father!'"

Notice who is doing the crying in Galatians. It is the Spirit. The Holy Spirit lives in you, and He is already calling God "Father" from the inside. You are not trying to start something. You are joining something that is already going on in you.

And Romans 8:16 adds: "The Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God." That is a quiet, inner sense that you belong. It does not always come with strong feelings. Often it is just a settled knowing that you are His.

## What does Abba Father mean?

Abba is the Aramaic word for father. Aramaic was the everyday language Jesus spoke. Abba was the word used at home, inside the family.

## Does Abba mean Daddy?

You may have heard that Abba simply means "Daddy". That is only half right. It is not baby talk. Grown men used it for their fathers too. So it is close and warm, but it is also respectful. Think of a grown son who still calls his dad "Dad". It is near, but not casual.

The word appears only three times in the New Testament: Mark 14:36, Romans 8:15 and Galatians 4:6. And in the Jewish prayers we have from that time, people almost never spoke to God this way. God was honored and feared, but He was not usually called by the family word for father. When Jesus did it, it was new. When He gave it to us, it was a gift.

## Abba on the worst night

The first time we hear Jesus say Abba, He is not on a mountain on a sunny day. He is in the garden of Gethsemane, the night before the cross. He is in deep distress.

"And He said, 'Abba, Father, all things are possible for You. Take this cup away from Me; nevertheless, not what I will, but what You will'" (Mark 14:36).

So Abba is not only for good days. It is the word you use when you are scared, when you are under pressure, when you do not know how this is going to end. It is the word of a son who runs to his father instead of away from him.

## A judge or a heavenly Father?

Many men only know God as an angry judge. They believe He exists. They may even believe He forgives. But in their gut, they expect Him to be disappointed.

Israel had this problem at Mount Sinai. When God spoke, there was thunder, fire and smoke. The people stood far off and told Moses, "You speak with us, and we will hear; but let not God speak with us, lest we die" (Exodus 20:19). They wanted a middleman because they were afraid to come close.

Hebrews 12:18-24 says we have not come to that mountain. We have come to Mount Zion, to the city of the living God, and to Jesus.

Jesus came to show us what the heavenly Father is really like. "He who has seen Me has seen the Father" (John 14:9). Look at how Jesus treated sinners, the sick, the ashamed. That is how the heavenly Father treats you.

Now the Holy Spirit does the same thing in your heart. His job is to reveal the heavenly Father to you. That means you do not have to work up the right feelings. You can ask Him to show you.

## Fear or trust

A slave relates to his master through fear and performance. He works so he will not be punished. He watches the master's face to see what kind of day it is going to be.

A son relates to his father through trust. He still obeys. He still works. But he does it from a place of belonging, not from fear of being thrown out.

"There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear, because fear involves torment" (1 John 4:18).

Here is why this matters for your reset. A lot of the habits you are fighting are ways of handling fear and shame. When you feel like a slave who failed again, you hide. Hiding is where the old habits grow. When you know you are a son, you can come into the light, tell the truth and get back up.

## When "father" is a hard word

Let's be honest. For some men, the word "father" hurts. Maybe your dad was absent. Maybe he was harsh, or drunk, or just never really there even when he was in the room. Maybe you never heard him say he was proud of you.

If that is you, it makes sense that "Father" does not feel warm. But your heavenly Father is not a bigger version of your earthly dad. He is the heavenly Father your dad was supposed to show you and could not.

"When my father and my mother forsake me, then the Lord will take care of me" (Psalm 27:10).

One thing that helps a lot is forgiving your earthly parents. Forgiving does not mean saying what they did was fine. It means you hand the debt over to God and stop carrying it. That breaks the pattern, so their failures do not keep shaping how you see God, or how you treat your own kids one day.

## The lie and the truth

Here are some common thoughts that keep men at a distance from God, and what He says back.

| The lie | The truth |
| --- | --- |
| "God is mostly disappointed in me." | You received the Spirit of adoption, not the spirit of bondage to fear. (Romans 8:15) |
| "I can only call Him Father when I am doing well." | Jesus cried "Abba, Father" on His hardest night. (Mark 14:36) |
| "I have to feel close to God before I belong." | The Spirit bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God. (Romans 8:16) |
| "God is like my earthly father." | He who has seen Jesus has seen the Father. (John 14:9) |
| "My parents left me, so I am on my own." | When my father and my mother forsake me, then the Lord will take care of me. (Psalm 27:10) |

## Parenting moments

Here is something simple you can use every day. Call them parenting moments.

When you are stressed, triggered, ashamed or hurt, stop for a few seconds and pray: "Abba, come and be my heavenly Father in this moment."

That is the whole prayer. Short prayers count. You do not need the right words or a quiet room. A son in trouble just says "Dad" and his father turns around.

You can use it in the car after a hard meeting. At night when the urge hits. After you snap at someone you love. In the moment you want to hide.
