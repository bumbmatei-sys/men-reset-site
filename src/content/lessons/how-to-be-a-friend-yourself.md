In the last lesson you started looking for one friend. Here is the other half, and it is the half you control. The surest way to have a friend is to be one.

## What is a true Christian friend?

Jesus answered that better than anyone. "Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one's life for his friends. You are My friends if you do whatever I command you. No longer do I call you servants, for a servant does not know what his master is doing; but I have called you friends" (John 15:13-15).

He called them friends, told them what was on His heart, and then laid down His life for them. A true Christian friend is a man who loves the way Jesus loved, in small daily ways long before any big moment.

Solomon put it plainly. "A friend loves at all times, And a brother is born for adversity" (Proverbs 17:17). At all times. Not only when it is convenient or fun. And especially in the bad weeks.

Here is something freeing. You do not have to wait until you feel less lonely to start being a friend. You can be one today, to someone who may be just as alone as you.

## Show up

Most of friendship is simply being there. The coffee you said you would come to. The text the day after his surgery. The funeral you drive two hours to attend.

When Job lost everything, three friends came. "So they sat down with him on the ground seven days and seven nights, and no one spoke a word to him, for they saw that his grief was very great" (Job 2:13). Later they said a lot of wrong things. But that first week, when they just sat with him, was the best thing they did.

You do not need the right words. You need to be there.

## Listen without fixing

Many men listen for about ten seconds and then start fixing. "Here is what you should do." It feels helpful. To the other man, it often feels like being handled.

James wrote, "So then, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath" (James 1:19). Swift to hear. Ask one more question before you say anything. "How are you holding up with that?" "What was the hardest part?"

Then remember what he told you, and ask about it next time. That one habit tells a man he matters more than almost anything else you could say.

## Keep a confidence

If he tells you something private, it stays with you. Not with your wife "just so she can pray." Not as a prayer request at small group. With you.

"A talebearer reveals secrets, But he who is of a faithful spirit conceals a matter" (Proverbs 11:13). A man who repeats one secret will not be trusted with a second.

There is one exception. If he tells you he plans to hurt himself or someone else, or that a child is being harmed, you do not keep that quiet. You get help right away, and you get emergency help if it is urgent. Love sometimes means telling him you cannot keep this one.

## Say the good you see

Most men go years without anyone telling them something good about themselves. You can change that for someone.

Paul wrote, "Therefore comfort each other and edify one another, just as you also are doing" (1 Thessalonians 5:11). To edify is to build up. Notice what is good in a man and tell him in plain words. "You were patient with your son today. I noticed." "You always make new guys feel welcome." It costs nothing, and many men remember those sentences for years.

Pray for him, too. Pray for the men you are building a friendship with by name. You will find it hard to stay distant from a man you pray for every week.

## Tell him the truth

A real friend is not only nice. "Faithful are the wounds of a friend, But the kisses of an enemy are deceitful" (Proverbs 27:6). If you see him drifting, from his wife, from God, from church, say something. Gently, in private, after you have earned the right by showing up. That is love too.

## How do you maintain Christian friendships?

Make the next call. Do not keep score of who reached out last. Many friendships die not because of a fight, but because both men waited for the other one to text.

Put him in your calendar if you have to. A text every week or two. A meal once a month. A call on his birthday. Friendship is kept the same way it was built, with small, repeated time.

| What a friend does | What it looks like this week | Done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shows up | Go to the thing you said you would, or check in after a hard day | ☐ |
| Listens | Ask one more question before giving advice | ☐ |
| Remembers | Follow up on something he told you | ☐ |
| Keeps a confidence | What he shared stays with you | ☐ |
| Builds up | Tell him one good thing you see in him | ☐ |
| Prays | Pray for him by name | ☐ |
