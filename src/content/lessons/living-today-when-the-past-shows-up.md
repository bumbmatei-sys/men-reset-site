This is the last lesson in the course. If you skipped ahead, start at [You Are Not What Happened to You](/tracks/overcoming-trauma/you-are-not-what-happened-to-you).

Many people expect healing to feel like a finish line. One day you will wake up, and it will all be gone. When that does not happen, they feel like the whole thing failed.

But for most people, healing looks much more ordinary than that. It looks like a Tuesday where you got through a trigger faster than last month. A night you slept. A conversation where you did not shut down. A prayer that felt a little more honest. This lesson is about living those ordinary days well, even when the past still shows up.

## How do you live with trauma?

One day at a time. That is not a slogan. It is what Jesus told us to do.

"Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about its own things. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble" (Matthew 6:34).

When you are carrying something heavy, it is easy to look at the whole road ahead. "Will I feel like this for the rest of my life? Will I ever be normal?" Those questions are too big for one morning. Today has enough in it. Your job is just today.

So each morning, ask one question: what is one next step I can take today? Not the whole healing. One step. Call the counselor. Go to bed on time. Eat a real meal. Tell a friend the truth. Read one psalm. Take a walk.

Small steps, taken on ordinary days, add up.

## Does trauma stay with you forever?

The memory of what happened will probably stay with you in some form. You are not going to wake up one day with the past erased. But the power it has over today can change, and for many people it does, especially with good help, safe people, and time.

Over months and years, many people find that they react less often, recover faster, sleep better, and feel more present with the people they love. The memory becomes something that happened to them, rather than something that is still happening.

You may still have hard days, anniversaries, and moments that catch you off guard. That does not mean you are back at the start. It means you are a person who went through something real.

## What healing can look like

It may help to know what to look for, so you notice progress when it comes:

- You catch a trigger sooner, and use your plan.
- You come back to yourself faster after a hard moment.
- You can talk about it a little without shutting down.
- You start to believe, at least on some days, what your heavenly Father says about you.
- You let one or two people know the real you.
- You have more good hours than you used to.

None of these needs to be perfect. If you see even one of them, that is God at work.

## Jesus kept His scars

After Jesus rose from the dead, His body was glorified and whole. And yet He still had the marks of the nails. He said to Thomas, "Reach your finger here, and look at My hands; and reach your hand here, and put it into My side" (John 20:27).

The scars were still there. But they no longer meant defeat. They meant victory and love.

Healing does not always erase the mark. Sometimes it changes what the mark means. Your story does not end with what was done to you. It goes on, and it can become part of how God comforts others. "The Father of mercies and God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any trouble" (2 Corinthians 1:3-4). You do not have to rush toward that. But it is there, ahead.

## Fruitful in the land of your affliction

Joseph went through years of betrayal, slavery, false accusation, and prison. Later, when he had children, he named his second son Ephraim, saying, "For God has caused me to be fruitful in the land of my affliction" (Genesis 41:52).

Notice: not after he left the land of his affliction. In it. God did not wait until everything was fixed to make Joseph's life good again. He made it fruitful right there.

You may still be living near the place where you were hurt, in your memory, your family, or your body. God can make your life fruitful there too, one ordinary day at a time.

## When a hard day comes

Some days will still be hard. On those days, go back to the tools from this course:

- Read your identity table from lesson 1.
- Use your "when it hits" plan from lesson 2.
- Write a short lament from lesson 3.
- Remember the distinctions table from lesson 4 if anger or pressure to forgive is rising.
- Reach out to someone on your support plan from lesson 5.

If a day gets dark, and you feel unsafe or think about ending your life, reach out right away, any time. Tell someone near you and get help. You are not a burden. Help exists for exactly that moment.

## What this course was about

This course was never about getting over it quickly. It was about walking with your heavenly Father through what happened, at a pace you can handle, with people beside you.

You learned that what happened is not your name. That your body's reactions are not sin and can be met with a plan. That you can bring the rawest pain to God. That forgiveness does not mean pretending or going back to danger. That you need safe people and a safe pace. And that healing is mostly made of ordinary days.

If you want to keep going, [Know Who You Are](/tracks/know-who-you-are) goes deeper into your identity as a son of God, and [Renew Your Mind](/tracks/renew-your-mind) helps with the thoughts that keep coming back.
