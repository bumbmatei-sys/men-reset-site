In the last lesson you learned to leave the room when you close the game and to give your hands one real thing to do. If you have not practiced that yet, go back to [The First Ten Minutes After You Close the Game](/tracks/quit-gaming-and-the-phone/the-first-ten-minutes-after-you-close-the-game).

Now we deal with the phone. For a lot of people, the phone is where the night really goes. The console gets shut off at a decent time, and then the phone keeps going until 1 a.m. Or the phone is the console, and the games live in your pocket.

## Why willpower fails at 11 p.m.

By late evening you are tired. You have made hundreds of decisions since you woke up. Your mind is slower, and your guard is down.

That is exactly when the phone is closest. In your hand, on the pillow, glowing in the dark. Every app on it was built by people who are very good at keeping you there.

So we stop asking the tired version of you to win that fight. We set things up earlier, in daylight, when you are clear. Then at 11 p.m. there is less to fight.

## Make no provision

Paul wrote, "But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to fulfill its lusts" (Romans 13:14).

A provision is something you set aside ahead of time, like food packed for a trip. Paul is saying, do not pack for the old way. Do not leave it snacks.

A phone in bed with games on the first screen and no limits is a packed lunch for the habit. This lesson is about unpacking it.

## How do I stop using my phone at night?

You change four things. Do them today if you can.

**1. Take the games off the phone.** If you game on your phone, delete those games, or at least move them off the home screen into a folder on the last page. If deleting feels like too much right now, sign out of them. Every extra step between you and the game helps.

**2. Turn on a downtime schedule.** Most phones already have this. On an iPhone it is Screen Time, with a setting called Downtime ([Apple's guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-up-screen-time-for-yourself-iphbfa595995/ios)). On Android it is usually Digital Wellbeing, with Bedtime mode and app timers ([Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9346420)). Set it to start at your phone curfew, the time the phone is done for the night. Allow calls and the few apps you truly need. Block the rest.

**3. Give the phone a place to sleep.** Pick one charging spot outside the bedroom. The kitchen counter. A shelf in the hallway. Put the charger there and leave it there. At curfew, the phone goes there.

**4. Turn off the night pulls.** Go through your notifications and turn off anything that is not from a real person. Game alerts, store alerts, social media likes, streaming suggestions. Those are the taps on the shoulder that start a late session.

## What time should you stop using your phone at night?

Set your phone curfew before your gaming stop, or at the same time. If your hard stop from lesson 2 is 9:45, your phone curfew might be 10:00.

Many sleep guides suggest putting screens away about an hour before bed. Count back from when you want to be asleep. But the real point is the same as with the game: the time is decided ahead, written down, and kept. Not chosen at 11:30 when you are already in bed.

## What if I keep overriding the limit?

Most downtime settings can be skipped with one tap. If you find yourself tapping "ignore limit" every night, add a passcode. Then ask your spouse, a parent, or a trusted friend to set the code so you do not know it.

That can feel embarrassing. It is not. It is wise. You are not asking them to police you. You are asking them to help you keep a promise you already made. If you want more structure than that, [Accountability Apps Compared](/teachings/accountability-apps-compared-2026) walks through the options.

## The bedroom rule for the evening

Here is the simplest rule in this whole course. After curfew, the phone does not come into the bedroom.

Not to check the weather. Not "just to set the alarm." If you need an alarm, use a basic alarm clock, or leave the phone outside the bedroom door where you can still hear it.

The bedroom is for sleep, rest and the people you share your home with. When the phone stays out, the room goes back to being what it was meant to be.

If you share a room or live in a small place, do your best version. The phone charges on the far side of the room, face down, with downtime on. The point is distance and a decision made ahead.

## A setup is not a cage

Some people hear all this and feel trapped. Like the phone is now a punishment.

It is the opposite. You are making room. Every evening the phone does not take is an evening you get to give to sleep, to people, and to your heavenly Father. David wrote, "I will both lie down in peace, and sleep" (Psalm 4:8). Peace at bedtime is a gift He gives. A quiet room helps you receive it.
