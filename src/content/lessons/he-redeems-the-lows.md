Job lost his children, his money and his health in a short time. His friends sat with him and then told him it was his fault. And in the middle of all that, sitting in ashes, he said this: "I know that my Redeemer lives."

He didn't say, "I know why this happened." He didn't have that. What he had was a name for God. So let's look at what that name actually means.

## What redeem actually means

Think of a gift voucher. On its own it's useless. Just paper. But when you take it to the shop and redeem it, you walk out with something real. Same with empty bottles. In a lot of places you bring them back and get coins for them.

Some bottles go one step further. They don't only get their value back. They get washed and filled again.

That's what redeem means. God puts value back into something you thought was spent. The years you think you lost, the relationship you think you ruined, the body or the mind you think you damaged. You look at it and see an empty bottle. He looks at it and sees something He can fill again.

## A redeemer was family

In the Old Testament the word for redeemer is goel. It wasn't a religious title. It was a job inside a family. If you got into debt and had to sell your land, or even sell yourself as a worker, your closest relative had the right to pay the price and buy you back (Leviticus 25:25, 47-49).

So the redeemer wasn't a stranger doing charity. He was someone related to you, who chose to pay what you couldn't.

Now think about that when Job says "my Redeemer". He is saying God is close enough to him to count as family. And that God is willing to pay.

## Naomi came home empty

The book of Ruth shows this better than any definition. Naomi left Bethlehem with her husband and two sons during a famine. In Moab her husband died. Then both her sons died. She came back with nothing except a foreign daughter-in-law, Ruth.

When the women in town saw her, she said, "Don't call me Naomi. Call me Mara," which means bitter. "I went out full, and the Lord has brought me back empty" (Ruth 1:20-21).

That's honest. And God didn't argue with her. He just kept working.

Ruth goes to collect leftover grain in a field. The field happens to belong to Boaz, a relative of Naomi's family. Boaz steps in as their goel. He buys back the family land and marries Ruth. They have a son, Obed. And the same women who heard Naomi say "empty" now put that baby in her arms and say, "He will restore your life" (Ruth 4:15).

Obed became the grandfather of King David. Which means the woman who called herself bitter ended up in the family line of Jesus.

Notice something. Naomi's redemption didn't start when she felt better. It started while she was still calling herself empty.

## There is no place too low

Maybe your low isn't a famine. Maybe it's an addiction, a divorce, a secret you've carried for years, or a season where you just stopped believing. It can feel like you went too far down for God to bother.

David wrote, "If I make my bed in Sheol, behold, You are there" (Psalm 139:8). Sheol was the lowest place anyone could imagine. Even there.

And Jesus didn't only reach down. He went down. Paul says He "descended into the lower parts of the earth" (Ephesians 4:9). He took the cross, the shame and death itself. So whatever your lowest place is, Jesus has already been lower. He's not shouting instructions from the top of the pit. He's the one who climbed in.

Peter says you were redeemed "not with perishable things like silver or gold, but with precious blood, as of a lamb unblemished" (1 Peter 1:18-19). That's the price your Redeemer paid. He doesn't buy you back to keep you on a shelf.

If you still feel dirty about your past, read [shame vs guilt](/teachings/shame-vs-guilt-jesus-2026). Shame says the bottle is broken. Jesus says it's been bought back.

## Filled again

Here is the part people skip. Redemption isn't only about the past being paid for. It's about you being filled again.

Through the prophet Joel, God told a people who had lost everything to locusts, "I will make up to you for the years that the swarming locust has eaten" (Joel 2:25). Not just forgive the years. Make up for them.

So you don't have to live like a cleaned-out bottle sitting in the corner. The Holy Spirit fills what Jesus bought back. Your identity isn't "the one who lost those years". It's [a son or daughter of God](/teachings/identity-in-christ-2026), and that changes what you're carrying now.

If your low is still happening right now, start with [Break Free](/tracks/break-free). You don't have to climb out first. Your Redeemer is already on His way down to you.
