Two men can get angry at the same thing. A child spills milk at dinner. One man sighs and grabs a towel. The other explodes. The milk is the same. What is underneath is different.

If you only work on the explosion, you will spend your life holding a lid on a pot that keeps boiling. This lesson is about turning down the heat.

## Where we are in this course

You have named your anger, learned your early signs, built a moment plan, and learned how to repair. Those are the tools for the moment. Now we look at the source. Why does the same anger keep coming back?

## The question God asked Cain

The first time anger shows up in the Bible, God asks a question. Cain is furious that his offering was not accepted. "So the LORD said to Cain, 'Why are you angry? And why has your countenance fallen?'" (Genesis 4:6).

God already knew the answer. He was inviting Cain to look underneath. Then He warned him: "sin lies at the door. And its desire is for you, but you should rule over it" (Genesis 4:7). Cain did not look underneath. He let the anger grow, and it ended in his brother's death.

The same question is for you. Not "How do I stop?" first, but "Why am I angry?"

## What is the root of anger?

Anger is often a second feeling. It covers a first feeling that is harder to sit with. Here are the most common ones for men.

- **Fear.** About money, a job, your health, your kids. Fear feels weak. Anger feels strong. So fear comes out as anger.
- **Shame.** Something touched a place where you already feel like a failure. A comment about money, your work, how you parent.
- **Hurt.** She said something that stung, or you felt left out. Anger shows up so you do not have to feel the hurt.
- **Pride.** You were corrected, interrupted, or embarrassed, especially in front of others.
- **Exhaustion.** Too little sleep, too much work, no rest. Your patience is gone before anyone does anything.
- **Feeling unheard or disrespected.** You have said it before and nothing changed.
- **Unmet expectations.** You expected a quiet evening, a clean kitchen, a thank you. The gap between what you expected and what happened fills with anger.

None of these make your outburst okay. But naming them gives you something real to bring to God and to talk about at home.

## Is pride the root of anger?

Often, yes, though not always. Pride says, "I should not have to put up with this." It is behind a lot of anger about being inconvenienced or corrected. But sometimes the root is fear or real hurt, and calling everything pride misses it. Be honest about which one it actually is.

## Sin and the root

"If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). There are two parts in that verse. Forgiveness for the sins, and cleansing of what is underneath.

An anger problem needs both. You repent of the outburst, the shouting and the cutting words. And you let your heavenly Father deal with the anger underneath, the fear or shame or wound that keeps feeding it. If you only repent of the outburst, the root is still there tomorrow.

## The offense you keep replaying

Much long-term anger starts with an offense that comes into your thoughts and gets replayed. What she said last month. What your boss did. What your father never said. Every replay makes it heavier.

King Saul threw a spear at David, "for he said, 'I will pin David to the wall!'" (1 Samuel 18:11). David escaped. And David never threw a spear back, even when he had the chance later. The danger was not only the spear in Saul's hand. It was whether a spear would grow in David's heart.

"Looking carefully lest anyone fall short of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up cause trouble, and by this many become defiled" (Hebrews 12:15). A root of bitterness in a father does not stay in the father. It spreads to the whole house. If you notice a thought you keep replaying, the course [Renew Your Mind](/tracks/renew-your-mind) can help you handle it.

## Take it to your heavenly Father and a brother

The root needs somewhere to go. If it does not go up to God or out to a brother, it goes out on your home.

Take it to your heavenly Father. "Search me, O God, and know my heart; Try me, and know my anxieties; And see if there is any wicked way in me, And lead me in the way everlasting" (Psalm 139:23-24). Notice the verse names anxieties. God expects fear to be part of it. "Casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you" (1 Peter 5:7).

Take it to a brother. Tell one trusted man, "I've noticed I get angry when I'm afraid about money," or "I think I'm angry because I feel like a failure at work." Saying it out loud takes some of its power away.

Then, when you are calm, you can say the real thing at home, without the anger: "I've been scared about money. I'm sorry it came out at you."

## When to get more help

Sometimes what is under the anger is heavy. Grief, trauma, depression, or something from childhood. A licensed Christian counselor can help you look at it safely. That is wisdom, not weak faith. If what you find leaves you feeling very low or unsafe, call or text **988**, any time. If your anger has led you to hurt, threaten, or frighten anyone, get help today: call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at **1-800-799-7233**, or **911** if anyone is in danger, and do not ask anyone to keep it quiet.
