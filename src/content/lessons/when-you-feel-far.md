You did the thing again. The one you promised you were done with. And now there is a heavy quietness where God used to feel close.

Maybe you are asking if He is finished with you. If there is a limit, and you just hit it.

Let's look at a man who failed about as badly as anyone could, and see what Jesus did with him.

## Peter's worst night

Peter was the bold one. At the Last Supper he told Jesus, "Even if I have to die with You, I will not deny You" (Matthew 26:35). He meant it.

Hours later, Jesus was arrested. Peter followed at a distance into the high priest's courtyard. It was cold, and he stood warming himself by a charcoal fire (John 18:18).

Then a servant girl asked if he was one of Jesus' followers. He said no. Someone else asked. No again. A third person pressed him, and Peter swore he did not know the man. Then the rooster crowed. Luke says Jesus turned and looked at him, and Peter went out and wept bitterly (Luke 22:61-62).

That is about as low as it gets. Not a small slip. Three times, he denied the One he loved, right when Jesus needed him.

## Jesus went looking for him

After the resurrection, Peter went back to fishing. That is what we do when we think we have disqualified ourselves. We go back to the old life.

And Jesus showed up on the shore. He had breakfast ready. And John made sure to tell us what it was cooked on: a fire of coals (John 21:9).

Think about that. The last time Peter stood by a charcoal fire, he denied Jesus. Now Jesus lit another one. Not to shame him. To heal the place where it broke.

## Three questions

After breakfast, Jesus asked Peter, "Do you love Me?" Three times. One question for every denial.

Notice what Jesus did not say. He did not say, "How could you?" He did not list what Peter did. He did not put him on probation for a year. He asked about love.

And after each answer, Jesus gave him a job. "Feed My lambs." "Tend My sheep." "Feed My sheep." The man who thought he was finished was handed the care of the church that same morning.

Restoration did not take months. It happened right there on the beach. Weeks later, Peter stood up in Jerusalem and preached, and thousands came to Jesus (Acts 2).

## No condemnation, only invitation

Here is the difference you need to learn to hear.

Conviction is the Holy Spirit showing you something is wrong so you can come home. It is specific, it points to Jesus, and it leaves hope. Condemnation is a voice that says you are the problem, you will never change, and you should stay away. It is vague, it points at you, and it leaves you hiding.

Romans 8:1 says there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. So if the voice in your head is pushing you away from God, it is not His. He convicts to draw you closer. He never condemns to drive you off.

If this is where you get stuck, read [Shame vs Guilt](/teachings/shame-vs-guilt-jesus-2026). Guilt says, "I did wrong." Shame says, "I am wrong." Jesus answers both.

## He does not leave

Psalm 27:10 says, "When my father and my mother forsake me, then the Lord will take care of me." Some of us were left by people who should have stayed. So we expect God to leave too, especially after we fail.

He doesn't. Your sin did not surprise Him. Jesus told Peter ahead of time that he would deny Him, and in the same breath said, "I have prayed for you, that your faith should not fail" (Luke 22:32). He knew, and He had already planned the way back.

He knows about your fall too. He has already prayed for you.

## Run toward Him

When we fail, most of us try to clean ourselves up first. We wait a few days. We try to feel sorry enough. We do some good things to balance it out. Then we come back.

But that is just another blockage. It keeps you far away at the exact moment you need Him most.

Look at Peter in John 21. When John said, "It is the Lord," Peter did not wait for the boat. He jumped in the water and swam to Jesus (John 21:7). That is the posture. Not waiting until you feel clean, but running towards the One who makes you clean.

1 John 1:9 says if we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us and cleanse us. Confession is not groveling. It is just agreeing with God about what happened and letting Him deal with it.

## Do this today

The next time you fail this week, do not wait. Within the hour, tell God plainly what you did. No speech. Just the truth.

Then read John 21:15-17 and hear Jesus ask you, "Do you love Me?" Answer Him honestly. Then get up and do the next good thing in front of you, because He is not done with you.

## Reflect

After you fail, what do you usually do first: hide, punish yourself, or run back to Him? What would it look like to run back faster?
