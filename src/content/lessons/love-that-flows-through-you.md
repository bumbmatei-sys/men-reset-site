Most advice on loving like Jesus sounds like a to-do list. Be kinder. Be more patient. Serve more. Forgive faster. All true. But if you have tried that, you know how it goes. You last a few days, then someone gets on your nerves and it all falls apart.

There is a reason. You cannot give away what you have not received.

## First receive, then give

1 John 4:11 says, "Beloved, if God so loved us, we also ought to love one another." Notice the order. God loves us first. Then we love others. The word "so" matters. Our love is supposed to be a response to His, not a replacement for it.

Think of the garden hose one last time. Through this course we have been clearing the blockages so His love can reach you. But a hose is not meant to hold water. It is meant to carry it somewhere. When His love flows freely into you, it starts flowing out of you.

That is how Jesus loved. He said, "As the Father loved Me, I also have loved you" (John 15:9). He received from the Father, then gave it away. You get to live the same way.

## Agape: love decided by the giver

In [lesson one](/tracks/know-gods-love/loved-before-you-do-anything) we met the Greek word agape. It is love that comes from the one giving it, not from how lovable the other person is.

Most human love works the other way. I love you if you are kind to me, attractive to me, useful to me. When you stop being those things, the love dries up.

Agape doesn't depend on the other person. It is a decision rooted in what God has already given you. That is why Jesus could love the people who were nailing Him to a cross. His love was not coming from them. It was coming from the Father.

## Love yourself with His love

Jesus said to love your neighbor as yourself (Matthew 22:39). That assumes something. If you despise yourself, you will have a hard time loving anyone else well.

This is not about self-esteem or telling yourself you're amazing. It is about agreeing with God. If He calls you loved, forgiven, and His, you do not get to overrule Him. Treating yourself with the patience He shows you is not selfish. It is honest.

People who are harsh with themselves are usually harsh with others too. People who have received grace find it easier to give.

## What blocks love in us

1 Corinthians 13 is read at a lot of weddings, but it is really a mirror. Paul says love is patient and kind. It does not envy, does not brag, is not proud, is not rude, does not seek its own, is not easily provoked, and keeps no record of wrongs (1 Corinthians 13:4-5).

Read that slowly and you will find your own blockages. Maybe it is impatience with your kids. Envy when a friend gets ahead. A short fuse in traffic. A mental list of everything your spouse or coworker has done wrong.

Here is the good news. You do not fix those by gritting your teeth. You bring them to God, let Him love you right there, and ask Him to love that person through you. Over time, the obstacles shrink because the love inside grows.

## Love without a hook

A lot of what we call love has a hook in it. I'll help you, but I expect you to notice. I'll forgive you, but I'll remind you later. I'll be generous, but you owe me.

People can feel the hook, even if they cannot name it. And it turns love into a trade.

Jesus loved without a hook. He washed Judas' feet knowing what Judas was about to do (John 13:5). He healed ten lepers and only one came back to say thanks (Luke 17:17). He kept giving anyway.

You can only love like that when your own tank is full. If you need people to love you back to feel okay, you will always attach strings. If you are already loved by God, you can let people go, let them fail you, and keep wanting good for them.

## Who have you stopped wanting good for?

Here is a hard question. Is there someone you have quietly stopped wanting good things for?

Not someone you hate. Just someone you have written off. An ex. A parent. A brother who hurt you. A boss. You would not wish them harm, but you would not mind much if things went badly for them either.

That is usually where love has stopped flowing. And it is often where God wants to start. Forgiving does not mean pretending it did not hurt or letting someone keep hurting you. It means handing the debt to God and choosing to want good for them again.

## The end of this course, the start of a life

Over these six lessons we have walked a simple path. God loved you before you did anything. His heart toward you has never changed. Some things in you were blocking that love, and you can let Him clear them. You are the one Jesus loves. When you fail, He comes looking for you with an invitation, not a verdict.

And now that love has somewhere to go.

You do not have to perform for it anymore. You do not have to earn it or protect it. You get to live from it, and let it spill over to the people around you. That is what it means to love like Jesus.

If you want to keep growing in who you are in Him, [Identity in Christ](/teachings/identity-in-christ-2026) is a good next step.

## Do this today

Ask God, "Who have I stopped wanting good for?" Write down the first name that comes, even if you don't like the answer.

This week, pray for good things for that person by name every day. Then do one kind thing for them that they cannot repay. Do it quietly, with no hook.

## Reflect

Where does your love come with a hook, and what would it look like to give it freely this week?
