**A good Christian morning routine for a man with a job is about 20 minutes with God before the phone, built on the Word, prayer, and one step of obedience for the day.** If the morning goes badly, there is a 5-minute version below, because a short routine you actually keep beats a long one you drop by Thursday.

You know the picture. The alarm goes off and your hand finds the phone before your feet find the floor. Inbox, messages, the news, somebody else's emergency. By the time you are out the door you are already behind, and God got whatever was left over, which was usually nothing. This is about a simpler way, one that fits before a real job, an early shift, a long commute, and kids who are already awake.

## What should a Christian morning routine look like?

There is a saying I keep coming back to: "If the first button on a shirt is wrong, every button after it is wrong." In plain words, the first thing of the day sets the direction for everything that follows it. If Jesus is the first button, the rest of the day has a chance to sit right. If the phone is the first button, you spend the day chasing whatever the screen told you to chase.

So the simple answer is this: before the phone, the Word, prayer, and one step of obedience. Not a productivity system. Not a checklist that makes you feel spiritual. Jesus Himself, first.

C.S. Lewis put it this way in a [1951 letter](https://www.cslewisinstitute.org/resources/reflections-july-2017/): "Put first things first and we get second things thrown in: put second things first and we lose both first and second things." In plain words, when God is first, your work, your family, and your focus tend to come along. When you put them first and push Him to the leftovers, you end up losing both the closeness and the performance you were chasing.

> **[Matthew 6:33-34 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+6:33-34&version=NKJV)** "But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you. Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about its own things. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble."

## Why pray in the morning?

Because the morning is when you need Him most and when the day is still quiet enough to hear Him. Scripture is not shy about this.

> **[Psalm 5:3 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+5:3&version=NKJV)** "My voice You shall hear in the morning, O LORD; In the morning I will direct it to You, And I will look up."

> **[Psalm 143:8 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+143:8&version=NKJV)** "Cause me to hear Your lovingkindness in the morning, For in You do I trust; Cause me to know the way in which I should walk, For I lift up my soul to You."

Jesus did the same, and He did it before daylight, in the middle of a busy season of ministry.

> **[Mark 1:35 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+1:35&version=NKJV)** "Now in the morning, having risen a long while before daylight, He went out and departed to a solitary place; and there He prayed."

Notice what this is not. It is not a rule about a clock. Jesus is not telling you that 5 a.m. makes you holy. The point is closeness, meeting with your heavenly Father before the day pulls on you from ten directions. If your morning starts at 4:30 a.m. because of a shift, then your morning prayer happens at 4:30 a.m. If your morning starts at 8, it happens at 8. God is not watching the hour. He is looking for you.

And here is the mercy that holds the whole thing together.

> **[Lamentations 3:22-23 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Lamentations+3:22-23&version=NKJV)** "Through the LORD's mercies we are not consumed, Because His compassions fail not. They are new every morning; Great is Your faithfulness."

His mercies are new every morning. That means you get today's portion today. Worrying about tomorrow is trying to carry tomorrow without tomorrow's mercy. The morning is for receiving what you need for these 24 hours, not for solving the whole week before breakfast.

## How long should a morning devotion be?

Honest answer: 20 minutes kept is better than an hour you abandon by Thursday. The goal is not a number on the clock. The goal is staying in the presence of God long enough that your heart settles and your mind remembers who you are.

Think about the lily.

> **[Matthew 6:28 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+6:28&version=NKJV)** "So why do you worry about clothing? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow: they neither toil nor spin"

A lily does not toil to bloom. It stays in the sun, takes the water and the soil God gives, and the beauty grows from the inside out. A morning with God is not you working hard to grow. It is you standing in the sun. You open the Word. You speak to Him. He does the growing.

One more thing about lilies. They grow in clusters, not alone. If you can, do this with your spouse, a brother or sister in Christ, or a friend from church once a week. Dependence on God is not isolation from His people.

## The 20-minute routine for men with a job

This is the version I would hand to any working man or woman, whether you have followed Jesus for years or started last week. Twenty minutes. Simple, repeatable, and finished before the day has a chance to argue with you.

| Minutes | What you do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Feet on the floor. Phone stays in the other room. One sentence to your heavenly Father. | The first voice of the day should be His, not the inbox. You start as a son or daughter, not an employee. |
| 2 to 10 | Read one short passage out loud (a Psalm, a Proverb, or the Gospel you are in). Underline one verse. | Faith comes by hearing. One verse that lands is worth more than five chapters you skim. |
| 10 to 15 | Pray. Thank Him. Confess. Ask for your family, your work, and the people you will see today. | The day goes where you aim it. Prayer aims it at people, not tasks. |
| 15 to 18 | Speak who you are in Christ, two or three verses out loud. | The world will tell you what you are all day. Say the truth first, before it does. |
| 18 to 20 | Write one step of obedience for today, and the verse beside it, on a card or in your notes. | Obedience is how the Word becomes your life, not just your reading. |

If the identity part feels unfamiliar, start with a short list from [Identity in Christ](/teachings/identity-in-christ-2026) and read the same two or three verses for a week until they are yours. The step of obedience can be small. Call your father. Speak kindly to a coworker. Do the task you have been avoiding.

> **[Colossians 3:23 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Colossians+3:23&version=NKJV)** "And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men."

**Set it up the night before:**

- Phone charges outside the bedroom, in the kitchen or hallway.
- A real alarm clock on the nightstand, so the phone is not your excuse.
- Bible open on the table to the passage you will read.
- Coffee ready to start, or the kettle filled.
- A wake time you can actually hit four days a week, not the perfect one you hit twice.

## What do you do in a morning devotion?

Four movements, and none of them need to be long: thank, surrender, ask, and bless one person.

Thank Him first, for something specific from yesterday or from this morning. Then surrender the day, meaning you tell Him the schedule is His, and ask Him to interrupt it if He wants to. Then ask, for your family, your work, the meeting, the person who is hard to love. Then bring one name before Him, someone you want God to bless today.

A simple morning prayer you can say in your own words:

"Thank You, heavenly Father, for waking me up and for Your mercy that is new this morning. I give this day to You, my work, my family, my schedule, and my mouth. Please forgive me for yesterday, and help me walk clean today. Be with my family, and give me what I need for the job in front of me. Bless \[name\] today and help me be a light to the people I see. I love You. In Jesus' name, amen."

That is not a performance. It is a son talking to his Father. If you want to go deeper in learning to hear Him this way, [Loved Before You Do Anything](/tracks/know-gods-love/loved-before-you-do-anything) is a good place to sit for a few days.

## What if you start work at 6 a.m. or work shifts?

Then you need real versions, not ideal ones. Here are three.

**The 5-minute version.** For the morning you overslept, the baby was up all night, or the day started with an emergency. Read one Psalm out loud. Thank Him for one thing. Confess one thing. Ask for help with the one thing that worries you most today. Write one verse on a card and take it with you. Five honest minutes with God is a real devotion. Do not let a perfect routine be the reason you skip Him entirely.

**The shift worker's version.** Your morning is whenever your day starts. Night shift means the routine happens at 5 p.m. before you leave. Early shift means it happens at 4 a.m., and it can be shorter. God is not confined to sunrise. He is after the first part of whatever you call today. If you work rotating shifts, pick a fixed anchor instead of a fixed hour, like the first ten minutes after you wake up, whatever the clock says.

**The commute version.** Radio off for one stretch of the drive. Listen to one chapter of Scripture, a recording is fine, or read one verse at a red light and think on it the rest of the way. Then pray out loud in the car. Praying out loud keeps your mind from drifting, and nobody else needs to hear it.

> **[Romans 10:17 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+10:17&version=NKJV)** "So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God."

Hearing is part of the design, not a lesser option for busy people.

## What about the phone?

The first input of the day shapes the rest of it. If the first thing your mind receives is bad news, other people's opinions, and requests, you will spend the morning reacting instead of walking. Give God the first input and let the phone wait twenty minutes. It will still be there.

This is not about hating technology. It is about ordering the day so that God is before the screen. If the phone is the habit you cannot break yet, [take every thought captive](/teachings/take-every-thought-captive-2026) and [Discipline and a Morning Routine](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine) will help you build the muscle slowly, without shame.

## What if you miss a morning?

You will. And when you do, here is what is true: you did not fail a test, and God is not standing there with folded arms. His mercies were new this morning, and they are still available at lunch.

> **[Proverbs 24:16 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+24:16&version=NKJV)** "For a righteous man may fall seven times And rise again, But the wicked shall fall by calamity."

The righteous man is not the one who never falls. He is the one who gets back up. So come back the same day, even if it is noon, even if it is five minutes in a parked car. Do not wait for Monday to start over, and do not skip the whole week because one morning went wrong. If you want a gentle structure for that kind of restart, the [30-Day Reset](/tracks/30-day-reset) is built for anyone who is coming back, not for people who never left.

## Where do you start tomorrow?

Start small and start real. Tonight, put the phone to charge in another room and leave your Bible open on the table. Tomorrow morning, sit down before the screen, read one passage out loud, pray for your family and your work, and write one step of obedience on a card.

Remember the lily. You are not toiling to bloom. You are standing in the sun, taking what your Father gives, and letting Him grow the beauty from the inside out.

> **[Acts 17:28 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+17:28&version=NKJV)** "for in Him we live and move and have our being"

There is a saying: "God is our natural habitat." In plain words, you were made to live in Him the way a fish is made to live in water. The morning routine is not a new demand. It is going back to where you belong.
