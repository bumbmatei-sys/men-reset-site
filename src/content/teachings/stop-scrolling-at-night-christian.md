**Stop scrolling at night by giving your phone a bedtime, a place to sleep that is not your bed, and a three-minute handoff where you tell God what you are still holding.** That is the whole reset. You do not need willpower at 11:47 p.m. You need a plan you set up at 3:00 p.m.

Most of us have lost a night to a glowing screen. Not to anything evil. Just to a feed that kept going while we kept sinking. This is for men and women alike. It is not a lecture. It is a practical Christian phone reset for bedtime.

## Why do I scroll at night?

Because the day ended and something in you has not.

You are tired, but your mind is still switched on. The feed gives you a kind of low effort motion. No decisions. No dishes. No one asking anything of you. It feels like rest. It is not rest. It is a slow burn.

There is something deeper too. Late scrolling is often a night watch. You are staying reachable. Not missing anything. Keeping up with the news, the group chat, the comment, the comparison. You have quietly taken a job that belongs to God.

Think of a night watchman holding the keys to a building he did not build. He paces. He checks the gate. He refuses to sleep because someone has to be in control. Your phone lets you play watchman over your own life. God is asking for the keys back.

> **[Psalm 121:3-4 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+121:3-4&version=NKJV)** "He will not allow your foot to be moved; He who keeps you will not slumber. Behold, He who keeps Israel Shall neither slumber nor sleep."

He who keeps you does not get tired. That means you can stop doing His shift. You are allowed to hand over the watch.

## Is scrolling at night bad?

The phone is not evil. It holds people we love, useful tools, maps, messages, the Bible app. I am not here to trash it.

But what happens in bed is different from what happens on the couch. Scrolling keeps your mind switched on when it needs to come down. It feeds comparison when you are too tired to guard your thoughts. It fills the quiet where prayer usually lives. And sometimes it walks you into things that leave you ashamed by morning.

A boundary is a gift, not a punishment. A river without banks is just a swamp. A well has a rim, and that rim is what makes the water usable. The rim around your phone at night is not legalism. It is care.

"Scrolling is not a moral failure. But it is not neutral either." That is my plain take. What you feed at night grows.

## How do I stop scrolling at night?

You change the setup before you change the feeling.

Willpower at bedtime is the worst plan in the world, because the version of you at 11:00 p.m. is the weakest version of you. So you make the decision earlier, in daylight, when you are thinking clearly. This is what self-discipline actually looks like in practice. You can read more of that in [How to Build Self-Discipline as a Christian](/teachings/christian-self-discipline-2026).

Four things do most of the work:

1. Give the phone a bedtime that is earlier than yours.
2. Give the phone a place to sleep that is not your bed.
3. Give your mind something real to hold instead of a feed.
4. Give God what you are still carrying.

That last one is the one most people skip. And it is the one that actually settles you.

## The Night Handoff: a 3-minute routine

This is the tool I teach most often. Three moves. It takes about three minutes. Do it while the phone is charging across the room.

**1. Close.**
Put the phone down. Plug it in across the room, or better, outside the bedroom. Screen down. This is the physical act of closing the watch post.

**2. Hand over.**
Say out loud one thing you are still holding. One. A worry. A message you have not answered. The news. A comparison that stung. A thing you are afraid of tomorrow. Say it to your heavenly Father. Something like: "Father, I am still carrying this. I am handing it to you. You keep me, and you do not sleep."

> **[Psalm 4:8 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+4:8&version=NKJV)** "I will both lie down in peace, and sleep; For You alone, O Lord, make me dwell in safety."

David did not say he talked himself into sleep. He said God makes him dwell in safety. The peace comes from who God is, not from how well you performed today.

**3. Rest.**
Lie down and receive it. Not as a task you earned. As a son or a daughter. This is grace. The Night Handoff is not a rule to earn sleep or to earn God's approval. If you fail one night, nothing about your identity changes. Christ did that work. See [Identity in Christ](/teachings/identity-in-christ-2026) if that is a foggy area for you. Then just start again tonight.

> **[Psalm 127:2 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+127:2&version=NKJV)** "...For so He gives His beloved sleep."

He gives it. You receive it. That is the whole economy.

## What can I do instead of scrolling at night?

Before you pick a replacement, ask what the scroll is really giving you. People say "relaxation" and that is rarely true. Usually it is one of six things: escape, company, noise, comparison, numbness, or the feeling of still being productive.

Ask it plainly: what did I want when I picked this up?

Then choose something that actually meets that need. The answer is almost never "try harder." It is usually a warm shower, a paper book, a voice on the other end of a phone call, or a worry written on paper instead of carried in your chest.

> **[Matthew 11:28 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+11:28&version=NKJV)** "Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

Come to Me. Not come to the feed. The invitation is personal, and it is still open tonight.

## What you are really looking for tonight

| What you are really looking for | What the scroll gives | What to do instead tonight |
|---|---|---|
| Tired and want to switch off | More light and more noise | Warm shower, then a paper book in low light |
| Lonely | Company without being known | Text one friend earlier in the evening, or call someone before 9 p.m. |
| Anxious, or the news | More to carry | Write the worry on paper, then pray Psalm 4 out loud |
| Bored | Motion without meaning | Audio Bible with the screen locked, or a few minutes of stretching |
| Avoiding something or numbing | Numbness | Name it to God in one sentence, then take one small step in the morning |
| A hard day and you want a reward | Cheap payoff | Tea, prayer, bed. Treat rest as the reward |

Print that if you want. Tape it inside a cupboard. The point is to get honest about the real need, then meet it with something that leaves you whole instead of hollow.

## How do I stop doomscrolling at night?

Doomscrolling is scrolling with a specific hunger: you want to feel prepared for the bad thing, so you keep watching for it. The feed has no bottom. You do. That is the saying, and here is the plain meaning: the content will always produce more, but your mind and body have a limit, and honoring that limit is wisdom, not weakness.

Three moves help.

First, decide in the morning what you will read at night, if anything. A chosen source with a stopping point beats an endless feed.

Second, practice taking thoughts captive in real time. When you notice your chest tightening, say the thought out loud to God and ask if it is true and if it is yours to carry tonight. There is a full teaching on this in [Take Every Thought Captive](/teachings/take-every-thought-captive-2026).

Third, if your late scrolling includes porn, that is a different battle and it deserves a different plan. No shame here. Just honesty and tools. Read [How to Quit Porn as a Christian](/teachings/quit-porn-christian-2026) and [Accountability Apps Compared](/teachings/accountability-apps-compared-2026). If last night already went wrong, read [What to Do After a Porn Relapse](/teachings/what-to-do-after-porn-relapse). Bring in another person. That is how the weight actually moves.

## What if my phone is my alarm?

Buy a cheap alarm clock. This is the single most practical purchase in this whole reset. A basic one costs little, has no feed, and wakes you up just fine.

If buying one is not possible tonight, put the phone across the room on loud. Same effect. You have to stand up to turn it off, and standing up ends the trance.

Do not tell yourself the phone has to live by your pillow. It does not. "I need it for the alarm" is usually the last defense of a habit, not a real requirement.

## When should I stop scrolling before bed?

Pick a phone bedtime that is 30 to 60 minutes before your own. It gives your mind time to come down without making the rule so strict that you quit by Thursday.

Choose the same time every night. Consistency does more than intensity. And if you miss it, do not spiral. Get up, plug the phone in across the room, and do the Night Handoff anyway. Grace is for Tuesday nights too.

By the way, the other end of the day matters just as much. A phone-free first 20 minutes in the morning sets the tone that the evening can follow. I wrote about that in [A Christian Morning Routine for Men With a Job](/teachings/christian-morning-routine-men-2026). It is for women too. Start the day with God before you start it with the feed.

## Set it up tonight in 10 minutes

Do this now, not at bedtime. Menu names vary a little by phone.

1. Pick your phone bedtime, 30 to 60 minutes before sleep. Write the time down.
2. Buy a cheap alarm clock, or plan to order one this week.
3. Move the charger across the room, or outside the bedroom.
4. Turn on Sleep Focus on iPhone (Settings, Focus, Sleep) or Bedtime mode on Android (Settings, Digital Wellbeing, Bedtime mode).
5. Log out of the app you normally open in bed. Logging out adds one honest pause.
6. Put a paper book or a Bible where the phone used to sit. Make the good thing the easy thing.
7. Tell one trusted person, someone of the same sex, what you are changing and ask them to check on you in a week.

That is it. Ten minutes today for a different night every night after.
