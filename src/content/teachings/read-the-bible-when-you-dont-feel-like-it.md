**When you don't feel like reading the Bible, first name why, then make the plan smaller than your worst day: one psalm or one short passage, at the same time and place, before you touch your phone. Read it slowly with one question, "What does this show me about my heavenly Father?", write one line, and pray it back. The Word works even when you feel nothing, and the desire usually comes back after the habit, not before it.**

Everybody has days like this. You know you should read. You even want to want to. But the Bible sits there and you scroll instead, or you read three verses and remember nothing.

That does not make you a bad Christian. It makes you a person. The question is what to do about it, and the answer is usually smaller and simpler than you think.

## Why don't I feel like reading my Bible?

Before you fix it, find the real reason. Different reasons need different fixes.

- **I am tired.** You are reading at the end of a long day, or you are not sleeping well.
- **I feel guilty.** You missed a week and now opening it feels like facing a teacher.
- **I do not understand it.** You started Leviticus with good intentions.
- **My plan is too big.** You set a goal on a good day and now every normal day feels like failure.
- **My phone gets there first.** By the time you think of Scripture, you have already read thirty headlines.
- **I feel far from God.** Reading feels pointless because you are not sure He is listening.
- **I am hiding something.** It is hard to sit with God's Word when there is sin you do not want to look at.

Be honest about which one is yours. Usually it is one or two of these.

## Tell your heavenly Father the truth

Start here, whatever the reason. Pray one honest sentence before you open the page.

"Father, I do not feel like reading today. I am tired and my heart is cold. Please meet me anyway."

That is not weak faith. That is relationship. The psalms are full of men telling God exactly how they feel.

> **[Psalm 119:25 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+119:25&version=NKJV)** "My soul clings to the dust; Revive me according to Your word."

Notice the order. The man feels like dust first. Then he asks to be revived by the Word. He does not wait to feel alive before he reads. He reads to become alive.

## Make it smaller than your worst day

Most Bible reading plans are built on your best day. Then a hard week comes and the plan breaks.

Build your plan for your worst day instead. What could you do even when you are sick, tired and discouraged? One psalm. Five verses from Mark. One chapter of Proverbs. That is your minimum. On good days you will read more, and that is a gift. On bad days you still keep the minimum.

A small plan you keep beats a big plan you quit. The [Discipline and a Morning Routine](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine) lesson [A Few Minutes With Scripture](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/a-few-minutes-with-scripture) builds this into a whole morning, if you want the step-by-step.

## Same time, same place, Bible before phone

The less you have to decide, the more likely you are to do it.

- **Pick one time.** For most people, early morning works best, before the day takes over. If mornings are impossible, pick lunch or the train.
- **Pick one place.** The same chair, the same corner of the kitchen table. Leave your Bible there open.
- **Bible before phone.** If the phone comes first, the Bible usually loses. Charge the phone outside your bedroom so the first thing you reach for is not the screen. [The Phone Sleeps in Another Room](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/the-phone-sleeps-in-another-room) shows how.
- **Use paper if a screen tempts you.** A Bible app is fine, but notifications are not.

## Read with one question

A question gives your reading a job. Without one, eyes slide over the words. Keep it simple.

"What does this show me about my heavenly Father?"

That is it. Read the passage slowly. When a line answers that question, stop and write it down in one sentence. You are not trying to master the text. You are trying to meet a Person.

Some days the answer is big. Some days it is small, like "He is patient." Write it down anyway. Over a month, those small lines add up to a picture of who He is.

## What should I read on a dry day?

Not the hardest book in the Bible. On dry days, pick something short and clear.

| If you feel... | Try reading... | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tired and empty | A psalm, such as Psalm 23, 63 or 103 | Short, honest, written by people who felt the same |
| Far from God | Luke 15 | The Father runs to the son who left |
| Guilty | Psalm 32 or 1 John 1 | Confession and forgiveness, plainly |
| Confused | A few verses from Mark | Short, fast, all about Jesus |
| Anxious | Matthew 6:25-34 or Philippians 4 | Words for a worried mind |
| Bored | A chapter of Proverbs | Practical and quick |
| Tempted | James 1 or 1 Corinthians 10 | The path of temptation and the way out |

If you already have a plan, keep going in it. This menu is for the days you have nothing.

## Listen, read out loud, or read with someone

Reading silently at a desk is not the only way.

- **Listen.** An audio Bible on a walk or a commute keeps you in the Word on tired days. For most of history, most believers heard Scripture rather than read it.
- **Read out loud.** It slows you down and keeps your mind from drifting.
- **Read with someone.** A friend, your wife, your accountability partner. Read the same passage and text each other one line. It makes the habit stronger and the reading richer.

## The Word works even when you feel nothing

This may be the most freeing truth for dry days.

> **[Isaiah 55:10-11 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+55:10-11&version=NKJV)** "For as the rain comes down, and the snow from heaven, And do not return there, But water the earth, And make it bring forth and bud, That it may give seed to the sower And bread to the eater, So shall My word be that goes forth from My mouth; It shall not return to Me void, But it shall accomplish what I please, And it shall prosper in the thing for which I sent it."

Rain soaks into the ground, and for a while you see nothing. Then things grow. God's Word works like that in you. On the days you feel nothing, it is still soaking in.

> **[Psalm 1:2-3 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+1:2-3&version=NKJV)** "But his delight is in the law of the Lord, And in His law he meditates day and night. He shall be like a tree Planted by the rivers of water, That brings forth its fruit in its season, Whose leaf also shall not wither; And whatever he does shall prosper."

A tree does not feel itself growing. It just stays planted by the water.

## What if I missed a week?

Start again today. Do not try to catch up. Do not read seven chapters to make up for seven days. Just open to today's passage, or pick up where you stopped, and read your minimum.

> **[Lamentations 3:22-23 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Lamentations+3:22-23&version=NKJV)** "Through the Lord's mercies we are not consumed, Because His compassions fail not. They are new every morning; Great is Your faithfulness."

New every morning. Including this one. Your heavenly Father is not waiting with a record of missed days. He is glad you came.

And if it is hidden sin that keeps you away from the Word, bring that first. [How to Pray When You Keep Falling](/teachings/how-to-pray-when-you-keep-falling) can help you start.

## Open it anyway

You do not have to feel like it. You only have to open it.

Pray one honest sentence. Read one small passage. Ask one question. Write one line. Then go into your day. Tomorrow, do it again.

Over time, many people find what the prophet found: "Your words were found, and I ate them, And Your word was to me the joy and rejoicing of my heart" ([Jeremiah 15:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah+15:16&version=NKJV)). The joy often comes after the eating.
