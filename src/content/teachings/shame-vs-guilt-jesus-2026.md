**Shame and guilt are not the same thing. Guilt says I did something wrong. Shame says something is wrong with me. Jesus dealt with both on the cross, and your shame was carried there so you would not have to carry it anymore.**

Basically, guilt points at an action. Shame points at you as a person. Guilt can lead you back to Jesus. Shame pushes you away from Him, because it tells you that you are the problem, not just what you did. So let's look at the difference and how Jesus took it away.

## What is the difference between shame and guilt?

Guilt says, "I did something wrong." Shame says, "Something is wrong with me." One is about behavior. The other is about identity.

Jesus forgave what you did. He also removed who the enemy says you are. Both were handled at the cross.

| Guilt | Shame |
|---|---|
| Points at an action | Points at your whole person |
| Says "I did something wrong" | Says "something is wrong with me" |
| Can drive you to Jesus | Makes you hide from Jesus |
| Solved by forgiveness | Solved by a new identity |
| Ends at the cross | Ends at the cross too |

Guilt is not always bad. The Holy Spirit convicts to bring you back, not to crush you. But shame never comes from Jesus. It stays after the sin is forgiven, and it keeps talking. It says you are dirty, second class, disqualified. That is a lie about your identity, and the truth makes us uncomfortable because we are comfortable with the lie.

## How does shame show up in a believer's life?

Shame shows up as hiding and performing. You hide because you feel unclean. You perform because you feel you have to earn your place back.

Some common signs:

- Feeling like a second class citizen in the Kingdom, even while seeking God
- Living under a cloud of condemnation you cannot explain
- Believing God is punishing you for something from years ago
- Feeling unclean even when you are not walking in unrepentant sin
- Attributing every hard thing in your life to a past failure
- Addiction loops, where you lose control, feel deep shame, then go back to the same thing

That last one is important. Shame and addiction feed each other. You fail, shame hits, shame makes you feel hopeless, and hopelessness takes you right back. Basically shame steals your sense of control, and then the struggle controls you.

Shame also comes from other places. Things done to you. Abuse. Trauma. What others said about you. Your family name. Your appearance. Accusations that keep battering your mind. None of these define you.

## What does God do with your sin when you come to Jesus?

He forgives it completely and He does not keep a record of it.

> **1 John 1:7 (NASB)** "and the blood of Jesus His Son cleanses us from all sin."

> **Psalm 103:12 (NASB)** "As far as the east is from the west, so far has He removed our wrongdoings from us."

> **Isaiah 44:22 (NASB)** "I have wiped out your wrongdoings like a thick cloud and your sins like a heavy mist. Return to Me, for I have redeemed you."

So here is the foundation. The moment you got saved, you were washed, sanctified, and justified. First Corinthians 6:11 says so. God placed you in the righteous column, not because of what you do, but because of what Jesus did. He addresses you as a holy one from that moment on.

So. If God remembers your sins no more, and shame keeps reminding you of them, who is doing the talking? Not the Father. The devil has as much power as you give him by fixing your eyes on him. Let me be honest with you. It is not that God forgave most of it and kept the file open. He removed it.

## Why did the cross take away your shame?

Because crucifixion was designed to be the most shameful death possible. It was reserved for the worst criminals and rebels. The victim was stripped naked and hung in public. It was total humiliation, total defilement.

Jesus did not choose a clean, quiet way to die. He took the death of shame on purpose.

> **Hebrews 12:2 (NASB)** "who for the joy set before Him endured the cross, despising the shame, and has sat down at the right hand of the throne of God."

Isaiah 53:3 says He was despised and rejected. Philippians 2:8 says He humbled Himself and became obedient to death on a cross. Basically, no matter how low you have gone, no matter how humiliated you have been, Jesus went lower. He carried your shame in the most public way possible so that it would be finished.

This is the gospel and it is good news. Jesus did not only die for what you did. He died for how it made you feel about yourself. He took the condemnation, the filth, the sense of being rejected by God. He took it so you would not live under it.

## Is there any punishment left for you in God's love?

No. There is no punishment in love.

> **1 John 4:18 (NASB)** "There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear, because fear involves punishment, and the one who fears is not perfected in love."

God is love. Love drives out the fear that brings punishment. After my experience with the Lord I can say this plainly. You are not being punished for your past. Jesus was already punished for it.

Does that mean life is always easy? No. Does it mean every hard thing is God teaching you a lesson through pain? No. The Father is not using your suffering to pay you back. He heals because He is the healer. He forgives because He is the forgiver. That is His nature.

Don't misunderstand what I am saying. I am not saying sin does not matter. What I am saying is that your identity is not on the table anymore. Sin is real, repentance is real, and grace is still greater.

## How do you walk free from shame in daily life?

Basically, you walk out of shame the same way you walked into freedom. By believing what Jesus says about you more than what shame says.

- Agree with God about your identity. You are a new creation, a beloved child. Not a nobody.
- When shame speaks, answer with the truth. Say it out loud if you need to. The Word is a mirror, and you see what God says about you in it.
- Stop hiding. Bring the struggle into the light with a trusted brother or sister. Shame dies when it is spoken.
- Stop performing. You do not earn your place back. Fellowship with Jesus is the calling, and everything flows from there.
- Keep your eyes on Jesus, not on the enemy or on yourself. Fixing your eyes on the accuser gives him airtime he does not deserve.
- Repent quickly when needed, then move on. Do not sit in the feeling. If someone prepares a meal for me, I eat it. God prepared forgiveness for me, so I receive it.

The moment you live passively, you let your guard down. So take these steps on purpose. Not to earn His love, but because you already have it.

## My story: how Jesus took my shame

I grew up in a Christian family but I had nothing to do with Jesus. By 15 I was hooked on alcohol and smoking. I went to school drunk. I bullied teachers. I was angry all the time. Underneath that I was so lonely, and there was a huge emptiness inside me that turned into a deep depression. For a full year I wanted to die because I saw no reason to live.

One night I was in my room, lying on my bed, and I prayed one short simple prayer: Jesus if you really exist, help me. It came out of a crushed, broken heart. In that very moment I was set free from depression. That emptiness was filled with a warm liquid love I had never felt before. The anger and the loneliness were gone for good. Within moments I was a totally different person.

There is a part people rarely talk about. I also lived in shame. Constant shame, because I was a porn addict. I was afraid of failing. I felt like a failure. I was afraid of disappointing everyone around me. I felt like nothing. Shame was the cloud over my whole life.

But Jesus took all of it away. The fear, the insecurity, the shame. I was a nobody but God made me His beloved son. There is so much peace in my mind now, peace I never had before. That is what He does. Not a partial cleaning. A new creation.

## Where do you start?

Start by knowing who you are. Don't let what you are going through become your identity. Shame says you are the wound. Jesus says you are the healed one.
