**To tell your wife you watched porn, get help and a plan first, choose a calm and private time, then tell her the truth plainly: how long, how often, and what you hid, without graphic detail. Own it fully in a minute or two, then stop talking and let her respond however she needs to.** You are not telling her to feel lighter. You are telling her because she deserves the truth and your marriage cannot heal around a secret.

I will not pretend this is easy. It will probably be one of the hardest conversations of your life. It may hurt her deeply. She may be angry for a long time. This guide will not give you a script that makes it painless. It will help you do it honestly, carefully, and in a way that gives your marriage the best chance.

## Should I tell my wife I watched porn?

Yes. Here is why.

A secret like this does not stay still. It sits between you every time she reaches for your hand. It makes you defensive, distant, quick to snap. And it keeps you stuck, because sin that is hidden keeps its power.

> **[Proverbs 28:13 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+28:13&version=NKJV)** "He who covers his sins will not prosper, But whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy."

Covering does not protect her. It protects your image. Confessing is the first time in a long time that you choose her over your image.

If you are not married yet, this guide still applies to an engaged partner. If you are a woman who has watched porn and needs to tell your husband, almost everything here works the same way.

## Why am I telling her?

Settle this before you open your mouth, because the reason shapes the whole conversation.

The wrong reason is relief. If you confess mainly to get the weight off your chest, you will rush it, you will want her to say it is fine, and you will feel hurt when she does not. You will have moved your weight onto her.

The right reason is truth. You are ending the hiding. You are giving her an honest husband, even if the honest husband is someone she is upset with right now.

> **[Ephesians 4:25 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+4:25&version=NKJV)** "Therefore, putting away lying, 'Let each one of you speak truth with his neighbor,' for we are members of one another."

Members of one another. In marriage that is literal. Your secret has been her wound already, even if she did not know it.

## What should I do before I tell her?

Do not walk in with only an apology. Walk in with a plan already moving. That way she is not handed the job of fixing you on the worst night of her year.

Start with your heavenly Father. Confess it to Him first, plainly. You do not have to carry the weight of earning His forgiveness into that kitchen. You already have it. If you are struggling to believe that, read [Does God Still Love Me After Porn?](/teachings/does-god-still-love-me-after-porn) before anything else.

Then tell one trusted person of the same sex. A pastor, a mature friend, a mentor. Say it to him first so you have practiced honesty once, and so someone is praying for you both. If you do not have that person, read [How to Find a Christian Accountability Partner](/teachings/christian-accountability-partner).

Then, if you can, book a Christian counselor who works with sexual integrity and betrayal. Some couples choose to have the full disclosure inside a counseling session so she has support in the room. That is wise, especially if the history is long or includes more than porn.

## The preparation checklist

Work through this before the conversation. Write your answers on paper.

1. I have confessed this to my heavenly Father and received His forgiveness (1 John 1:9).
2. I have told one trusted person of the same sex, and he knows when I am telling her.
3. I have contacted a counselor, or I have a name and a date to call.
4. I have written down the breadth: when it started, how often, what kind of material, what devices, and what else I hid or lied about.
5. I have removed easy access on every device. The [Accountability Apps Compared](/teachings/accountability-apps-compared-2026) guide helps you pick one.
6. I have written one or two sentences of ownership with no excuses in them.
7. I have chosen a time with no rush, no kids in the room, and nowhere either of us has to be.
8. I have a plan for her support: a friend she can call, a counselor she can see, and space if she wants it.
9. I have decided I will not ask her for forgiveness tonight.

That last line matters. You are giving her the truth, not asking her for a verdict.

## How much should I tell her?

Tell her the breadth, not the graphic depth.

Breadth means the whole outline. When it started. How often it happened. What kind of material. Whether you hid it, lied about it, spent money on it, or went beyond porn. If you leave pieces out to protect yourself and she finds them later, the second discovery often hurts more than the first.

Depth means the graphic details. Specific sites, specific images, specific scenes. Those rarely help her heal. They just give her pictures she cannot unsee.

If she asks a direct question, answer it honestly. You can gently say, "I will answer anything you ask. Do you want the details, or do you want the outline?" Let her choose. It is her heart.

## What do I actually say?

Keep the confession short. One or two minutes. The conversation after it may take hours, or many days.

Something like:

"I need to tell you something hard, and I am not going to make excuses. I have been watching porn. It started about [when], and it has happened about [how often]. I hid it from you, and I lied when you asked. That was wrong. It was sin against my heavenly Father and against you. I am getting help. I have told [friend], I have an appointment with a counselor, and I have put protection on my devices. I am not asking you to forgive me tonight. I want you to know the truth."

Then stop. Do not fill the silence.

Notice what is missing. No "but." No "it was only." No "you know I have been stressed." No "at least it was not an affair." Every one of those moves the weight back onto her.

## What happens after I tell her?

Let her respond however she needs to. She may cry. She may get very angry. She may go completely quiet. She may ask a hundred questions, or none. She may want you to sleep somewhere else. All of that is normal.

Your job in that moment is simple. Stay present. Listen. Do not defend. Do not correct her. If she says something unfair, let it go for tonight. If she wants space, give it to her and tell her you will be there when she is ready.

Ask, gently, "How has this hurt you?" Then let her tell you. You will learn things about your marriage that you could not see from inside your secret.

> **[James 5:16 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=James+5:16&version=NKJV)** "Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed."

Healing comes after confession, not instead of the hard part.

## Forgiveness and trust are not the same thing

She may forgive you quickly. She may take months. Either way, forgiveness and trust are two different things.

Forgiveness is letting go of the debt. Trust is confidence about the future. She can forgive you and still need you to show her your phone. She can love you and still say no for a while. That is not her punishing you. That is wisdom, and you should welcome it.

Trust is rebuilt slowly, one honest week at a time. You rebuild it by doing what you said, by telling her before she has to ask, and by not getting defensive when she checks.

> **[Ephesians 5:25 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+5:25&version=NKJV)** "Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her,"

Christ gave Himself. Loving her now means giving up your right to be trusted on your schedule. If you want more on what that kind of love looks like, read [Love Her First](/tracks/stand-up-and-act-like-a-man/love-her-first) from the Stand Up and Act Like a Man course.

## What if I relapse after telling her?

Decide this together, ideally with a counselor, before it happens. Many couples choose a simple rule. You tell her within a set time, such as a day or two, and you tell your accountability partner the same day.

A confessed relapse hurts. A hidden relapse that she discovers later usually does far more damage, because it tells her the honesty was only for one night. Read [What to Do After a Porn Relapse](/teachings/what-to-do-after-porn-relapse) for the steps that keep one fall from becoming a spiral.

## You are not walking into that kitchen alone

This is going to be hard, and it is also the start of something better than the life you had with the secret. The hiding is ending. That matters.

Your heavenly Father is not waiting to see how the conversation goes before deciding how He feels about you. He is with you in it. Pray before you speak. Pray while she talks. Pray afterward, even if all you can say is "Father, help us."
