**To talk to your son about porn, start before you find anything, keep it calm and short, and have many small talks instead of one big lecture. Tell him he will probably see it, that it is designed to hook people, that God made sex good for marriage, and that he can always come to you without getting in trouble. Give him a simple plan for the moment it happens, and keep the door open for years.**

If you are a father reading this, you may feel unqualified. Maybe you have your own history with porn. Maybe your own father never said a word about any of this, and you do not know where to start. That is all right. You do not have to be perfect to have this talk. You only have to go first.

## Why talk before you find anything?

Because he will very likely see something long before you find out. Phones, school friends, games, social media and search results put sexual images close to almost every child. The question is not if he will see it, but who he will talk to when he does.

You want to be the first voice on this subject, not the last. If the internet teaches him about sex before you do, it will teach him lies. If you speak first, his heart has something true to hold onto when it happens.

> **[Proverbs 23:26 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+23:26&version=NKJV)** "My son, give me your heart, And let your eyes observe my ways."

A father asks for his son's heart. Not only his obedience. His heart. That is what this conversation is for.

## Many small talks, not one big lecture

The old idea was one awkward "talk" and then never again. That does not work. One big lecture is easy to forget, and it tells your son this subject is too uncomfortable to bring up twice.

Scripture shows a better way:

> **[Deuteronomy 6:6-7 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy+6:6-7&version=NKJV)** "And these words which I command you today shall be in your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up."

Along the way. In the car. On a walk. Fixing something in the garage. Many boys talk more easily side by side than face to face. Short, regular, normal. That is how trust is built.

## Before you talk: get your own heart ready

Your son will learn more from how you speak than from what you say. Prepare yourself first.

- **Pray.** Ask your heavenly Father for calm, wisdom and the right words.
- **Deal with your own life.** If you are still watching porn yourself, get help now. Your son does not need a perfect father, but he needs an honest one. [How to Quit Porn as a Christian](/teachings/quit-porn-christian-2026) is a good start.
- **Decide what you will say about your own past.** One honest sentence is usually enough. Details are not helpful.
- **Talk with his mother first** if you can, so you are on the same page.
- **Pick a calm time.** Not right after a fight. Not when you are angry. Not at midnight.

## What to say in the first conversation

Keep it short. Ten or fifteen minutes is plenty for a first talk. Here is a simple outline you can put in your own words.

1. **Start with love.** "I love you. Nothing you tell me will make me love you less. I want to talk about something a lot of guys deal with."
2. **Name it plainly.** "At some point you will probably see porn online, maybe by accident, maybe because someone shows you. Has that happened yet?" Then wait. Let the silence sit. Do not fill it.
3. **Tell him the truth about it.** "It is made to hook people. It shows a fake picture of sex and of women. It can turn into a habit that is hard to break."
4. **Tell him the truth about sex.** "God made sex good. It is a gift for marriage, and it is worth waiting for. Porn is a counterfeit of something beautiful."
5. **Share one honest sentence about yourself, if it fits.** "I struggled with this when I was younger. I know how strong the pull is." No details.
6. **Give him a plan.** "If you see it, close it, put the device down, walk away, and tell me. You will not be in trouble for telling me. Ever."
7. **Close with blessing.** "I am proud of you. You can come to me with anything."

That last line matters more than you think. The goal is not a perfect talk. It is a son who knows the door is open.

## If he has already seen it

Most teenage boys have, or will. If he says yes, stay calm. Your face matters as much as your words. If he sees shock, disgust or anger, he learns one lesson: hide next time.

- **Thank him for telling you.** "Thank you for being honest. That takes courage."
- **Ask, do not interrogate.** "How did it come up? How did you feel about it? Has it kept happening?"
- **Listen for what is underneath.** Curiosity, loneliness, boredom, stress, a friend who keeps sending things. The habit usually has a reason.
- **Do not demand every detail.** You need to know enough to help, not every image.
- **Make a plan together.** Which doors are open, and how will you close them? What will he do next time?
- **Point him to grace.** "God forgives. When you mess up, you come back to Him right away, and you come to me."

> **[1 Corinthians 10:13 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+10:13&version=NKJV)** "No temptation has overtaken you except such as is common to man; but God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make the way of escape, that you may be able to bear it."

Common to man. Let him know he is not strange or uniquely broken. And there is always a way out.

## If you found it on his device

Finding it is hard. Your first feeling might be anger, fear or grief. Do not have the conversation in that moment.

Do two things right away. Close the access point, and tell him calmly, "I found something. I love you. We are going to talk about it tomorrow." Then take a day to pray, calm down, and talk with his mother or a wise friend.

When you talk, start with what you know, give him room to respond, and do not call him names. If he denies it, do not turn it into a trial. Tell him the door is open and that he can come to you anytime. Then keep your word by staying calm.

## Practical walls at home

Rules do not change a heart, but walls help a heart that is trying. Explain them openly so they feel like protection, not punishment.

- **Phones and tablets charge outside bedrooms at night.** This one wall closes the most common door. The same rule helps adults, which is why [The Phone Sleeps in Another Room](/tracks/discipline-and-morning-routine/the-phone-sleeps-in-another-room) is a whole lesson.
- **Use filters and parental controls on every device**, including game consoles and the TV. [Accountability Apps Compared](/teachings/accountability-apps-compared-2026) can help you choose.
- **Keep screens in shared spaces** when you can.
- **Agree on what happens when he sees something.** The plan above. Close, walk away, tell.
- **Live by the same rules yourself.** Sons watch what fathers do.

## Give him an identity, not only rules

At 11 p.m., alone, with a phone in his hand, a rule may not hold. What holds is knowing who he is.

> **[Psalm 119:9 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+119:9&version=NKJV)** "How can a young man cleanse his way? By taking heed according to Your word."

Tell him often that he is loved by his heavenly Father, that he is not a slave to any habit, and that real men protect women instead of using them. Bless him out loud. Many men never heard their father say "I am proud of you." Say it.

And be patient with him.

> **[Ephesians 6:4 (NKJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians+6:4&version=NKJV)** "And you, fathers, do not provoke your children to wrath, but bring them up in the training and admonition of the Lord."

Training takes time. It includes failure, correction and starting again. Do not crush him. Walk with him.
